\title{Mediant Trees for Rational Approximation and Sturmian Word Generation}
\author{Jon Seymour}
\date{\today\ (v0.22)}
%------------------------------------------------------------

\begin{document}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Given an oracle for a real number $\theta$ of sufficient precision, we present the
following contributions.
First, a closed-form formula for the unique optimal integer weight $\wplus$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:optimal-w}): the weight whose weighted mediant of a
Farey-adjacent bounded pair straddles $\theta$.
This yields an algorithm that constructs a \emph{mediant tree} of increasingly
precise rational approximations to $\theta$, with every iterate (except, for
rational $\theta$, the final companion of the exact step) lying on the
Stern--Brocot path of $\theta$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:sb-path}).
Second, structural theorems underpinning the algorithm's efficiency: exponential
denominator growth (Lemma~\ref{lem:denominator-growth}) and the no-consecutive-unit-weights
property (Lemma~\ref{lem:no-consecutive-unit-weights}) together imply that $O(\log N)$
iterations suffice to produce an approximant of size $N$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:linear-depth}).
Third, the mediant tree can be encoded as a \emph{mediant word} --- a string over
$\{\OO, \EE\}$ --- and this word is identical to the Sturmian word
(the binary sequence $s_n = \lfloor(n{+}1)\alpha\rfloor - \lfloor n\alpha\rfloor$
with slope $\alpha = 1/(1{+}\theta)$) at all interior positions, with the terminal
symbol characterised by whether $r$ is an overestimate or underestimate of $\theta$
(Theorem~\ref{prop:sturmian}).
Two structural invariants follow: every non-leaf mediant word begins with $\EE\EE$
(Theorem~\ref{prop:EE-prefix}), and the interior slice $\pi(r)[1{:}L{-}1]$ of any
mediant word of length $L \geq 2$ is a palindrome (Theorem~\ref{prop:palindrome}).
Fourth, we show how to extend both algorithms to all real $\theta$ via simple
algebraic reductions to the $\theta > 1$ case.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
%------------------------------------------------------------

The approximation of irrational numbers by rationals is a classical subject in number theory.
The Stern--Brocot tree and Farey sequences provide a systematic account of best rational
approximants, and the continued-fraction expansion of a real number yields its
convergents --- the fractions closest to the target with bounded denominator.
Sturmian words, introduced by Morse and Hedlund~\cite{morse1940}, give a
combinatorial counterpart: the infinite binary sequence $s_n = \lfloor(n+1)\alpha\rfloor -
\lfloor n\alpha\rfloor$ with slope $\alpha \in (0,1)$ is the lower mechanical (Sturmian)
word of slope $\alpha$ with intercept $0$, having $\mathsf{O}$-density $\alpha$
(other shifts are also balanced and aperiodic with the same density).

The present paper makes four contributions to this classical picture.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Optimal weight formula.}
    We introduce the \emph{bounded-pair} framework, in which two Farey-adjacent rational
    estimates bracket $\theta$ from above and below.
    Each weighted mediant step is a run of $w$ steps in the Stern--Brocot tree in the
    same direction --- the classical construction (see Graham, Knuth and
    Patashnik~\cite{gkp1994}).
    What is new is Theorem~\ref{thm:optimal-w}: a closed-form expression for the unique
    \emph{optimal} integer weight $\wplus$ --- the one whose mediants straddle $\theta$
    --- derived from a sign-change bracket on the linear error function, enabling
    efficient iteration with no redundant steps.
  \item \textbf{Mediant word representation.}
    The mediant tree can be encoded as a string over $\{\OO, \EE\}$, the
    \emph{mediant word} (Section~\ref{sec:parity}).
    This representation connects rational approximation to combinatorics on words.
  \item \textbf{Sturmian identity.}
    The mediant word produced by the mediant tree is identical to the
    Sturmian word with slope $1/(1+\theta)$
    (Theorem~\ref{prop:sturmian}), agreeing at every position except possibly the last
    symbol of each finite prefix.
    The identification of Christoffel words with Sturmian prefixes is classical
    (Lothaire~\cite{lothaire2002}); the novelty is the direct proof via the mediant
    construction and the precise last-letter criterion.
  \item \textbf{Compressed transducer.}
    Each iterate produced by the algorithm (except, for rational $\theta$, the final
    companion $r^+$ of the exact step) is a convergent or semi-convergent of $\theta$
    (a node on the Stern--Brocot path of $\theta$) --- the
    best bracketing fraction achievable in one step from the current bounded pair.
    The mediant tree is therefore a \emph{compressed transducer} for the Sturmian word:
    the approximation path ($O(\log N)$ real-arithmetic iterations, each requiring $O(1)$ oracle
    evaluations; proved via exponential denominator growth,
    Theorem~\ref{thm:linear-depth})
    encodes the entire construction history, after which the $N$
    symbols are enumerated by a purely integer recursive traversal with no further
    real arithmetic.
\end{enumerate}

The algorithm requires an oracle for $\theta$ --- a means of evaluating
$\lceil o\theta \rceil$ to sufficient precision at each step --- but is otherwise entirely
general.
Given such an oracle, the mediant tree can represent \emph{any} real number as a mediant
word over $\{\OO, \EE\}$, together with a tag identifying the regime of
Table~\ref{tab:extension}, making it a universal encoder from reals to binary words.

\paragraph{Notation.}
Throughout, $\theta > 1$ is the target real number (the case $\theta \notin (1,\infty)$
is handled by reduction in Section~\ref{sec:extension}).
A rational approximant is written $r = e/o$ with numerator $e$ (\emph{even-count})
and denominator $o$ (\emph{odd-count}); $e, o > 0$ always, and $\gcd(e, o) = 1$
unless otherwise stated.

\textbf{Alphabet.}
Mediant words are strings over $\{\EE, \OO\}$, where $\EE$ stands for
\emph{even} and $\OO$ for \emph{odd}.

\textbf{Depth index.}
The algorithm progresses through depths $d = 0, 1, 2, \ldots$\,.
At each depth $d$ (for rational $\theta$, up to but not including the
terminal depth) the algorithm holds a \emph{bounded pair}
$(r_{2d},\, r_{2d+1})$, where $r_{2d+1}$ is always the better estimate
(smaller relative error $|\eps|$) and $r_{2d}$ the worse.
We call $r_{2d}$ the \emph{older sibling} and $r_{2d+1}$ the \emph{younger
sibling} of the pair at depth $d$; the older sibling is always the worse
estimate and the younger sibling always the better.
The flat linear subscripts $2d$ and $2d+1$ reflect the sequential history
of all estimates produced: $r_0, r_1$ are the initial integer bounds, and
each depth $d \geq 1$ contributes two new estimates $r_{2d}$ and $r_{2d+1}$
(one of which may coincide with $r_{2d-2}$ when $\wminus = 0$).

\textbf{Intermediate quantities (depth $d$ to $d+1$).}
Between depth $d$ and depth $d+1$ the algorithm computes several
quantities that are intermediate in the sense that they are derived from
the pair $(r_{2d}, r_{2d+1})$ and used to produce the next pair.
These carry \emph{superscript} decorations $+$, $-$ (or $*$ for a combined value)
to distinguish them from the depth-indexed estimates:
\begin{itemize}
  \item $\wplus$: the \emph{optimal weight}, i.e.\ the weight computed in Step~4 of the
    algorithm (Section~\ref{sec:optimal-w}).
    At rational approximation steps it is the unique integer such that
    $\mathrm{Mediant}(r_{2d}, r_{2d+1}, \wplus-1)$ and
    $\mathrm{Mediant}(r_{2d}, r_{2d+1}, \wplus)$ straddle $\theta$.
    At the exact step no weight straddles $\theta$ strictly; Step~4 then returns the integer
    one above the weight whose mediant equals $\theta$
    (Lemma~\ref{lem:exact-step}).
  \item $\wminus = \wplus - 1$: the companion (sub-optimal) weight.
  \item $\rplus$, $\rminus$: the two candidate mediants at weights $\wplus$ and $\wminus$;
    the better one becomes $r_{2d+3}$ and the worse $r_{2d+2}$.
  \item $\epsplus$, $\epsminus$: the relative errors of $\rplus$ and $\rminus$.
  \item $\lamplus$, $\lamminus$: the ratios $(e/o)/\theta$ for $\rplus$ and $\rminus$.
  \item $\eplus, \eminus$ and $\odenp, \odenm$: the numerators and denominators
    of $\rplus$ and $\rminus$.
\end{itemize}
\textbf{Superscript convention.}
The $+$ superscript marks the candidate mediant produced at the \emph{optimal}
weight $\wplus$, and the $-$ superscript marks the candidate at the
\emph{sub-optimal} weight $\wminus = \wplus - 1$.
At \emph{rational approximation} steps (those at which the weight that straddles $\theta$ is unique;
see \emph{Step types} in Section~\ref{sec:derived-quantities}), $\wplus$ is the unique weight
at which the mediant switches to the opposite side of $\theta$, so $\rplus$ lies on the
opposite side of $\theta$ from $r_{2d}$, while $\rminus$ lies on the same side as $r_{2d}$.
At the \emph{exact} step (which occurs only for rational $\theta$ and is the last step),
$\rminus = \theta$ exactly (on neither side), and $\rplus$ still lies strictly on the
opposite side of $\theta$ from $r_{2d}$, i.e.\ on the side of $r_{2d+1}$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:exact-step}).
The superscript/subscript distinction is systematic: a $+$/$-$ \emph{superscript}
always marks an intermediate quantity between two depths; a numeric \emph{subscript}
always marks a position in the flat estimate sequence.
These two systems are complementary and never overlap.
When the depth matters we write $w^\pm_d$ for the propagated weights of
Definition~\ref{def:parity-sequence}; in \S\S7--8 the depth is fixed and the subscript is omitted.

\textbf{Gender.}
A rational approximant $r = e/o$ is called \emph{female} if it overestimates
$\theta$ (i.e.\ $e/o > \theta$, signed error $> 0$) and \emph{male} if it
underestimates $\theta$ (i.e.\ $e/o < \theta$, signed error $< 0$).
At every approximation depth (every depth for irrational $\theta$; every depth before the
terminal one for rational $\theta$) the pair contains exactly one male and one female
member --- the \emph{bracketing invariant}.
At the terminal depth for rational $\theta$ the better member is $\theta$ itself, which
is neither male nor female; the other member is male if the exact step had $B < 0$ and
female if $B > 0$.
The exact step, in which $\rminus = \theta$ becomes the younger sibling, is
gender-changing only in the formal sense of the definition below.

A generation at depth $d \geq 0$ is called \emph{gender-changing} if $\rminus$
becomes the younger sibling of the depth-$(d+1)$ pair (i.e.\ $\rminus$ is the
better estimate), and \emph{gender-preserving} otherwise (i.e.\ $\rplus$ is the
younger sibling).
Lemma~\ref{lem:no-consecutive-unit-weights} (Section~\ref{sec:convergence}) states that $w^+_0 \ge 2$ always, and
that no two consecutive weights $w^+_d$ equal $1$.
Unit-weight ($w^+_d = 1$) generations are gender-preserving, but not conversely.

\textbf{Per-estimate scalar quantities.}
For a single estimate $r = e/o$ the key scalars are:
\begin{itemize}
  \item $\lambda(r) = (e/o)/\theta$: the ratio of $r$ to $\theta$; equals $1$ iff $r = \theta$.
  \item $\eps(r) = \lambda(r) - 1$: the \emph{relative error}; negative for underestimates,
    positive for overestimates.
  \item $\Delta(r, r') = e' o - e o'$: the \emph{Farey determinant} of two estimates;
    $|\Delta| = 1$ iff $r$ and $r'$ are Farey adjacent.
  \item $\Kval(r) = o(e - \theta o)$: the \emph{scaled error} of estimate $r = e/o$
    (Definition~\ref{def:delta-K}); for the current pair, $\Kval := \Kval(r_{2d+1})$.
    Equals $\theta\, o^2\,\eps$ and $\Kval = 0$ iff $r = \theta$ exactly.
\end{itemize}

\section{The Stern--Brocot Tree and Mediants}
\label{sec:background-sb}
%------------------------------------------------------------

The \emph{Stern--Brocot tree} is a complete binary search tree containing every
positive rational exactly once in lowest terms (see, e.g.,
Graham, Knuth, and Patashnik~\cite{gkp1994}).
It is constructed by repeated \emph{mediant} insertion: starting from the pair
$(0/1, 1/0)$, the mediant of two adjacent fractions $a/b$ and $c/d$ is
$(a+c)/(b+d)$, and inserting it between them yields two new adjacent pairs.
Every pair of fractions on adjacent branches of the Stern--Brocot tree satisfies
$bc - ad = 1$; this is the Farey adjacency condition (Definition~\ref{def:farey-adjacency},
Section~\ref{sec:background-farey}).
The Stern--Brocot tree encodes continued-fraction expansions as paths from the root:
a partial quotient of $k$ corresponds to $k$ steps in the same direction before
the first reversal.

The path from the root of the Stern--Brocot tree to $\theta$ passes through the
\emph{convergents} $p_k/q_k$ of $\theta$, defined as the fractions produced by
truncating the continued-fraction expansion $\theta = [a_0; a_1, a_2, \ldots]$.
The nodes visited between consecutive convergents $p_{k-1}/q_{k-1}$ and $p_k/q_k$
are the \emph{semi-convergents}:

\begin{definition}[Convergents and semi-convergents]
\label{def:semi-convergent}
Let $\theta = [a_0; a_1, a_2, \ldots]$ with $p_{-1}/q_{-1} = 1/0$ and
convergents $p_k/q_k$ for $k \geq 0$.
The \emph{semi-convergents} between consecutive convergents $p_k/q_k$ and
$p_{k+1}/q_{k+1}$ are the fractions
\[
  \frac{p_{k-1} + j\,p_k}{q_{k-1} + j\,q_k}, \quad 1 \leq j < a_{k+1}.
\]
(These appear on the SB path strictly between $p_k/q_k$ and $p_{k+1}/q_{k+1}$.)
The integer spine $1/1, 2/1, \ldots, (\lfloor\theta\rfloor-1)/1$ consists of the initial
SB-path nodes strictly before the first convergent $p_0/q_0 = \lfloor\theta\rfloor/1$.
The convergents and semi-convergents together, plus the integer spine, form exactly
the nodes on the Stern--Brocot path of $\theta$.
We use this broad definition throughout; in particular every SB-path node that
is not a convergent is called a semi-convergent, with no additional size
condition on $j$.
\end{definition}

\section{Farey Sequences}
\label{sec:background-farey}
%------------------------------------------------------------

The \emph{Farey sequence} $F_n$ is the increasing sequence of fractions in $[0,1]$
with denominator at most $n$, arranged in order.

\begin{definition}[Farey adjacency]
\label{def:farey-adjacency}
Two fractions $a/b$ and $c/d$ (in lowest terms, $a/b < c/d$) are
\emph{Farey adjacent} if
\[
  bc - ad = 1.
\]
Equivalently, $|bc - ad| = 1$ for an unordered pair.
Consecutive terms in any Farey sequence $F_n$ are Farey adjacent, and
Farey-adjacent fractions lie on adjacent branches of the Stern--Brocot tree.
Farey adjacency is equivalent to the condition that no rational with denominator
at most $\max(b, d)$ lies strictly between $a/b$ and $c/d$.
\end{definition}

The mediant $(a+c)/(b+d)$ of two Farey-adjacent fractions is itself Farey adjacent
to each of them; their mediant $(a+c)/(b+d)$ is also the unique fraction with
smallest denominator strictly between them.
This adjacency-preservation is the key structural property exploited by our algorithm.

\section{Sturmian Words}
\label{sec:background-sturmian}
%------------------------------------------------------------


\emph{Sturmian words} were introduced by Morse and Hedlund~\cite{morse1940} as
the simplest aperiodic symbolic sequences.
Let $\alpha \in (0,1)$ be irrational.
The \emph{lower Sturmian word} with slope $\alpha$ is defined by
\[
  s_n = \lfloor (n+1)\alpha \rfloor - \lfloor n\alpha \rfloor, \qquad n \geq 0.
\]
Encoding $s_n = 1$ as $\OO$ and $s_n = 0$ as $\EE$, this is a binary sequence
with $\OO$-density exactly $\alpha$.
Since $\alpha < 1$, we have $s_0 = \lfloor\alpha\rfloor = 0$, so the Sturmian word
always begins with $\EE$.

\begin{definition}[Sturmian word]
\label{def:sturmian}
The \emph{Sturmian word} $\mathbf{s}(\alpha)$ with slope $\alpha \in (0,1)$
is the infinite word over $\{\OO, \EE\}$ defined by
$s_n = \OO$ if $\lfloor(n+1)\alpha\rfloor > \lfloor n\alpha\rfloor$, and
$s_n = \EE$ otherwise.
\end{definition}

Sturmian words are \emph{balanced}: no two factors of the same length differ in
their $\OO$-count by more than one.
For irrational $\alpha$, the lower mechanical (Sturmian) word of slope $\alpha$ with
intercept $0$ is the canonical balanced aperiodic sequence; shifts of it are also balanced
and aperiodic~\cite{morse1940}.

The two-word structure is fundamental: a Sturmian word with slope
$\alpha \in (0, 1/2)$ consists of exactly two distinct \emph{primitive words}
(unbreakable building blocks), of lengths $\lfloor 1/\alpha \rfloor$ and
$\lceil 1/\alpha \rceil$, concatenated in the Sturmian pattern.
Concretely, with $\alpha = 1/(1+\theta)$ for $\theta > 1$, the two primitive words
are $\EE^{\lfloor\theta\rfloor}\OO$ and $\EE^{\lceil\theta\rceil}\OO$ --- exactly
the leaf words of the mediant tree (Section~\ref{sec:parity-leaf}).

Since $\alpha < 1/2$ for $\theta > 1$, the sequence avoids $\OO\OO$: two consecutive
$\OO$'s would require $\lfloor(n+2)\alpha\rfloor - \lfloor n\alpha\rfloor = 2$, but
$2\alpha < 1$ makes this impossible.
Conversely, when $\alpha > 1/2$ (i.e.\ $\theta < 1$), consecutive $\OO$'s are
unavoidable.

\paragraph{Sturmian morphisms.}
The two \emph{Sturmian morphisms}
$\sigma_E\colon \EE \mapsto \EE,\; \OO \mapsto \EE\OO$ and
$\sigma_O\colon \EE \mapsto \OO\EE,\; \OO \mapsto \OO$
map Sturmian words to Sturmian words (of a different slope in general).

\section{Christoffel Words}
\label{sec:background-deluca}
%------------------------------------------------------------

\begin{definition}[Christoffel words]
\label{def:christoffel}
For coprime positive integers $e$ and $o$, the \emph{lower Christoffel word}
$\mathrm{Ch}^-(e, o)$ is the unique primitive word of length $L = e + o$ over $\{\EE, \OO\}$
containing exactly $e$ copies of $\EE$ and $o$ copies of $\OO$ that ends in $\OO$,
with $\OO$-positions given by
\[
  \mathrm{Ch}^-(e, o)[i] = \OO \iff \lfloor (i+1)o/L \rfloor > \lfloor i \cdot o / L \rfloor.
\]
The \emph{upper Christoffel word} $\mathrm{Ch}^+(e, o)$ is the reverse of
$\mathrm{Ch}^-(e, o)$: it ends in $\EE$ instead of $\OO$.
Both words share the same palindromic interior $U$: $\mathrm{Ch}^-(e,o) = \EE \cdot U \cdot \OO$
and $\mathrm{Ch}^+(e,o) = \OO \cdot U \cdot \EE$
(Berstel~\cite{berstel2009}, Chapter~1).

$\mathrm{Ch}^-(e, o)$ is the lower Sturmian word of slope $o/L$ truncated to length $L$
and normalised to end in $\OO$; $\mathrm{Ch}^+(e, o)$ is the upper Sturmian word of the
same slope, ending in $\EE$ (Berstel~\cite{berstel2009}, Chapter~1).
\end{definition}

Each Christoffel word is characterised purely by its fraction $o/L$:
given $\gcd(e,o) = 1$, the pair $(e,o)$ is uniquely determined by $o/(e+o)$.

\begin{theorem}[Christoffel concatenation (Berstel~\cite{berstel2009}, Theorem~1.5.1)]
\label{thm:christoffel-concat}
Let $\mathrm{Ch}^-(e_a, o_a)$ and $\mathrm{Ch}^-(e_b, o_b)$ be two lower Christoffel
words with $o_a/L_a < o_b/L_b$ (where $L_a = e_a + o_a$, $L_b = e_b + o_b$) satisfying the Farey
adjacency condition $o_b L_a - o_a L_b = 1$.
Then for any integer $w \geq 1$:
\begin{align*}
  \mathrm{Ch}^-(e_a, o_a)^w \cdot \mathrm{Ch}^-(e_b, o_b)
  &\;=\; \mathrm{Ch}^-(w\,e_a + e_b,\; w\,o_a + o_b), \\
  \mathrm{Ch}^-(e_a, o_a) \cdot \mathrm{Ch}^-(e_b, o_b)^w
  &\;=\; \mathrm{Ch}^-(e_a + w\,e_b,\; o_a + w\,o_b).
\end{align*}
In the first identity the smaller-slope word is repeated; in the second the
larger-slope word is repeated.
\end{theorem}

\section{Definitions and Notation}
\label{sec:definitions}
%------------------------------------------------------------

Throughout Sections~\ref{sec:definitions}--\ref{sec:sturmian}, let $\theta > 1$ be a fixed
real number.
Section~\ref{sec:extension} handles all other real numbers by reduction to this case.
We write $\NN$ for the positive integers and $\lfloor \cdot \rfloor$, $\lceil \cdot \rceil$
for floor and ceiling.

\begin{definition}[Approximant]
\label{def:approximant}
An \emph{approximant} is an ordered pair $r = (e, o) \in \NN^2$ denoting
the rational number $e/o$.  The \emph{canonical} form satisfies
$\gcd(e, o) = 1$; this is assumed unless otherwise stated.  A non-canonical
representation is permitted when only the value $e/o$ matters.
An approximant $r = (e, o)$ is a \emph{rational estimate} of $\theta$ when used in that context.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Mediant]
\label{def:mediant}
Given two approximants $r = (e, o)$ and $r' = (e', o')$ and a non-negative integer $w \geq 0$,
the \emph{mediant} of $r$ and $r'$ with weight $w$ is the approximant
\[
  \mathrm{Mediant}(r,\, r',\, w) = (e + w\,e',\; o + w\,o'),
\]
which is in canonical form whenever $r$ and $r'$ are Farey-adjacent
(Lemma~\ref{lem:mediant-farey}(4)); in general it need not be.
The \emph{Farey determinant} of the pair $(r, r')$ is
\[
  \Delta(r, r') = e' o - e o'.
\]
The pair $(r, r')$ is \emph{Farey-adjacent} when $|\Delta(r, r')| = 1$
(Definition~\ref{def:farey-adjacency}).
The degenerate case $w = 0$ gives $\mathrm{Mediant}(r, r', 0) = r$.
Throughout this paper, when $\mathrm{Mediant}(r, r', w)$ is used as a rational
approximation of $\theta$, the convention is that $r'$ is the better estimate
in the sense that $|\eps'| < |\eps|$, i.e.\ $|r'/\theta - 1| < |r/\theta - 1|$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Good estimate]
\label{def:good-estimate}
An estimate $e/o$ is a \emph{good estimate} of $\theta$ if
\[
  e \in \{\lfloor o \cdot \theta \rfloor,\, \lceil o \cdot \theta \rceil\},
\]
i.e.\ $e/o$ is one of the two nearest rationals to $\theta$ with denominator $o$.
A good estimate with $e = \lceil o\theta \rceil$ satisfies $e/o \geq \theta$ and is an
\emph{upper bound}; one with $e = \lfloor o\theta \rfloor$ satisfies $e/o \leq \theta$
and is a \emph{lower bound}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Bounded pair]
\label{def:bounded-pair}
Two good estimates $e_l/o_l$ and $e_u/o_u$ form a \emph{bounded pair} if
$e_l/o_l$ is a lower bound, $e_u/o_u$ is an upper bound,
\[
  \frac{e_l}{o_l} < \theta \leq \frac{e_u}{o_u},
\]
and they satisfy the \emph{Farey adjacency condition}
\[
  e_u \cdot o_l - e_l \cdot o_u = 1.
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[$\lambda$ and $\varepsilon$]
\label{def:lambda-eps}
For an estimate $e/o$ of $\theta$, define the \emph{relative ratio}
\[
  \lam = \frac{e/o}{\theta}
\]
and the \emph{relative error}
\[
  \eps = \lam - 1 = \frac{e/o}{\theta} - 1.
\]
An upper-bound good estimate satisfies $\lam \geq 1$ (so $\eps \geq 0$);
a lower-bound good estimate satisfies $\lam \leq 1$ (so $\eps \leq 0$).
In both cases $|\eps|$ is small when $o$ is large.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Age ordering]
\label{def:age}
For approximants $r_i$ and $r_j$ produced by the mediant tree, define
\[
  \mathrm{age}(r_i, r_j) = (r_{\min(i,j)},\; r_{\max(i,j)}).
\]
Writing $(u, v) = \mathrm{age}(r_i, r_j)$ gives $u = r_{\min(i,j)}$ and
$v = r_{\max(i,j)}$, so $u$ was produced no later than $v$ in the mediant tree
($u$ is the \emph{older} estimate; siblings produced at the same step are ordered
by subscript).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Height ordering]
\label{def:height}
For approximants $r$ and $r'$ with $\eps \neq \eps'$, define
\[
  \mathrm{height}(r, r') = (r_{\mathrm{lo}},\; r_{\mathrm{hi}}),
\]
where $\eps_{\mathrm{lo}} < \eps_{\mathrm{hi}}$ (signed-error order).
Writing $(u, v) = \mathrm{height}(r, r')$ gives $\eps_u < \eps_v$.
When $r$ and $r'$ bracket $\theta$, $u$ is the \emph{underestimate}
($u/\theta < 1$) and $v$ is the \emph{overestimate} ($v/\theta \geq 1$);
in general only the signed-error ordering is guaranteed.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Fit ordering]
\label{def:fit}
For a target $\theta$ and approximants $r$, $r'$ with $|\eps| \neq |\eps'|$
(where $\eps = e/o - \theta$ evaluated at each), define
\[
  \mathrm{fit}(\theta,\, r,\, r') = (r_{\mathrm{worse}},\; r_{\mathrm{better}}),
\]
where $|\eps_{\mathrm{worse}}| > |\eps_{\mathrm{better}}|$ (absolute-error order).
Writing $(u, v) = \mathrm{fit}(\theta, r, r')$ gives $|\eps_u| > |\eps_v|$:
$u$ is the \emph{worse} estimate and $v$ is the \emph{better} estimate.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Alpha transformation]
\label{def:alpha-transform}
For real or rational numbers $x, y \geq 0$, define
\[
  \mathrm{alpha}(x, y) = \Bigl(\frac{1}{1+y},\; \frac{1}{1+x}\Bigr).
\]
The key invariant is that $x \mapsto 1/(1+x)$ is strictly decreasing, so
\[
  \frac{1}{1+y} \leq \frac{1}{1+x} \iff x \leq y.
\]
Writing $(u, v) = \mathrm{alpha}(x, y)$, the pair $(u, v)$ preserves the size
relations of $(x, y)$: $u \leq v \iff x \leq y$.
If $x$ and $y$ are Farey-adjacent approximants then so are $u$ and $v$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:farey-image}), with $\Delta(u, v) = \Delta(x, y)$.
The map $\mathrm{alpha}$ corresponds to the change of variable $\theta \mapsto \alpha = 1/(1+\theta)$
that relates the mediant algorithm for $\theta$ to the Sturmian slope $\alpha$;
it is named \textup{alpha} (not $\alpha$) to avoid clash with that symbol.
\end{definition}

\subsection*{Index convention}

Estimates are indexed so that $r_{2d}$ and $r_{2d+1}$ form the bounded pair at depth $d$,
with $r_{2d+1}$ always the better estimate (smaller $|\eps|$).
Their children are $r_{2d+2}$ and $r_{2d+3}$, the bounded pair at depth $d+1$.
We write $\lam_{2d}$, $\eps_{2d}$, etc.\ for the corresponding values.
In a bounded pair we always orient so that $|\eps_{2d}| > |\eps_{2d+1}|$, i.e.\ $r_{2d+1}$
is the better estimate of the two.

\subsection*{Farey adjacency and the Stern--Brocot tree}

The Farey adjacency condition in Definition~\ref{def:bounded-pair} ensures that the pair
lies on adjacent branches of the Stern--Brocot tree (Section~\ref{sec:background-sb}) and
that no rational with smaller denominator lies strictly between $e_l/o_l$ and $e_u/o_u$.
In terms of $\Dval$, the condition is precisely $|\Dval| = 1$;
the sign depends on which of $r_{2d}$, $r_{2d+1}$ is the upper bound.

\subsection*{Domain restriction}

The definitions in this section assume $\theta > 1$.
Section~\ref{sec:extension} shows how to reduce all other real numbers to this case.

\subsection*{Rational vs.\ irrational $\theta$}
\label{rem:rationality}

For rational $\theta$, $-A/B$ can be an integer $\wminus$; the depth is then
\emph{exact} (Section~\ref{sec:derived-quantities}, \emph{Step types}).
No integer satisfies the strict straddling condition: weights $\wminus$ and
$\wplus = \wminus + 1$ give product zero, and all others give a positive product.
The mediant at weight $\wminus$ equals $\theta$, Step~4 computes $\wplus = \wminus + 1$,
Step~5 places $\theta$ second, and the next depth is terminal
(Lemma~\ref{lem:exact-step}).
Hence for rational $\theta$ the algorithm terminates after finitely many steps.
All claims of Sections~\ref{sec:mediant}--\ref{sec:sturmian} hold at every approximation
depth and at every depth up to termination, with the following exceptions.
\begin{itemize}
  \item Theorems~\ref{prop:sturmian}, \ref{thm:never-skips}, \ref{thm:semi-r0},
        and Corollary~\ref{cor:mismatch} require $\theta$ irrational.
  \item \emph{At the exact step.}  The hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:optimal-w},
        Corollary~\ref{cor:children-bounded-pair}, and the strict bracketing hypothesis
        of Theorem~\ref{thm:mediant-convergence} and Corollary~\ref{cor:straddling} are
        unmet.  The conclusions of Theorem~\ref{thm:mediant-convergence} and the second
        inequality of Corollary~\ref{cor:straddling} still hold by direct argument; the
        first conclusion of Corollary~\ref{cor:straddling} fails ($\epsminus\epsplus = 0$);
        the conclusion of Corollary~\ref{cor:children-bounded-pair} holds if and only if
        $B < 0$.
        Theorems~\ref{thm:semi-r1} and~\ref{thm:semi-r0} are also excluded:
        at an exact depth, $\theta = r_{2d} \oplus r_{2d+1}$
        (Lemma~\ref{lem:exact-step}(3)), so $r_{2d+1}$ is one of the two
        Stern--Brocot parents of $\theta$, and the convergent/semi-convergent
        label of $r_{2d+1}$ is CF-convention-dependent;
        Theorem~\ref{thm:semi-r0} requires irrational $\theta$.
  \item \emph{At the terminal depth.}  $\operatorname{sgn}(\Kval) = \operatorname{sgn}(\Dval)$
        fails ($\Kval = 0$, $|\Dval| = 1$); the pair is a bounded pair if and only if
        $B < 0$ (with $B$ taken at the exact step); Theorem~\ref{thm:last-sturmian} applies to both $r_{2d+2}$ and $r_{2d+3} = \theta$
        (part~(3) of that theorem covers $r = \theta$); and $r_{2d+2} = \rplus$ is a Stern--Brocot descendant of $\theta$, so
        Theorems~\ref{thm:sb-path}, \ref{thm:never-skips}, and~\ref{thm:semi-r0}
        are restricted to approximation depths (the latter two require irrational $\theta$
        and so never encounter the terminal depth).
\end{itemize}
The pair produced at the exact step is $(r_{2d+2}, r_{2d+3}) = (\rplus, \theta)$, where
$\rplus$ lies strictly on the side of $r_{2d+1}$, opposite to $r_{2d}$.
If $B > 0$, both members satisfy $r \geq \theta$ and the pair is not a bounded pair.
If $B < 0$, it is a bounded pair with $\theta$ as its upper member.

%------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Derived Quantities}
\label{sec:derived-quantities}
%------------------------------------------------------------

The following quantities are defined in terms of the primitives of
Section~\ref{sec:definitions} and are used throughout
Sections~\ref{sec:mediant}--\ref{sec:sturmian}.

\begin{definition}[$\Delta$ and $\Theta$]
\label{def:delta-K}
Given approximants $r_{2d} = e_{2d}/o_{2d}$ and $r_{2d+1} = e_{2d+1}/o_{2d+1}$ (in
practice the pair held by the algorithm at depth $d$, which at every approximation depth is a
bounded pair with $|\eps_{2d}| > |\eps_{2d+1}|$), define
\[
  \Dval = e_{2d+1} o_{2d} - e_{2d} o_{2d+1}
\]
and
\[
  \Kval = o_{2d+1}(e_{2d+1} - \theta \cdot o_{2d+1}).
\]
\end{definition}

\subsection*{The invariants $\Delta$ and $\Theta$}

More generally, define $\Kval(r) = o(e - \theta o)$ for any estimate $r = e/o$;
then $\Kval := \Kval(r_{2d+1})$ for the current pair.
Geometrically, $\Kval(r)/o = e - \theta o$ is the signed vertical distance from
the lattice point $(o, e)$ to the line $e = \theta o$, and $|\Kval(r)|$ is twice
the area of the triangle with vertices $(0,0)$, $(o, \theta o)$, $(o, e)$.
Equivalently, $\Kval = \theta\, o_{2d+1}^2 \eps_{2d+1}$ is the signed absolute
error of $r_{2d+1}$ scaled by its denominator squared.
One checks that $|\Kval|/o_{2d+1} = |e_{2d+1} - \theta o_{2d+1}| < 1$ (the good-estimate
condition), and that $\operatorname{sgn}(\Kval) = \operatorname{sgn}(\Dval)$ at every non-terminal
step (i.e.\ when $\Kval \neq 0$).

\subsection*{Step types}
\label{def:step-type}

Let $(r_{2d}, r_{2d+1})$ be the pair held by the algorithm at depth $d$, and write
$A = e_{2d} - \theta\,o_{2d}$ and $B = e_{2d+1} - \theta\,o_{2d+1}$ for the scaled
errors of its members (the quantities of Section~\ref{sec:optimal-w}), so that
$\Kval = o_{2d+1}B$.
We call depth $d$
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{terminal} if $\Kval = 0$, i.e.\ $r_{2d+1} = \theta$: the algorithm halts
        at Step~3 and produces no children;
  \item \emph{approximation} if $\Kval \neq 0$.  An approximation depth is either
  \begin{itemize}
    \item \emph{rational approximation} if $-A/B \notin \mathbb{Z}$:
          Theorem~\ref{thm:optimal-w} and
          Corollary~\ref{cor:children-bounded-pair} apply, and $\wplus$ is the unique weight
          whose consecutive mediants straddle $\theta$; or
    \item \emph{exact} if $-A/B = \wminus \in \mathbb{Z}$: no integer weight straddles
          $\theta$ strictly, the mediant at weight $\wminus$ equals $\theta$, and
          Lemma~\ref{lem:exact-step} (Section~\ref{sec:optimal-w}) gives the full
          description.  The exact step can occur only for rational $\theta$, and when
          it does the next depth is terminal.
  \end{itemize}
\end{itemize}
The word ``non-terminal'' is reserved for approximation depths (rational approximation or exact) as a group.

\subsection*{Bounded pairs and the mediant tree}

A bounded pair is a purely analytic object: any two good estimates that bracket $\theta$
and satisfy the Farey adjacency condition qualify, regardless of how they were found.
The mediant construction (Section~\ref{sec:mediant}) provides a canonical way to
\emph{generate} bounded pairs, but the concept is wider.
The invariants $\Dval$ and $\Kval$ belong to the pair itself; the Christoffel word
structure (Section~\ref{sec:background-deluca}) is a property of how the pair was reached via
the mediant tree.
In particular, $\Dval = \pm 1$ holds for any bounded pair;
$\operatorname{sgn}(\Kval) = \operatorname{sgn}(\Dval)$ holds when $\Kval \neq 0$
(i.e.\ at non-terminal steps).

%------------------------------------------------------------
\section{The Optimal Weight Formula}
\label{sec:optimal-w}
%------------------------------------------------------------

A key question is: for which integer $w \geq 1$ do the two consecutive weighted mediants
\[
  \rminus = \mathrm{Mediant}(r_{2d}, r_{2d+1}, w-1), \qquad \rplus = \mathrm{Mediant}(r_{2d}, r_{2d+1}, w)
\]
straddle $\theta$, i.e.\ satisfy the bracketing condition $(\lamminus - 1)(\lamplus - 1) < 0$?

Writing $A = e_{2d} - \theta\,o_{2d}$ and $B = e_{2d+1} - \theta\,o_{2d+1}$ for the scaled
errors, the bracketing condition becomes $(A + (w-1)B)(A + wB) < 0$.
Observe that $A + (w-1)B$ and $A + wB$ are consecutive evaluations of the linear
function $f(w) = A + wB$ at $w-1$ and $w$: their product is negative iff $f$ changes
sign between $w-1$ and $w$, i.e.\ iff the zero $-A/B$ of $f$ lies in the interval
$(w-1, w)$.
Assuming $-A/B \notin \mathbb{Z}$ (the hypothesis of Theorem~\ref{thm:optimal-w}),
this is equivalent to $w = \lfloor -A/B \rfloor + 1$, and exactly one such $w$ exists.
(Note: $B = e_{2d+1} - \theta\,o_{2d+1} = \Theta/o_{2d+1}$, so $B \neq 0$ iff
$\Theta \neq 0$; the theorem is only invoked at non-terminal steps where $\Theta \neq 0$,
ensuring $-A/B$ is well-defined.)
The unique integer satisfying this bracketing condition is given by the following
closed-form expression.

\begin{theorem}[Optimal weight]
\label{thm:optimal-w}
Let $(r_{2d}, r_{2d+1})$ be a bounded pair bracketing $\theta$ with $-A/B \notin \mathbb{Z}$
(in particular when $\theta$ is irrational), where
\[
  A = e_{2d} - \theta\,o_{2d}, \qquad B = e_{2d+1} - \theta\,o_{2d+1},
\]
and set $\Dval = e_{2d+1}o_{2d} - e_{2d} o_{2d+1}$, $\Kval = o_{2d+1} B$.
Then the unique integer $w \geq 1$ for which $\rminus = \mathrm{Mediant}(r_{2d}, r_{2d+1}, w-1)$
and $\rplus = \mathrm{Mediant}(r_{2d}, r_{2d+1}, w)$ satisfy the bracketing condition
$(\lamminus - 1)(\lamplus - 1) < 0$ is
\[
  \wplus = \left\lfloor \frac{-A}{B} \right\rfloor + 1
      = \left\lfloor \frac{\Dval}{\Kval} - \frac{o_{2d}}{o_{2d+1}} \right\rfloor + 1.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
We prove the closed-form formula $\wplus = \lfloor -A/B \rfloor + 1$ directly from
the bracketing condition, using only a linear sign argument.

Since $r_{2d}$ and $r_{2d+1}$ are on opposite sides of $\theta$ (so $A, B \neq 0$
--- both are non-terminal estimates) we have $\operatorname{sgn}(A) = -\operatorname{sgn}(B)$,
so $A/B < 0$ and $c := -A/B > 0$.
The two weighted mediants are
\[
  \rminus = \frac{e_{2d} + (w-1)\,e_{2d+1}}{o_{2d} + (w-1)\,o_{2d+1}}, \qquad
  \rplus = \frac{e_{2d} + w\,e_{2d+1}}{o_{2d} + w\,o_{2d+1}},
\]
with signed errors (numerators of $\epsminus$, $\epsplus$ up to positive denominators)
\[
  A + (w-1)B \qquad \text{and} \qquad A + wB.
\]

\paragraph{Bracketing condition.}

The pair $(\rminus, \rplus)$ brackets $\theta$ iff $\epsminus$ and $\epsplus$ have opposite signs,
i.e.\ $(A + (w-1)B)(A + wB) < 0$.
Writing $c = -A/B$, this product equals $B^2\,(w - 1 - c)(w - c)$, which is negative
iff $w - 1 - c$ and $w - c$ have opposite signs, i.e.\
\[
  w - 1 < c < w,
\]
and since $c = -A/B \notin \mathbb{Z}$ by hypothesis,
this is equivalent to $w - 1 = \lfloor c \rfloor$, i.e.\ $w = \lfloor c \rfloor + 1$.
The hypothesis ensures exactly one integer $w$ satisfies the condition, namely
\[
  \wplus = \lfloor -A/B \rfloor + 1.
\]
We also write $\wminus = \wplus - 1 = \lfloor -A/B \rfloor$ for the companion weight.

\paragraph{Expression in terms of $\Dval$ and $\Kval$.}

Recall $\Kval = o_{2d+1}B$ and $A\,o_{2d+1} - B\,o_{2d} = -\Dval$, giving
$A = (Bo_{2d} - \Dval)/o_{2d+1}$, so
\[
  \frac{-A}{B} = \frac{\Dval}{o_{2d+1}B} - \frac{o_{2d}}{o_{2d+1}}
               = \frac{\Dval}{\Kval} - \frac{o_{2d}}{o_{2d+1}},
\]
and therefore
\[
  \wplus = \left\lfloor \frac{\Dval}{\Kval} - \frac{o_{2d}}{o_{2d+1}} \right\rfloor + 1,
\]
completing the proof.

\paragraph{Termination.}
When $\Kval = 0$, $r_{2d+1}$ is an exact rational representation of $\theta$;
no further improvement is possible and the algorithm terminates.
\end{proof}

When $\Kval = 0$, $r_{2d+1}$ is an exact rational representation of $\theta$
and no further improvement is possible; the algorithm terminates.

\begin{corollary}[Children at $\wplus$ form a bounded pair]
\label{cor:children-bounded-pair}
Under the conditions of Theorem~\ref{thm:optimal-w}, the two children
$\rminus = \mathrm{Mediant}(r_{2d}, r_{2d+1}, \wminus)$ and
$\rplus = \mathrm{Mediant}(r_{2d}, r_{2d+1}, \wplus)$ form a bounded pair.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Farey adjacency $|\eplus\odenm - \eminus\odenp| = 1$ follows from
Lemma~\ref{lem:mediant-farey}(3), since $\rminus = M_{\wminus}$ and $\rplus = M_{\wplus}$
are consecutive mediants ($\wplus = \wminus + 1$).
It remains to show both children are good estimates.
Using $f(w) = A + wB$ from the Section~\ref{sec:optimal-w} preamble, for each
$w^\dagger \in \{\wminus, \wplus\}$ set $r^\dagger = \mathrm{Mediant}(r_{2d}, r_{2d+1}, w^\dagger)$
with components $(e^\dagger, o^\dagger)$; then $f(w^\dagger) = e^\dagger - \theta\, o^\dagger$
(the scaled error of $r^\dagger$).
The bracketing condition $f(\wminus)\,f(\wplus) < 0$ means the two values have opposite signs,
so
\[
  |f(\wminus)|\,\odenp + |f(\wplus)|\,\odenm
  = |f(\wminus)\,\odenp - f(\wplus)\,\odenm|
  = |\eminus\,\odenp - \eplus\,\odenm|
  = |\Dval| = 1.
\]
Both terms on the left are in fact positive: the bracketing condition
$f(\wminus)\,f(\wplus) < 0$ implies $f(\wminus) \neq 0$ and $f(\wplus) \neq 0$,
so $|f(\wminus)|$ and $|f(\wplus)|$ are both nonzero; and $\odenm, \odenp \geq 1$.
Since two positive terms sum to $1$, each is strictly less than $1$,
i.e.\ $|\eminus - \theta \odenm| < 1$ and $|\eplus - \theta \odenp| < 1$,
confirming both children are good estimates.
Moreover, $\gcd(\eplus, \odenp) = \gcd(\eminus, \odenm) = 1$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:mediant-farey}(4)), so the children are approximants in canonical form.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Tree approximants are good estimates]
\label{lem:tree-good-estimate}
Every approximant produced by the mediant tree is a good estimate of $\theta$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By induction on depth.  The inductive hypothesis is: the pair at depth $d$ is
a bounded pair of good estimates (valid for all depths at which the algorithm
steps, i.e.\ while $\Kval \neq 0$).  The seed pair $(r_0, r_1)$ satisfies this by
assumption.

At each subsequent step, let $(r_{2d}, r_{2d+1})$ be the depth-$d$ bounded pair.
Two cases arise.

\emph{Case 1: $-A/B \notin \mathbb{Z}$.}  The bracketing condition
$f(\wminus)\,f(\wplus) < 0$ implies both $f$-values are nonzero.
The proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:children-bounded-pair} gives
$|f(\wminus)|\,\odenp + |f(\wplus)|\,\odenm = 1$, with both terms positive and
$\odenm, \odenp \geq 1$, so each term is strictly less than~$1$.
Hence $|\eminus - \theta\,\odenm| < 1$ and $|\eplus - \theta\,\odenp| < 1$:
both children are good estimates.  By Corollary~\ref{cor:children-bounded-pair},
they form a bounded pair.

\emph{Case 2: $-A/B = \wminus \in \mathbb{Z}$.}
Then $f(\wminus) = 0$, so $r^- = \theta$ exactly, which is trivially a good estimate.
For the other child, $f(\wplus) = f(\wminus) + B = B$, and $|B| < 1$ because
$r_{2d+1}$ is a good estimate (inductive hypothesis).
Thus $|\eplus - \theta\,\odenp| = |B| < 1$, so $r^+$ is also a good estimate.
(In this case the next depth is terminal; see Lemma~\ref{lem:exact-step}.)
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The exact step]
\label{lem:exact-step}
Let depth $d$ be exact (\emph{Step types}, Section~\ref{sec:derived-quantities}):
$(r_{2d}, r_{2d+1})$ is a bounded pair with $|\eps_{2d}| > |\eps_{2d+1}|$,
$\Kval \neq 0$, and $-A/B = \wminus \in \mathbb{Z}$.
Let $\rminus = \mathrm{Mediant}(r_{2d}, r_{2d+1}, \wminus)$ and
$\rplus = \mathrm{Mediant}(r_{2d}, r_{2d+1}, \wplus)$ with $\wplus = \wminus + 1$.
Then:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item $\wminus \geq 1$, and Step~4 computes
        $\lfloor \Dval/\Kval - o_{2d}/o_{2d+1} \rfloor + 1 = \wminus + 1 = \wplus$;
        in particular $\wplus \geq 2$.
  \item No integer $w$ satisfies $f(w-1)\,f(w) < 0$.
  \item $\rminus = \theta$ exactly and $f(\wplus) = \eplus - \theta\,\odenp = B$;
        moreover $\odenm B = \Dval$, so $|B| = 1/\odenm$ and
        $\operatorname{sgn} B = \operatorname{sgn} \Dval$.
  \item $\rplus$ lies strictly on the same side of $\theta$ as $r_{2d+1}$, i.e.\
        strictly on the opposite side from $r_{2d}$.
  \item $\epsminus = 0$, so $\epsminus\epsplus = 0$; $\epsplus \neq 0$ has the sign
        of $\eps_{2d+1}$, and $|\epsplus| < |\eps_{2d+1}| < |\eps_{2d}|$.
  \item Both children are good estimates, $\eplus\odenm - \eminus\odenp = \Dval$, and
        Step~5 assigns $r_{2d+3} = \rminus = \theta$ and $r_{2d+2} = \rplus$.
  \item $(r_{2d+2}, r_{2d+3}) = (\rplus, \theta)$ is a bounded pair (lower member $\rplus$,
        upper member $\theta$) if and only if $B < 0$.
        If $B > 0$ both members are $\geq \theta$ and the pair is not a bounded pair in
        either orientation.
  \item Depth $d+1$ is terminal: $\Kval(r_{2d+3}) = 0$, while
        $\Dval(r_{2d+2}, r_{2d+3}) = e_{2d+3}o_{2d+2} - e_{2d+2}o_{2d+3} = -\Dval = \mp 1$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Since $(r_{2d}, r_{2d+1})$ is a bounded pair with $\Kval \neq 0$, and
$r_{2d} \neq \theta$ (because $|\eps_{2d}| > |\eps_{2d+1}| \geq 0$), $A$ and $B$
are nonzero with opposite signs; hence $\wminus = -A/B > 0$, i.e.\ $\wminus \geq 1$.
(1)~$\Dval = Bo_{2d} - Ao_{2d+1}$, so
$\Dval/\Kval - o_{2d}/o_{2d+1} = -A/B = \wminus$.
(2)~With $A = -\wminus B$, $f(w-1)f(w) = B^2(w-1-\wminus)(w-\wminus) \geq 0$, a
product of two consecutive integers times $B^2$.
(3)~$f(\wminus) = 0$ gives $\eminus = \theta\,\odenm$, and $f(\wplus) = f(\wminus) + B = B$.
Lemma~\ref{lem:mediant-farey}(3) gives $\eplus\odenm - \eminus\odenp = \Dval$;
substituting $\eminus = \theta\odenm$ yields $\odenm(\eplus - \theta\odenp) = \Dval$.
(4)~$f(\wplus) = B$ has the sign of $B$, which is opposite to that of $A$.
(5)~$\epsminus = f(\wminus)/(\theta\odenm) = 0$, $\epsplus = B/(\theta\odenp)$ and
$\eps_{2d+1} = B/(\theta\,o_{2d+1})$ with $\odenp > o_{2d+1}$.
(6)~$\rminus = \theta$ is trivially a good estimate, and $|\eplus - \theta\odenp| = |B| < 1$
because $r_{2d+1}$ is a good estimate; the Farey identity is Lemma~\ref{lem:mediant-farey}(3),
and the assignment follows from $|\epsminus| = 0 < |\epsplus|$.
(7)~If $B < 0$ then $\rplus < \theta = \rminus$, $\eplus = \lfloor\odenp\theta\rfloor$,
$\eminus = \lceil\odenm\theta\rceil$, and by~(3) $\Dval = \odenm B < 0$, so $\Dval = -1$
and $\eminus\odenp - \eplus\odenm = -\Dval = 1$.
If $B > 0$ then $\rplus > \theta = \rminus$.
(8)~$\Kval(r_{2d+3}) = \odenm(\eminus - \theta\odenm) = 0$, and the determinant is
that of~(6) with the order reversed.
\end{proof}

\noindent
For example, $\theta = 5/3$ with $(r_0, r_1) = (1/1, 2/1)$ has $B = 1/3 > 0$,
$\wminus = 2$ and terminal pair $(7/4,\, 5/3)$, which is not a bounded pair;
$\theta = 4/3$ with $(r_0, r_1) = (2/1, 1/1)$ has $B = -1/3 < 0$, $\wminus = 2$
and terminal pair $(5/4,\, 4/3)$, which is a bounded pair.

\subsection*{The continuous optimum and the discrete formula coincide}

To see why $\wplus$ is the natural choice, consider treating $w$ as a real variable
and finding the stationary point of the error product $f(w-1)\,f(w)$, where
$f(w) = A + wB$.
Differentiating with respect to $w$ and setting the derivative to zero:
\[
  \frac{d}{dw}\bigl[f(w-1)\,f(w)\bigr]
  = B\bigl[(A + wB) + (A + (w-1)B)\bigr] = 0.
\]
Solving gives the unique real critical point
\[
  w^* = \tfrac{1}{2} - \frac{A}{B} = c + \tfrac{1}{2},
\]
where $c = -A/B > 0$.
The product $f(w-1)\,f(w)$ has roots at $w = c$ (where $f(w)=0$) and $w = c+1$
(where $f(w-1)=0$), and $w^* = c + \tfrac{1}{2}$ is their midpoint — the unique
real maximum of $|f(w-1)\,f(w)|$ between the roots.
The unique integer $w$ for which $f(w-1)$ and $f(w)$ have opposite signs
satisfies $c < w < c+1$, i.e.\ $w = \lfloor c \rfloor + 1 = \wplus$.
Since $w^* = c + \tfrac{1}{2}$ also lies in $(c,\, c+1)$, and $\wplus$ is the unique integer
in that interval, $\wplus$ is the nearest integer to $w^*$: both the continuous stationary
point and the sign-change bracket single out the same $\wplus$.

\begin{remark}
This analysis applies at rational approximation steps, where $c = -A/B \notin \mathbb{Z}$ so the
interval $(c,\, c+1)$ contains exactly one integer.
At the exact step $c \in \mathbb{Z}$ and the open interval $(c,\, c+1)$ contains no
integer; instead $w^- = c$ and $w^+ = c+1$ are the two bracket endpoints, and both
children are good estimates (Lemma~\ref{lem:exact-step}(1,2)).
\end{remark}

\paragraph{Algorithm.}
The iteration proceeds as follows.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Set $d = 0$ and $(r_0, r_1) = \mathrm{fit}(\theta,\, \lfloor\theta\rfloor/1,\, \lceil\theta\rceil/1)$.
  \item Compute $\Dval$ and $\Kval$ for the current pair $(r_{2d}, r_{2d+1})$.
  \item If $\Kval = 0$, terminate.
  \item Compute $\wplus = \lfloor \Dval/\Kval - o_{2d}/o_{2d+1} \rfloor + 1$ and set $\wminus = \wplus - 1$.
  \item Set
    \[
      r^- = \mathrm{Mediant}(r_{2d}, r_{2d+1}, \wminus), \qquad
      r^+ = \mathrm{Mediant}(r_{2d}, r_{2d+1}, \wplus).
    \]
    Increment $d$ and set $(r_{2d}, r_{2d+1}) = \mathrm{fit}(\theta,\, r^-,\, r^+)$.
    Repeat from step~2.
\end{enumerate}
When $w^+ \geq 2$, the denominator of each new mediant strictly exceeds both
parent denominators.
When $w^+ = 1$ only one new fraction $r^+$ is produced (the older sibling
$r^-$ coincides with $r_{2d}$, so the generation is gender-preserving),
but $r^+$ has strictly larger denominator.
In either case the denominators of the better estimates grow without bound and
the iteration converges to $\theta$ (Corollary~\ref{cor:convergence}).

%------------------------------------------------------------
\section{The Mediant Construction}
\label{sec:mediant}
%------------------------------------------------------------

The classical \emph{mediant} of two fractions $e_{2d}/o_{2d}$ and $e_{2d+1}/o_{2d+1}$ is
$(e_{2d} + e_{2d+1})/(o_{2d} + o_{2d+1})$.
We generalise this to a weighted family parametrised by a non-negative integer $w$.

\begin{lemma}[Farey-adjacency of mediants]
\label{lem:mediant-farey}
Let $(r, r')$ be a Farey-adjacent pair and $w \geq 0$.  Write
$M_w = \mathrm{Mediant}(r, r', w)$.  Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $\Delta(M_w, r') = \Delta(r, r')$,
      so $M_w$ is Farey-adjacent to $r'$ for all $w \geq 0$.
\item $\Delta(r, M_w) = w \cdot \Delta(r, r')$,
      so $M_w$ is Farey-adjacent to $r$ if and only if $w = 1$.
\item $\Delta(M_{w-1}, M_w) = \Delta(r, r')$ for all $w \geq 1$,
      so consecutive mediants $M_{w-1}$ and $M_w$ are Farey-adjacent.
\item $M_w$ is in lowest terms, i.e.\ $\gcd(e + we', o + wo') = 1$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write $M_w = (e + we', o + wo')$.
For~(1): $\Delta(M_w, r') = e'(o + wo') - (e + we')o' = e'o - eo' = \Delta(r, r')$,
so $|\Delta(M_w, r')| = 1$.
For~(2): $\Delta(r, M_w) = (e + we')o - e(o + wo') = w(e'o - eo') = w \cdot \Delta(r, r')$.
For~(3): $\Delta(M_{w-1}, M_w) = (e+we')(o+(w-1)o') - (e+(w-1)e')(o+wo')
= e'o - eo' = \Delta(r,r')$, so $|\Delta(M_{w-1},M_w)| = 1$.
For~(4): any common divisor $g$ of $e + we'$ and $o + wo'$ also divides
$e'(o+wo') - o'(e+we') = e'o - eo' = \Delta(r,r')$, which has absolute value~$1$,
so $g = 1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Farey adjacency under $x \mapsto 1/(1+x)$]
\label{lem:farey-image}
If $r = (e, o)$ and $r' = (e', o')$ are Farey-adjacent approximants, then
$\hat{\alpha} = (o, e+o)$ and $\hat{\alpha}' = (o', e'+o')$
(i.e.\ $1/(1+r) = o/(e+o)$ and $1/(1+r') = o'/(e'+o')$)
are also Farey-adjacent.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
$\Delta(\hat{\alpha}, \hat{\alpha}') = o'(e+o) - o(e'+o') = o'e - oe' = -\Delta(r, r')$,
so $|\Delta(\hat{\alpha}, \hat{\alpha}')| = |\Delta(r, r')| = 1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Mediant convergence]
\label{thm:mediant-convergence}
Let $(r_{2d}, r_{2d+1})$ be a bounded pair for $\theta$ with
$|\eps_{2d}| > |\eps_{2d+1}|$, and let $w \geq 1$ be a positive integer such that
\[
  \bigl(\mathrm{Mediant}(r_{2d}, r_{2d+1}, w-1),\;
         \mathrm{Mediant}(r_{2d}, r_{2d+1}, w)\bigr)
\]
strictly brackets $\theta$ (i.e.\ $\eps^-\,\eps^+ < 0$, where
$\eps^\pm = e^\pm - \theta\, o^\pm$ are the signed errors of the two candidates).
Write $\rminus = \mathrm{Mediant}(r_{2d}, r_{2d+1}, w-1)$ and
$\rplus = \mathrm{Mediant}(r_{2d}, r_{2d+1}, w)$ for these two candidates.
Then
\[
  |\epsminus| \leq |\eps_{2d}| \qquad \text{and} \qquad |\epsplus| < |\eps_{2d+1}| < |\eps_{2d}|.
\]
(When $w = 1$, $\rminus = r_{2d}$ exactly so $|\epsminus| = |\eps_{2d}|$; when $w \geq 2$,
$|\epsminus| < |\eps_{2d}|$ strictly.)
Moreover $|\eplus\,\odenm - \eminus\,\odenp| = 1$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:mediant-farey}(3), since $\rminus = M_{w-1}$ and $\rplus = M_w$), so $(\rminus, \rplus)$ is Farey-adjacent.
The two mediants are assigned by
$(r_{2d+2}, r_{2d+3}) = \mathrm{fit}(\theta, \rminus, \rplus)$
(Definition~\ref{def:fit}): $r_{2d+3}$ is the better estimate (smaller $|\eps|$)
and $r_{2d+2}$ is the worse.
Since $\rplus$ always satisfies $|\epsplus| < |\eps_{2d+1}|$, we have
$|\eps_{2d+3}| < |\eps_{2d+1}|$ regardless of which candidate is assigned to $r_{2d+3}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The denominators satisfy $\odenm = o_{2d} + (w-1)o_{2d+1} < o_{2d} + w\,o_{2d+1} = \odenp$, so
$\rminus$ and $\rplus$ are distinct.
The ratio $\lamminus$ is a weighted combination of $\lam_{2d}$ and $\lam_{2d+1}$:
\[
  \lamminus = \frac{o_{2d}\,\lam_{2d} + (w-1)o_{2d+1}\,\lam_{2d+1}}{o_{2d} + (w-1)o_{2d+1}}.
\]
When $w = 1$ this gives $\lamminus = \lam_{2d}$ exactly, so $|\epsminus| = |\eps_{2d}|$.
When $w \geq 2$, $\rminus$ lies strictly between $r_{2d}$ and $r_{2d+1}$.
Write $f(w) = A + wB$ where $A = e_{2d} - \theta o_{2d}$ and $B = e_{2d+1} - \theta o_{2d+1}$
are the scaled errors of $r_{2d}$ and $r_{2d+1}$; then $f(w) = \eplus - \theta\,\odenp$.
From the bracketing hypothesis $f(w-1)f(w) < 0$ it follows that $B \neq 0$
(otherwise $f$ would be constant and could not change sign), and that the zero
$c = -A/B$ of $f$ lies in $(w-1, w)$.
Thus $f(w-1)$ has the sign of $A$ (the side of $r_{2d}$) and $f(w)$ has the sign of $B$
(the side of $r_{2d+1}$).
Since the bracketing condition places $\rminus$ on the same side of $\theta$ as $r_{2d}$,
the ratio $\rminus/\theta$ lies between $r_{2d}/\theta$ and $1$,
so $|\lamminus - 1| < |\lam_{2d} - 1|$, i.e.\ $|\epsminus| < |\eps_{2d}|$
(this strict inequality holds for $w \ge 2$; for $w = 1$, $\rminus = r_{2d}$ and equality holds,
as noted in the statement).
For $\rplus$ (weight $w \geq 1$), the bracketing condition places $\rplus$ on the same side
of $\theta$ as $r_{2d+1}$, so $\rplus/\theta$ lies between $r_{2d+1}/\theta$ and $1$,
giving $|\lamplus - 1| < |\lam_{2d+1} - 1|$, i.e.\ $|\epsplus| < |\eps_{2d+1}| < |\eps_{2d}|$.
The Farey adjacency condition for $(\rminus, \rplus)$ follows by direct expansion:
\begin{align*}
  \eplus\,\odenm - \eminus\,\odenp
    &= (e_{2d} + w\,e_{2d+1})(o_{2d} + (w-1)o_{2d+1})
     - (e_{2d} + (w-1)e_{2d+1})(o_{2d} + w\,o_{2d+1}) \\
    &= \bigl[(w-1) - w\bigr]e_{2d}\,o_{2d+1}
     + \bigl[w - (w-1)\bigr]e_{2d+1}\,o_{2d} \\
    &= -e_{2d}\,o_{2d+1} + e_{2d+1}\,o_{2d} = \Dval,
\end{align*}
so $|\eplus\,\odenm - \eminus\,\odenp| = |\Dval| = 1$, and the Farey adjacency of $(\rminus, \rplus)$
is inherited directly from that of $(r_{2d}, r_{2d+1})$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Straddling inequality]
\label{cor:straddling}
Under the conditions of Theorem~\ref{thm:mediant-convergence},
\[
  \epsminus\,\epsplus < 0 \qquad \text{and} \qquad |\epsminus||\epsplus| < |\eps_{2d}|\,|\eps_{2d+1}|.
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The first inequality holds because $\rminus$ and $\rplus$ straddle $\theta$, so their errors
have opposite signs.
For the second: $|\epsminus| \leq |\eps_{2d}|$ and $|\epsplus| < |\eps_{2d+1}|$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:mediant-convergence}), so
$|\epsminus||\epsplus| \leq |\eps_{2d}||\epsplus| < |\eps_{2d}|\,|\eps_{2d+1}|$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The exact step]
At the exact step (rational $\theta$, $-A/B = \wminus \in \mathbb{Z}$) the strict
bracketing hypothesis of Theorem~\ref{thm:mediant-convergence} fails, because
$f(\wminus) = 0$.  All conclusions of the theorem nevertheless hold: $|\epsminus| = 0 <
|\eps_{2d}|$, $|\epsplus| < |\eps_{2d+1}| < |\eps_{2d}|$, $(\rminus, \rplus)$ is
Farey-adjacent, and $|\eps_{2d+3}| = 0 < |\eps_{2d+1}|$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:exact-step}).
In Corollary~\ref{cor:straddling} the second inequality holds (its left side is~$0$),
but the first fails: $\epsminus\epsplus = 0$.
\end{remark}

%------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Mediant Words and the Mediant Tree}
\label{sec:parity}
%------------------------------------------------------------

The mediant construction admits a compact combinatorial encoding.
Every estimate produced by the algorithm is either a \emph{leaf} or a \emph{branch},
and the recursive structure of all estimates defines a binary tree whose nodes carry
both rational values and combinatorial words over $\{\OO, \EE\}$.

%------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Leaf words}
\label{sec:parity-leaf}
%------------------------------------------------------------

\begin{definition}[Leaf mediant word]
\label{def:parity-alphabet}
Let $\{\OO, \EE\}^*$ denote the set of finite strings over the two-letter alphabet
$\{\OO, \EE\}$.
A \emph{leaf} estimate has denominator $o = 1$, i.e.\ it is a base estimate $e/1$
for some positive integer $e$.
Its \emph{mediant word} is
\[
  \pi(e/1) = \underbrace{\EE \cdots \EE}_{e} \OO,
\]
a string of $e$ copies of $\EE$ followed by one $\OO$.
\end{definition}

The two leaf estimates that initialise the algorithm are $\lfloor\theta\rfloor/1$
and $\lceil\theta\rceil/1$, ordered as $(r_0, r_1)$ so that $r_1$ is the better estimate
(i.e.\ $|\eps_{r_1}| < |\eps_{r_0}|$).
Their mediant words are $\EE^{\lfloor\theta\rfloor}\OO$ and $\EE^{\lceil\theta\rceil}\OO$
respectively.
We assume $\theta \notin \mathbb{Z}$ (for $\theta \in \mathbb{Z}$ the approximant
$\theta/1$ is exact and the algorithm is not needed).
Then $\lceil\theta\rceil = \lfloor\theta\rfloor + 1$, so $\pi(r_0)$ and $\pi(r_1)$
differ by exactly one $\EE$.
If $\{\theta\} = 1/2$ the two leaves have equal $|\eps|$; we adopt the tie-break
$r_1 = \lfloor\theta\rfloor/1$ (so $r_0 = \lceil\theta\rceil/1$),
so depth~$0$ is an exact step with $\wminus = 1$.
Each word $\EE^k\OO$ contains exactly one $\OO$, so it is not a power of a shorter
word; both $\pi(r_0)$ and $\pi(r_1)$ are therefore primitive.
Moreover, $\EE^k\OO$ is the lower Christoffel word $\mathrm{Ch}^-(k, 1)$
(Definition~\ref{def:christoffel}): its $\OO$ appears at position $k$, matching
the Christoffel formula.
Thus both leaf words are lower Christoffel words of their respective fractions.

%------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Branch words and the weighted operators}
\label{sec:parity-branch}
%------------------------------------------------------------

Each branch step produces two new mediant words from the parent pair
$(u, v) = (\pi(r_{2d-2}),\allowbreak\, \pi(r_{2d-1}))$ by concatenation:

\begin{definition}[Branch mediant word]
\label{def:parity-sequence}
Let $d \geq 1$ be such that depth $d-1$ is an approximation depth, so that the algorithm produced the
pair $(r_{2d}, r_{2d+1})$ from $(r_{2d-2}, r_{2d-1})$.
This includes the terminal pair for rational $\theta$; $(r_{2d}, r_{2d+1})$ is a bounded
pair unless depth $d$ is terminal and the exact step had $B > 0$.
The pair consists of estimates $r_{2d}$ and $r_{2d+1}$,
with $r_{2d+1}$ always the better estimate (smaller $|\eps|$).
Each estimate $r_{2d}$ or $r_{2d+1}$ has two \emph{ordered parents}
$(r_{2d-2}, r_{2d-1})$ and a non-negative integer \emph{weight} $w$
(equal to zero only when $w^+_{d-1} = 1$, in which case $r_{2d} = r_{2d-2}$).
The two estimates $r_{2d}$ and $r_{2d+1}$ carry weights $w^-_{d-1}$ and $w^+_{d-1}$.
We distinguish two uses of the word \emph{weight}:
\begin{itemize}
  \item The \emph{inherited weights} $w_{2d}$ and $w_{2d+1}$ of the two estimates
        $r_{2d}$ and $r_{2d+1}$ are the weights with which they were formed from
        the pair $(r_{2d-2}, r_{2d-1})$ at depth $d-1$ --- specifically
        $\{w_{2d}, w_{2d+1}\} = \{w^-_{d-1}, w^+_{d-1}\}$ with $w_{2d} = w^+_{d-1} - 1$
        and $w_{2d+1} = w^+_{d-1}$ (or vice versa, depending on which child is the better
        estimate). Each inherited weight is used to form exactly one of the two
        estimates: $r_{2d} = \mathrm{Mediant}(r_{2d-2}, r_{2d-1}, w_{2d})$ and
        $r_{2d+1} = \mathrm{Mediant}(r_{2d-2}, r_{2d-1}, w_{2d+1})$.
  \item The \emph{propagated weights} $w^-_d$ and $w^+_d$ of a bounded pair $(r_{2d}, r_{2d+1})$
        are the optimal weights computed \emph{from} that pair to produce the next
        pair $(r_{2d+2}, r_{2d+3})$ --- computed by Step~4
        (Theorem~\ref{thm:optimal-w} at rational approximation depths; Lemma~\ref{lem:exact-step}(1)
        at the exact depth).
\end{itemize}
These differ in general: the inherited weights $w_{2d}$ and $w_{2d+1}$ record how $r_{2d}$ and $r_{2d+1}$ were born;
the propagated weights $w^-_d$ and $w^+_d$ determine where the algorithm goes next.
Write $u = \pi(r_{2d-2})$ and $v = \pi(r_{2d-1})$ for the \emph{parent} mediant words
(at depth $d-1$; these become the parents of the depth-$d$ pair).

The two branch words at each depth share the same parents and differ only in the
multiplicity of $v$: one estimate uses weight $w^+_{d-1}$ and the other uses weight $w^-_{d-1}$.
Which is the better estimate depends on the errors: writing $\epsminus$ and $\epsplus$ for
the errors of the two candidates (Definition~\ref{def:lambda-eps}),
\[
  w_{2d+1} - w_{2d} = \begin{cases} +1 & \text{if } |\epsplus| < |\epsminus|, \\ -1 & \text{otherwise.}\end{cases}
\]
In either case $|\pi(r_{2d+1})| - |\pi(r_{2d})| = \pm|\pi(r_{2d-1})|$.

The branch mediant words are (using $w = w^+_{d-1}$, the propagated optimal weight of the parent pair $(r_{2d-2}, r_{2d-1})$, and $\Dval$ the determinant of that parent pair):
\begin{itemize}
  \item $\Dval > 0$ ($r_{2d-1}$ is the upper bound, $v$ plays the role of $\EE$):
    \begin{align*}
      \pi(r^-) &= v^{w^+_{d-1}-1} \cdot u, \\
      \pi(r^+) &= v^{w^+_{d-1}} \cdot u.
    \end{align*}
  \item $\Dval < 0$ ($r_{2d-1}$ is the lower bound, $v$ plays the role of $\OO$):
    \begin{align*}
      \pi(r^-) &= u \cdot v^{w^+_{d-1}-1}, \\
      \pi(r^+) &= u \cdot v^{w^+_{d-1}}.
    \end{align*}
\end{itemize}
Which of $r^-$ and $r^+$ is the better estimate $r_{2d+1}$ (and which is $r_{2d}$)
is determined by comparing $|\epsminus|$ and $|\epsplus|$; the assignment is not
fixed in advance.
\end{definition}

\textbf{Concrete example} (for $\theta = \log_2 3$: $w = 2$, depth-$0$ leaves
$\pi(r_0) = \mathtt{EO}$, $\pi(r_1) = \mathtt{EEO}$, $\Dval > 0$).
Write $\Phi^w(v, u) = (v^w \cdot u,\; v^{w-1} \cdot u)$ for the pair of branch words
at weight $w$ (the $\Dval > 0$ case):
\[
  \Phi^2\bigl(\mathtt{EEO},\, \mathtt{EO}\bigr)
  = \bigl(\mathtt{EEO}^2\,\mathtt{EO},\; \mathtt{EEO}\,\mathtt{EO}\bigr)
  = \bigl(\mathtt{EEOEEOEO},\; \mathtt{EEOEO}\bigr),
\]
so $\pi(r_3) = \mathtt{EEOEEOEO}$ and $\pi(r_2) = \mathtt{EEOEO}$
(the second component of $\Phi^2(\mathtt{EEO}, \mathtt{EO})$, i.e.\ the weight-$w^- = 1$ child).

%------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Mediant words are Christoffel words}
\label{sec:parity-standard}
%------------------------------------------------------------

A word is \emph{primitive} if it is not a power $x^k$ of a strictly shorter word $x$
(with $k \geq 2$).

\begin{lemma}[Every mediant word is a lower Christoffel word]
\label{lem:branch-standard-pair}
For every estimate $r = (e, o)$ produced by the mediant tree,
$\pi(r) = \mathrm{Ch}^-(e, o)$, the lower Christoffel word at slope $o/(e+o)$.
In particular, $\pi(r)$ is never an upper Christoffel word, since by
Theorem~\ref{thm:parity-ends-O} every mediant word ends in $\OO$, whereas upper
Christoffel words end in $\EE$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By structural induction on the mediant tree.

\emph{Base case.} By §\ref{sec:parity-leaf}, $\pi(e/1) = \EE^e\OO = \mathrm{Ch}^-(e, 1)$.

\emph{Inductive step.}
Let $(r_{2d}, r_{2d+1})$ be a bounded pair with $\Delta = e_{2d+1} o_{2d} - e_{2d} o_{2d+1}$.
By induction $\pi(r_{2d}) = \mathrm{Ch}^-(e_{2d}, o_{2d})$ and $\pi(r_{2d+1}) = \mathrm{Ch}^-(e_{2d+1}, o_{2d+1})$,
both lower Christoffel words (ending in $\OO$), with slopes $o_{2d}/L_{2d}$ and $o_{2d+1}/L_{2d+1}$
(where $L_k = e_k + o_k$) on opposite sides of $\alpha = 1/(1+\theta)$.

When $\Delta > 0$: $r_{2d+1}$ is the upper estimate so $e_{2d+1}/o_{2d+1} > \theta$,
giving $o_{2d+1}/L_{2d+1} < \alpha < o_{2d}/L_{2d}$.
The new word is $v^w u = \pi(r_{2d+1})^w \cdot \pi(r_{2d})$: the smaller-slope word
$\pi(r_{2d+1})$ repeated $w$ times, followed by $\pi(r_{2d})$ once.
Since $(r_{2d}, r_{2d+1})$ is Farey-adjacent, Lemma~\ref{lem:farey-image} gives
$o_{2d} L_{2d+1} - o_{2d+1} L_{2d} = 1$, the Farey hypothesis of
Theorem~\ref{thm:christoffel-concat} (with $e_a = e_{2d+1}$, $e_b = e_{2d}$),
so this equals $\mathrm{Ch}^-(w\,e_{2d+1} + e_{2d},\; w\,o_{2d+1} + o_{2d})$.

When $\Delta < 0$: $r_{2d+1}$ is the lower estimate so $e_{2d+1}/o_{2d+1} < \theta$,
giving $o_{2d}/L_{2d} < \alpha < o_{2d+1}/L_{2d+1}$.
Thus $\pi(r_{2d})$ has the smaller slope and $\pi(r_{2d+1})$ has the larger slope.
The new word is $u v^w = \pi(r_{2d}) \cdot \pi(r_{2d+1})^w$: the single smaller-slope
word followed by the larger-slope word repeated $w$ times.
Since $(r_{2d}, r_{2d+1})$ is Farey-adjacent, Lemma~\ref{lem:farey-image} gives
$o_{2d+1} L_{2d} - o_{2d} L_{2d+1} = 1$, the Farey hypothesis of
Theorem~\ref{thm:christoffel-concat} (with $e_a = e_{2d}$, $e_b = e_{2d+1}$),
so this equals $\mathrm{Ch}^-(e_{2d} + w\,e_{2d+1},\; o_{2d} + w\,o_{2d+1})$.

In both cases $\gcd(e_{2d} + w e_{2d+1},\; o_{2d} + w o_{2d+1}) = 1$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:mediant-farey}(4), since $(r_{2d}, r_{2d+1})$ is Farey-adjacent),
confirming the result is a valid lower Christoffel word.
\end{proof}

The Christoffel identification is the key fact connecting the mediant algorithm
to the combinatorics of Sturmian words.
The recurrence $\pi(r_{2d+3}) = v^{w^\pm_d} \cdot u$ (or $u \cdot v^{w^\pm_d}$),
where $u = \pi(r_{2d})$ and $v = \pi(r_{2d+1})$ are the parent words at depth $d$
and $w^\pm_d$ is the propagated weight of the pair $(r_{2d}, r_{2d+1})$,
matches the standard sequence generation rule for Sturmian words
(Widmer~\cite{widmer2010}, Proposition~2.4.6).

\begin{remark}
The Christoffel tree --- the binary tree whose nodes are lower Christoffel words
under the same concatenation rules --- is a classical object (Berstel et
al.~\cite{berstel2009}, Chapter~2).
Lemma~\ref{lem:branch-standard-pair} says that the mediant algorithm traverses
exactly this tree: every estimate produced is a node of the Christoffel tree, and
the branch rule is the Christoffel concatenation.
The novelty is not the tree itself but the \emph{driving criterion}: the optimal
weight $\wplus$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:optimal-w}) selects the unique child that
straddles $\theta$, making the traversal goal-directed rather than exhaustive.
\end{remark}

%------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{The mediant tree}
\label{sec:tree}
%------------------------------------------------------------

The mediant tree is the practical device for generating mediant words of arbitrary
length and precision.
Given a target $\theta$ and a desired precision, one runs the algorithm until the
bounded pair $(r_{2d}, r_{2d+1})$ achieves the required length $e_{2d+1} + o_{2d+1}$.
The mediant word $\pi(r_{2d+1})$ is then read off the tree by the recursive rule:
an interior node $\mathrm{Mediant}(s, t, w)$ with $\Delta > 0$ yields
$\pi(t)^w \cdot \pi(s)$, and with $\Delta < 0$ yields
$\pi(s) \cdot \pi(t)^w$.
Deeper trees produce longer prefixes of the Sturmian word with slope $1/(\theta+1)$
(Section~\ref{sec:sturmian}).

\begin{definition}[Mediant tree]
\label{def:mediant-tree}
The \emph{mediant tree} of an estimate $r$ is defined recursively:
\begin{itemize}
  \item If $r \in \{\lfloor \theta \rfloor, \lceil \theta \rceil\}$, then $r$ is a
        \emph{leaf node}.
  \item Otherwise $r$ is an \emph{interior node} $\mathrm{Mediant}(s,\, t,\, w)$
        with left subtree $s$, right subtree $t$, and integer weight $w \geq 0$,
        representing the rational
        \[
          r = \frac{e_s + w\,e_t}{o_s + w\,o_t}.
        \]
\end{itemize}
\end{definition}

The two leaves are the unique consecutive integers bracketing $\theta$,
forming the initial bounded pair $(r_0, r_1)$, ordered so that $r_1$ is the better estimate
(i.e.\ $|\varepsilon_{r_1}| < |\varepsilon_{r_0}|$).
Each bounded pair $(r_{2d}, r_{2d+1})$ in the algorithm's sequence consists of two
mediant trees sharing the same leaves; as the iteration advances the trees grow deeper,
recording the full construction history of each estimate.

A detailed worked example for $\theta = \log_2 3$, tracing the first four steps of the
algorithm and the corresponding mediant word decompositions, is given in
Appendix~\ref{app:examples}.

%------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{The mediant word encodes the fraction}
\label{sec:parity-encoding}
%------------------------------------------------------------

By Lemma~\ref{lem:branch-standard-pair}, the mediant word $\pi(r)$ of an estimate
$r = e/o$ is uniquely determined by its fraction: $\pi(r) = \mathrm{Ch}^-(e, o)$.
The fraction $e/o$ records the full construction history of $r$ via the Euclidean
structure of the mediant algorithm.
Conversely, the fraction $e/o$ can be read off from $\pi(r)$ by counting $\EE$'s and
$\OO$'s.
As the iteration advances, the ratio of $\EE$'s to $\OO$'s in $\pi(r_{2d+1})$ converges to
$\theta$, with strict improvement guaranteed across iterations
(Theorem~\ref{thm:convergence}) but not within a single depth
(the worse estimate $r_{2d}$ need not improve).

\section{Convergence Analysis}
\label{sec:convergence}
%------------------------------------------------------------

\begin{theorem}[Strict decrease of $|\eps|$]
\label{thm:convergence}
At each iteration of the algorithm, $|\eps_{2d+1}|$ strictly decreases.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By Theorem~\ref{thm:mediant-convergence}, $|\epsplus| < |\eps_{2d+1}|$.
The next iterate assigns the better of $\rminus$ and $\rplus$ as $r_{2d+3}$, so
$|\eps_{2d+3}| \leq |\epsplus| < |\eps_{2d+1}|$.
(At the exact step for rational $\theta$, $r^- = \theta$ exactly so
$|\eps^-| = 0 < |\eps_{2d+1}|$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:exact-step}(3)); the better
child is $r^-$ and the conclusion holds trivially.)
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Convergence to $\theta$]
\label{cor:convergence}
The sequence of better estimates produced by the algorithm satisfies $\eps_{2d+1} \to 0$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
From the proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:children-bounded-pair} (at a rational approximation step; at the
exact step the same identity holds with $f(\wminus) = 0$, since
$|f(\wminus)\odenp - f(\wplus)\odenm| = |\Dval| = 1$ for any two consecutive weights
and the product $f(\wminus)f(\wplus) \leq 0$), with $f(w^\dagger) = A + w^\dagger B$
the linear error function and $\odenp, \odenm$ the two child denominators:
\[
  |f(\wminus)|\,\odenp + |f(\wplus)|\,\odenm = |\Dval| = 1.
\]
Since $f(w^\dagger) = e^\dagger - \theta\,o^\dagger$ and $\eps^\dagger = f(w^\dagger)/(\theta\,o^\dagger)$
for $r^\dagger = \mathrm{Mediant}(r_{2d}, r_{2d+1}, w^\dagger)$, substituting
$|f(w^\dagger)| = |\eps^\dagger|\,\theta\,o^\dagger$ gives
\[
  \bigl(|\epsminus| + |\epsplus|\bigr)\,\theta\,\odenm\,\odenp = 1,
\]
so in particular the better child satisfies
\[
  |\eps_{\mathrm{better}}| \leq \frac{1}{2\,\theta\,\odenm\,\odenp}.
\]
(When $w^+ = 1$, so $\wminus = 0$: $r^- = r_{2d}$ coincides with the parent and is
not a new fraction; the better child is $r^+$ in this case since
$|\epsplus| < |\eps_{2d+1}| < |\eps_{2d}|$.
If the better child is $r^-$, then $w^- \geq 1$ and $o^- = o_{2d} + (w^+-1)\,o_{2d+1} > o_{2d+1}$.
In either case the bound $|\eps_\mathrm{better}| \leq 1/(2\theta\,\odenm\,\odenp)$ holds.)
Since $\odenp = o_{2d} + \wplus\,o_{2d+1} \geq o_{2d} + o_{2d+1} > o_{2d+1}$, the
denominator of the better child strictly exceeds $o_{2d+1}$; by induction the
denominators diverge.
Since $|\eps_{\mathrm{better}}| \leq 1/(2\,\theta\,\odenm\,\odenp)$ and
$\odenp \to \infty$, we have $\eps_{2d+1} \to 0$.
For rational $\theta$ the sequence is finite and its last term has $\eps = 0$.
\end{proof}
\begin{theorem}[MT fractions lie on the Stern--Brocot path]
\label{thm:sb-path}
Every fraction appearing as $r_{2d}$ or $r_{2d+1}$ at an approximation depth, together with
$\theta$ itself when $\theta$ is rational, is a convergent or semi-convergent of
$\theta$, i.e.\ lies on the Stern--Brocot path of $\theta$.
(For rational $\theta$ the other member $r_{2d+2} = \rplus$ of the terminal pair is a
child of $\theta$ in the Stern--Brocot tree and is not on the path.)
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The initial bounds $\lfloor\theta\rfloor/1$ and $\lceil\theta\rceil/1$ are
consecutive integers, hence Farey adjacent, and both lie on the Stern--Brocot
path of $\theta$.

At each depth $d$, the algorithm forms the two mediants
$r^\pm = \mathrm{Mediant}(r_{2d}, r_{2d+1}, w^\pm)$ with $w^- = w^+ - 1$.
For $w \geq 2$ these are \emph{weighted} mediants, not ordinary mediants of
$(r_{2d}, r_{2d+1})$, so the one-step argument does not apply directly.
Instead, use the identity
\[
  P + w Q = (P + (w-1)Q) \oplus Q,
  \qquad
  \det(P+(w-1)Q,\, Q) = \det(P, Q),
\]
which shows that $\mathrm{Mediant}(r_{2d}, r_{2d+1}, w)$ is the ordinary mediant of
$(r_{2d} + (w-1)\,r_{2d+1},\; r_{2d+1})$, a Farey-adjacent pair with the same
determinant as $(r_{2d}, r_{2d+1})$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:mediant-farey}(1)).
By induction on $w = 1, \ldots, w^+$: the pair
$(r_{2d} + (w-1)\,r_{2d+1},\; r_{2d+1})$ is Farey adjacent and straddles
$\theta$ for each $w \leq w^+$ at a rational approximation step, and for each $w \leq w^-$ at the
exact step (where the node at $w = w^-$ is $\theta = r^-$, the end of the path).
By the standard Stern--Brocot lemma, any Farey-adjacent pair $(P, Q)$ of
non-negative fractions defines a unique SB-tree interval, and $\theta$
lies in the interval of $(P, Q)$ if and only if $P \oplus Q$ lies on the
SB path to $\theta$; so its ordinary mediant
$r_{2d} + w\,r_{2d+1}$ is a node on the Stern--Brocot path of $\theta$.
The case $w^- = 0$ (i.e.\ $w^+ = 1$) is immediate: $r^- = r_{2d}$ is
already on the path by inductive hypothesis, and $r^+ = r_{2d} + r_{2d+1}$
is the unique ordinary mediant of the Farey pair $(r_{2d}, r_{2d+1})$.
Both $r^-$ and $r^+$ are produced this way at a rational approximation step, so both lie on the path.
(At the exact step $r^- = \theta = r_{2d+3}$ is the endpoint of the SB path and lies on
the path by definition; $r^+ = r_{2d+2}$ lies on the same side of $\theta$ as $r_{2d+1}$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:exact-step}(4)) and is a child of $\theta$ in the SB tree, hence not on
the path --- which is why the theorem statement excludes it explicitly.)
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[MT never skips a convergent]
\label{thm:never-skips}
  Assume $\theta$ is irrational.
  Every CF convergent $p_k/q_k$ of $\theta$ appears as $r_{2d}$ or $r_{2d+1}$
  in some MT pair (a bounded pair at an approximation depth, or the terminal pair).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
A convergent $p_k/q_k$ is characterised as the \emph{last} node in a run of
same-side steps on the SB path of $\theta$, i.e.\ the node just before the path
crosses to the other side.
In the induction of Theorem~\ref{thm:sb-path}, the path nodes visited within
stage $d$ are $r_{2d} + w\,r_{2d+1}$ for $w = 1, \ldots, w^+$.
For $w < w^+$ the node lies on the same side of $\theta$ as $r_{2d}$.
When $w^+ \geq 2$, $r^- = r_{2d} + (w^+-1)\,r_{2d+1}$ has the same gender as
$r_{2d}$ and is the last node on $r_{2d}$'s side, so $r^-$ is a convergent.
(When $w^+ = 1$ the generation is gender-preserving and $r_{2d}$ itself is
carried forward as the older sibling; it is a convergent from the previous stage.)

We show that every convergent appears in the MT sequence.
After the integer spine and the two leaves, the Stern--Brocot path of $\theta$
is the concatenation of the stage blocks
$\{r_{2d} + w\,r_{2d+1} : 1 \le w \le w^+_d\}$, $d = 0, 1, 2, \ldots$.
(After the last node $r^+$ of block $d-1$, the SB interval is
$(r_{2d}, r_{2d+1})$, so the next path node is $r_{2d} \oplus r_{2d+1}$,
the first node of block $d$.)
Within a block, the nodes with $w < w^+_d$ lie on the same side of $\theta$ as
$r_{2d}$, and $r^+$ lies on the other side.
The nodes that end a run---and hence are convergents---are:
$r^- = r_{2d} + (w^+_d - 1)\,r_{2d+1}$ (when $w^+_d \ge 2$);
$r^+$;
and, when $w^+_d = 1$, $r_{2d}$ itself (carried forward from the previous block).
All of these are members of an MT pair.
Together with the leaf $\lfloor\theta\rfloor/1 = p_0/q_0$, every convergent appears.
\end{proof}

The next two results give precise characterisations of which MT fractions are
semi-convergents rather than convergents.

\begin{remark}[Integer leaves]
\label{rem:d0-leaves}
  $\lfloor\theta\rfloor/1 = p_0/q_0$ is always a convergent.
  If $\lceil\theta\rceil/1$ is the better leaf (i.e.\ $|\varepsilon_{\lceil\theta\rceil}| <
  |\varepsilon_{\lfloor\theta\rfloor}|$, which holds iff $\{\theta\} > 1/2$),
  then $\{\theta\} > 1/2$ forces $a_1 = 1$, so $\lceil\theta\rceil/1 = p_1/q_1$ is the
  first convergent.
  If $\lfloor\theta\rfloor/1$ is the better leaf (i.e.\ $\{\theta\} < 1/2$),
  then $\lceil\theta\rceil/1$ is a semi-convergent iff $a_1 > 1$, and a convergent iff
  $a_1 = 1$.
  In either ordering, the better leaf $r_1$ is always a convergent.
  Leaves carry no weight, so the weight condition of Theorem~\ref{thm:semi-r1}
  does not apply at $d = 0$; instead the $d = 0$ case is handled directly in the proof.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[Semi-convergent characterisation of $r_{2d+1}$]
\label{thm:semi-r1}
  For all $d \geq 0$ such that depth $d$ is not exact, $r_{2d+1}$ is a
  semi-convergent of $\theta$ if and only if $w^+_d = 1$, i.e.\ the propagated
  weight of the pair $(r_{2d}, r_{2d+1})$ equals~$1$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Write $A_d = e_{2d} - \theta o_{2d}$ and $B_d = e_{2d+1} - \theta o_{2d+1}$ for the
signed errors of the current pair, so $w^+_d = \lfloor|A_d|/|B_d|\rfloor + 1$.

\medskip
\textbf{Case $d \ge 1$.}
Let $r^-_{d-1}, r^+_{d-1}$ be the children formed at depth $d-1$, with $o^-_{d-1} < o^+_{d-1}$.
The new pair is $(r_{2d}, r_{2d+1}) = (r^-_{d-1}, r^+_{d-1})$ or $(r^+_{d-1}, r^-_{d-1})$ according to which
has smaller $|\varepsilon|$; by convention $r_{2d+1}$ is the better estimate, so
either $r_{2d+1} = r^+_{d-1}$ (a gender-preserving generation) or $r_{2d+1} = r^-_{d-1}$
(a gender-changing generation).

\textit{Case $r_{2d+1} = r^+_{d-1}$} (gender-preserving generation: $r^+_{d-1}$ is the younger sibling).
After the path visits $r^+_{d-1}$, the SB interval narrows to $(r^-_{d-1}, r^+_{d-1}) = (r_{2d}, r_{2d+1})$,
so the next path node is $r_{2d} \oplus r_{2d+1}$.
By definition of $w^+_d = \lfloor|A_d|/|B_d|\rfloor + 1$,
$w^+_d = 1$ iff $|A_d| < |B_d|$, i.e.\ $r_{2d} \oplus r_{2d+1}$ lies on the same side as $r_{2d+1}$,
i.e.\ the SB path continues on $r_{2d+1}$'s side; when this holds, $r^+_{d-1}$ is not the last node of its run.
Hence $r^+_{d-1}$ is the last node of its run (a convergent) iff $w^+_d \ge 2$, and a
semi-convergent iff $w^+_d = 1$.

\textit{Case $r_{2d+1} = r^-_{d-1}$} (gender-changing generation: $r^-_{d-1}$ is the younger sibling).
Since $r_{2d+1} = r^-_{d-1}$ is the better estimate, we have $o_{2d+1} = o^-_{d-1} < o^+_{d-1} = o_{2d}$
(as $w^-_{d-1} < w^+_{d-1}$ implies $o^-_{d-1} < o^+_{d-1}$).
By Theorem~\ref{thm:sb-path}, the SB path visits $r^-_{d-1}$ at weight $w = w^+_{d-1} - 1$
within stage $d-1$, immediately before crossing to $r^+_{d-1}$ at weight $w^+_{d-1}$.
Hence $r^-_{d-1}$ is the last same-side node before the crossing, i.e.\ a convergent.
For the weight condition: since $r_{2d+1} = r^-_{d-1}$ is the better estimate,
$|\varepsilon_{2d+1}| < |\varepsilon_{2d}|$, i.e.\ $|B_d|/o_{2d+1} < |A_d|/o_{2d}$ in terms
of relative errors.
In terms of scaled errors, $|A_d| = \theta\,o_{2d}\,|\varepsilon_{2d}|$ and
$|B_d| = \theta\,o_{2d+1}\,|\varepsilon_{2d+1}|$, so
\[
  \frac{|A_d|}{|B_d|} = \frac{o_{2d}}{o_{2d+1}} \cdot \frac{|\varepsilon_{2d}|}{|\varepsilon_{2d+1}|} > 1,
\]
since both factors exceed $1$ ($o_{2d} > o_{2d+1}$ and $|\varepsilon_{2d}| > |\varepsilon_{2d+1}|$).
Hence $|A_d| > |B_d|$ and $w^+_d = \lfloor|A_d|/|B_d|\rfloor + 1 \ge 2$.
The statement holds in this case too.

\medskip
\textbf{Case $d = 0$.}
The seed pair is $(r_0, r_1) = (\lfloor\theta\rfloor/1, \lceil\theta\rceil/1)$
or $(\lceil\theta\rceil/1, \lfloor\theta\rfloor/1)$, ordered so that $r_1$ is the
better estimate (smaller $|\varepsilon|$).
Write $\gamma = \{\theta\} \in (0,1)$ for the fractional part of $\theta$.
For the integer leaves $o_0 = o_1 = 1$, the signed errors satisfy
$|A_0| = \theta\,|\varepsilon_{r_0}|$ and $|B_0| = \theta\,|\varepsilon_{r_1}|$.
The values $|\varepsilon_{r_0}|$ and $|\varepsilon_{r_1}|$ are $\{1-\gamma, \gamma-0\} = \{1-\gamma, \gamma\}$
in some order (for $\lfloor\theta\rfloor/1$ the relative error is $\gamma/\theta$,
for $\lceil\theta\rceil/1$ it is $(1-\gamma)/\theta$; scaled by $\theta$ these are $\gamma$ and $1-\gamma$).
With $|B_0| < |A_0|$ by definition of the better-estimate ordering, we have
$|A_0|/|B_0| = \max(\gamma,1-\gamma)/\min(\gamma,1-\gamma) > 1$,
so $w^+_0 = \lfloor|A_0|/|B_0|\rfloor + 1 \ge 2$.
Since $w^+_0 \ge 2 \ne 1$, the biconditional at $d = 0$ requires $r_1$ not to be a
semi-convergent.  By Remark~\ref{rem:d0-leaves}, $r_1$ is always a convergent at $d = 0$.
Hence the statement holds.

\medskip
In all cases the characterisation is complete.

\medskip
\textbf{Exclusion of exact depths.}
At an exact depth, $\theta = r_{2d} \oplus r_{2d+1}$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:exact-step}(3)),
so $r_{2d+1}$ is one of the two Stern--Brocot parents of $\theta$.
The theorem characterises $r_{2d+1}$ as a convergent or semi-convergent by asking
whether it is the last same-side node before the SB path reaches $\theta$.
But the SB path to a rational $\theta$ terminates at $\theta$, and which of its two
parents is the ``last same-side node'' (i.e.\ a convergent) depends on which of the
two standard CF expansions of $\theta$ is used.
Since the paper does not fix a CF convention for rational $\theta$, the
convergent/semi-convergent label of $r_{2d+1}$ at an exact depth is undefined, and
the exact depth is excluded from the statement.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
Note that $w^+_d$ (the propagated weight of $(r_{2d}, r_{2d+1})$) differs in
general from $w_{2d+1}$ (the inherited weight of $r_{2d+1}$).
A statement in terms of the inherited weight $w_{2d+1}$ is false: for $\theta = \log_2 3$,
$r_5 = 8/5$ carries inherited weight~$1$ but is a convergent, and $r_3 = 5/3$ carries
inherited weight~$2$ but is a semi-convergent.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[Semi-convergent characterisation of $r_{2d}$]
\label{thm:semi-r0}
  Assume $\theta$ is irrational.
  For $d \geq 1$ such that depth $d$ is an approximation depth, $r_{2d}$ is a semi-convergent of
  $\theta$ if and only if $w_{2d} > w_{2d+1}$, i.e.\ the inherited weight of $r_{2d}$
  exceeds that of its sibling $r_{2d+1}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $r^-_{d-1}, r^+_{d-1}$ be the children formed at depth $d-1$, with absolute scaled errors
$x = |e^-_{d-1} - \theta o^-_{d-1}|$ and $y = |e^+_{d-1} - \theta o^+_{d-1}|$ and $o^-_{d-1} < o^+_{d-1}$.
By convention, $r_{2d}$ is the worse child (larger $|\varepsilon|$)
and $r_{2d+1}$ is the better child.

\medskip
\noindent$(\Leftarrow)$
Suppose $w_{2d} > w_{2d+1}$, so this is a gender-changing generation and
$r_{2d} = r^+_{d-1}$ is the older sibling (worse estimate).
Then $x/o^-_{d-1} < y/o^+_{d-1}$, and since $o^-_{d-1} < o^+_{d-1}$ we get $x < y$.

Since $(r^-_{d-1}, r^+_{d-1})$ is a bounded pair (Corollary~\ref{cor:children-bounded-pair}),
their signed errors $e^-_{d-1} - \theta o^-_{d-1}$ and $e^+_{d-1} - \theta o^+_{d-1}$ have opposite signs.
Write them as $-x$ and $+y$ (i.e.\ $r^-_{d-1}$ underestimates and $r^+_{d-1}$ overestimates;
the argument is symmetric if the signs are reversed).
The signed error of the unweighted mediant $r^-_{d-1} \oplus r^+_{d-1}$ is then
\[
  (e^-_{d-1} + e^+_{d-1}) - \theta(o^-_{d-1} + o^+_{d-1}) = (-x) + y = y - x > 0,
\]
which has the same sign as the error of $r^+_{d-1}$.
Hence $\theta$ still lies on the $r^-_{d-1}$ side of $r^-_{d-1} \oplus r^+_{d-1}$, so the
SB path continues beyond $r^+_{d-1}$: $r^+_{d-1}$ is not the last node of its run.
Hence $r_{2d} = r^+_{d-1}$ is a semi-convergent.

  \centering

    the last node before the SB-path crosses $\theta$, which is exactly the
    definition of a convergent.
    This is consistent with Theorem~\ref{thm:semi-r1}: since $w^+_1 = 15 \ge 2$,

\medskip
\noindent$(\Rightarrow)$
We proceed by induction on $d \ge 1$.

\textbf{Base case $d = 1$.}
$r_2$ is a child of the depth-$0$ pair, formed at weight $w^+_0 - 1$ or $w^+_0$.
By the $d = 0$ case of Theorem~\ref{thm:semi-r1}, $w^+_0 \ge 2$ always.
If $w_{2} \le w_{3}$, then this is a gender-preserving generation and
$r_2 = r^-_0$ is the older sibling at weight $w = w^+_0 - 1 \ge 1$.
By the block structure of Theorem~\ref{thm:sb-path}, $r_2$ is the last same-side node
at stage $0$, hence a convergent.
($w^-_0 = 0$ would force $w^+_0 = 1$, contradicting $w^+_0 \ge 2$, so this sub-case
cannot arise at $d = 1$.)

\textbf{Inductive step.}
Suppose $(\Rightarrow)$ holds at all depths $< d$.
Suppose $w_{2d} \le w_{2d+1}$, so this is a gender-preserving generation
and $r_{2d} = r^-_{d-1}$ is the older sibling,
i.e.\ $r^-_{d-1} = r_{2d-2} + (w^+_{d-1} - 1)\,r_{2d-1}$.
\begin{itemize}
  \item If $w^-_{d-1} \ge 1$: by the block structure established in the proof of
  Theorem~\ref{thm:sb-path}, the SB path visits the nodes
  $r_{2d-2} + w\,r_{2d-1}$ for $w = 1, \ldots, w^+_{d-1}$ in order within
  stage $d-1$.  All nodes with $w < w^+_{d-1}$ lie on the same side of $\theta$
  as $r_{2d-2}$; the node at $w = w^+_{d-1}$ (namely $r^+_{d-1}$) lies on the
  other side.  Since $r^-_{d-1} = r_{2d-2} + (w^+_{d-1}-1)\,r_{2d-1}$ is the node at
  $w = w^+_{d-1} - 1 \ge 1$, it is the last same-side node before the crossing.
  Hence $r_{2d} = r^-_{d-1}$ is a convergent.
  \item If $w^-_{d-1} = 0$: then $w^+_{d-1} = 1$ and $r_{2d} = r_{2d-2}$
  (the coarser bound is carried forward unchanged).
  Since $d \ge 2$ (the case $d = 1$ is handled above), the gender-changing
  case of Theorem~\ref{thm:semi-r1} shows that $r_{2d-1} = r^-_{d-2}$ would imply
  $|A_{d-1}| > |B_{d-1}|$ and hence $w^+_{d-1} \ge 2$; by contraposition, $w^+_{d-1} = 1$
  forces $r_{2d-1} = r^+_{d-2}$, so the generation at depth $d-2$ is gender-preserving
  with $r_{2d-2} = r^-_{d-2}$ as older sibling.
  Therefore $w_{2d-2} \le w_{2d-1}$, and by the induction hypothesis $(\Rightarrow)$
  at depth $d-1$, $r_{2d-2}$ is a convergent.  Hence $r_{2d} = r_{2d-2}$ is a convergent.
\end{itemize}
In both sub-cases $r_{2d}$ is a convergent, completing the induction.
\end{proof}

All steps in both proofs are argued by hand.

\subsection*{Denominator growth and the depth bound}
\label{sec:denominator-growth}

The two lemmas below are the analytical engine behind the $O(\log N)$ depth bound
(Theorem~\ref{thm:linear-depth}): the first gives a lower bound on weight values,
the second converts that into exponential denominator growth.

\begin{lemma}[No two consecutive unit weights]
\label{lem:no-consecutive-unit-weights}
$w^+_0 \geq 2$ always (the depth-$0$ weight is at least $2$), and for no $d$
does $w^+_d = w^+_{d+1} = 1$ hold simultaneously.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write $A_d, B_d$ for the signed errors at depth $d$, with $r_{2d+1}$ finer
(smaller relative error $|B_d|/o_{2d+1} < |A_d|/o_{2d}$).
The weight is $w^+_d = \lfloor|A_d|/|B_d|\rfloor + 1$, so $w^+_d = 1$ iff
$|A_d| < |B_d|$.

\emph{Base case.}
At $d = 0$, the initialisation sets $o_0 = o_1 = 1$ and $r_1$ is chosen as
the finer estimate, so $|B_0| < |A_0|$.  Hence $|A_0|/|B_0| > 1$, giving
$w^+_0 \geq 2$, and $\mathcal{I}(0)$ holds vacuously.

\emph{Invariant.}
Define $\mathcal{I}(d)$: if $|A_d| < |B_d|$ then $|B_d| < 2|A_d|$.
We show $\mathcal{I}(d) \Rightarrow \mathcal{I}(d+1)$ for all $d \geq 0$.

The two child candidates produced from depth $d$ are
\begin{align*}
r^- &= \mathrm{Mediant}(r_{2d},\, r_{2d+1},\, w^+_d - 1),
  & \text{error } A^-_d &= A_d + (w^+_d-1)B_d,
  \quad o^-_d = o_{2d} + (w^+_d-1)\,o_{2d+1},\\
r^+ &= \mathrm{Mediant}(r_{2d},\, r_{2d+1},\, w^+_d),
  & \text{error } B^+_d &= A_d + w^+_d B_d,
  \quad o^+_d = o_{2d} + w^+_d\,o_{2d+1}.
\end{align*}
The depth-$(d+1)$ pair assigns $r_{2(d+1)+1}$ to whichever child has the
smaller relative error (finer), and $r_{2(d+1)}$ to the other (coarser).

\textit{Case~A ($w^+_d = 1$).}
Then $w^-_d = 0$, so $r^- = r_{2d}$ is carried forward unchanged:
$A^-_d = A_d$ and $o^-_d = o_{2d}$.  For $r^+$: $B^+_d = A_d + B_d$ and
$|B^+_d| = |B_d| - |A_d|$ (since $A_d$ and $B_d$ have opposite signs and
$|A_d| < |B_d|$).  The relative errors satisfy
\[
  \frac{|B^+_d|}{o^+_d}
  = \frac{|B_d|-|A_d|}{o_{2d}+o_{2d+1}}
  < \frac{|B_d|}{o_{2d+1}}
  < \frac{|A_d|}{o_{2d}}
  = \frac{|A^-_d|}{o^-_d},
\]
so $r^+$ is strictly finer.  The assignment is therefore unambiguous:
$A_{d+1} = A^-_d = A_d$ and $B_{d+1} = B^+_d = A_d + B_d$.
Thus $|A_{d+1}| = |A_d|$ and $|B_{d+1}| = |B_d| - |A_d|$.
By $\mathcal{I}(d)$, since $|A_d| < |B_d|$, we have $|B_d| < 2|A_d|$,
so $|B_{d+1}| = |B_d| - |A_d| < |A_d| = |A_{d+1}|$.
Hence $w^+_{d+1} \geq 2$ and $\mathcal{I}(d+1)$ holds vacuously.

\textit{Case~B ($w^+_d = k \geq 2$, i.e.\ $|A_d| > |B_d|$).}
Let $\delta = \mathrm{frac}(|A_d|/|B_d|) \in [0,1)$ be the fractional part of
$|A_d|/|B_d|$.
If $\delta = 0$ (i.e.\ $-A_d/B_d \in \mathbb{Z}$) the step is exact and depth $d+1$
is terminal, so there is nothing to prove.
If $\delta = 1/2$ then $|A^-_d| = |B^+_d|$, and $r^+$ is finer since
$o^+_d > o^-_d$; hence $|A_{d+1}| = |B_{d+1}|$ and $\mathcal{I}(d+1)$ holds
vacuously (depth $d+1$ is then an exact step with $\wminus = 1$).
Otherwise $\delta \in (0,1) \setminus \{1/2\}$.
Thus $|A^-_d| = \delta|B_d|$ and $|B^+_d| = (1-\delta)|B_d|$,
with $|A^-_d| + |B^+_d| = |B_d|$ and $|A^-_d| \neq |B^+_d|$.
If the ordering at depth $d+1$ gives $|A_{d+1}| \geq |B_{d+1}|$, then
$w^+_{d+1} \geq 2$ and $\mathcal{I}(d+1)$ holds vacuously; done.

Assume therefore $|A_{d+1}| < |B_{d+1}|$, so
$|A_{d+1}| = \min(|A^-_d|,|B^+_d|)$ and $|B_{d+1}| = \max(|A^-_d|,|B^+_d|)$.
We must show $|B_{d+1}| < 2|A_{d+1}|$.

\emph{Subcase $\delta < 1/2$}: then $|A^-_d| < |B^+_d|$, so
$|A_{d+1}| = \delta|B_d|$.  For this assignment, $r^+$ must be finer than $r^-$,
i.e.\ $|B^+_d|/o^+_d < |A^-_d|/o^-_d$.  Since $k \geq 2$ and $o_{2d} \geq 1$,
we have $o^-_d = o_{2d} + (k-1)o_{2d+1} \geq o_{2d} + o_{2d+1} > o_{2d+1}$,
so this gives $(1-\delta)o^-_d < \delta\,o^-_d + \delta\,o_{2d+1}$,
hence $(1-2\delta)o^-_d < \delta\,o_{2d+1} < \delta\,o^-_d$,
so $1 - 2\delta < \delta$, i.e.\ $\delta > 1/3$.
Then $|B_{d+1}| = (1-\delta)|B_d| < \tfrac{2}{3}|B_d| < 2\delta|B_d| = 2|A_{d+1}|$.

\emph{Subcase $\delta > 1/2$}: then $|B^+_d| < |A^-_d|$.
If $\delta \geq 2/3$, then $|A^-_d| = \delta|B_d| \geq \tfrac{2}{3}|B_d|$
and $|B^+_d| = (1-\delta)|B_d| \leq \tfrac{1}{3}|B_d|$, so $|A^-_d|/o^-_d > |B^+_d|/o^+_d$
(the denominator $o^+_d > o^-_d$ only reinforces this), forcing $r^+$ to be finer.
The assignment then gives $A_{d+1} = A^-_d$ and $B_{d+1} = B^+_d$, so
$|A_{d+1}| = \delta|B_d| > |B_{d+1}| = (1-\delta)|B_d|$, contradicting
$|A_{d+1}| < |B_{d+1}|$.  Hence $\delta < 2/3$, and then
$|B_{d+1}| = \delta|B_d| < \tfrac{2}{3}|B_d| < 2(1-\delta)|B_d| = 2|A_{d+1}|$.

In all sub-cases $\mathcal{I}(d+1)$ holds, completing the induction.
Since Case~A gives $w^+_{d+1} \geq 2$ whenever $w^+_d = 1$, two consecutive
unit weights are impossible.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Younger-sibling weight]
\label{lem:younger-sibling-weight}
For every depth $d \geq 1$, the younger sibling $r_{2d+1}$ inherits weight
$w_{2d+1} \geq 1$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The inherited weights of the depth-$d$ pair are $\{w_{2d}, w_{2d+1}\} = \{w^+_{d-1}-1,\, w^+_{d-1}\}$.
The younger sibling $r_{2d+1}$ is the better (smaller relative error) child.

In a \emph{gender-preserving} generation $r^+$ is the better child, so
$w_{2d+1} = w^+_{d-1} \geq 1$.

In a \emph{gender-changing} generation $r^-$ is the better child, so
$w_{2d+1} = w^+_{d-1} - 1$.
By Lemma~\ref{lem:no-consecutive-unit-weights} (Case~A), $w^+_{d-1} = 1$ implies
gender-preserving, so a gender-changing generation must have $w^+_{d-1} \geq 2$,
giving $w_{2d+1} \geq 1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Older-sibling weight]
\label{cor:older-sibling-weight}
For every depth $d \geq 1$, the older sibling $r_{2d}$ inherits weight $w_{2d} \geq 0$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
$w_{2d} = w^+_{d-1} - 1 \geq 0$ since $w^+_{d-1} \geq 1$ by definition.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Pair-sum invariant]
\label{lem:pair-sum-invariant}
For every depth $d \geq 0$,
\[
  o_{2d} \;<\; 2\,o_{2d+1}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
At $d = 0$: $o_0 = o_1 = 1$, so $1 < 2$.

For $d \geq 1$ the flat-sequence recurrences give
\[
  o_{2d} = o_{2d-2} + w_{2d}\,o_{2d-1}, \qquad
  o_{2d+1} = o_{2d-2} + w_{2d+1}\,o_{2d-1},
\]
where $\{w_{2d},\, w_{2d+1}\} = \{w^+_{d-1}-1,\, w^+_{d-1}\}$, so the two
weights differ by exactly $1$.
By Lemma~\ref{lem:younger-sibling-weight}, $w_{2d+1} \geq 1$.  Hence
\[
  2\,o_{2d+1} - o_{2d}
  \;=\; o_{2d-2} + (2w_{2d+1} - w_{2d})\,o_{2d-1}.
\]
\textit{Case~A ($w_{2d} = w_{2d+1} + 1$):}
$2\,o_{2d+1} - o_{2d} = o_{2d-2} + (w_{2d+1}-1)\,o_{2d-1} \geq o_{2d-2} \geq 1 > 0$.

\textit{Case~B ($w_{2d} = w_{2d+1} - 1$):}
$2\,o_{2d+1} - o_{2d} = o_{2d-2} + (w_{2d+1}+1)\,o_{2d-1} \geq o_{2d-2} + 2\,o_{2d-1} \geq 3 > 0$.

In both cases $2\,o_{2d+1} > o_{2d}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Mirror invariant]
\label{lem:mirror-invariant}
If $w^+_d = 1$, then $o_{2d+1} < 2\,o_{2d}$ (equivalently, $3\,o_{2d} > S_d$).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
At $d = 0$: $o_0 = o_1 = 1$, so $o_1 = 1 \leq 2 = 2\,o_0$.

For $d \geq 1$: since $w^+_d = 1$,
Lemma~\ref{lem:no-consecutive-unit-weights} (Case~A) forces $w^+_{d-1} \geq 2$.
The flat-sequence recurrences give
\[
  o_{2d} = o_{2d-2} + w_{2d}\,o_{2d-1}, \qquad
  o_{2d+1} = o_{2d-2} + w_{2d+1}\,o_{2d-1},
\]
where $\{w_{2d},\, w_{2d+1}\} = \{w^+_{d-1}-1,\, w^+_{d-1}\}$.  Subtracting twice the first
equation from the second:
\[
  o_{2d+1} - 2\,o_{2d}
  = (o_{2d-2} + w_{2d+1}\,o_{2d-1}) - 2(o_{2d-2} + w_{2d}\,o_{2d-1})
  = -o_{2d-2} + (w_{2d+1} - 2w_{2d})\,o_{2d-1}.
\]
Since $d \geq 1$ the depth-$(d-1)$ pair exists and $o_{2d-2} \geq 1$.

\textit{Case~A ($w_{2d} = w^+_{d-1}$, $w_{2d+1} = w^+_{d-1}-1$):}
$o_{2d+1} - 2\,o_{2d} = -o_{2d-2} - (w^+_{d-1}+1)\,o_{2d-1} < 0$.

\textit{Case~B ($w_{2d} = w^+_{d-1}-1$, $w_{2d+1} = w^+_{d-1}$):}
$o_{2d+1} - 2\,o_{2d} = -o_{2d-2} + (2 - w^+_{d-1})\,o_{2d-1}$.
Since $w^+_{d-1} \geq 2$, the second term is $\leq 0$, so the whole expression
$\leq -o_{2d-2} \leq -1 < 0$.

In both cases $o_{2d+1} - 2\,o_{2d} < 0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Denominator growth]
\label{lem:denominator-growth}
For every depth $d \geq 0$, both denominators of the depth-$d$ pair satisfy
\[
  o_{2d},\, o_{2d+1} \;\geq\; \tfrac{2}{3}\cdot 3^{\lfloor d/2\rfloor} \;\geq\; 2^{\lfloor d/2\rfloor}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $S_d = o_{2d} + o_{2d+1}$.

\emph{Base cases.}
At $d = 0$: $o_0 = o_1 = 1$, so $S_0 = 2$ and both $\geq 2^0 = 1$.
At $d = 1$: since $w^+_0 \geq 2$, the child denominators are
$o_2 = o_0 + (w^+_0 - 1)o_1 \geq 1 + 1 = 2$ and $o_3 = o_0 + w^+_0 o_1 \geq 3$,
so both $\geq 2^1 = 2$.

\emph{Recurrence $S_{d+2} \geq 3S_d$.}
We split on which of $w^+_d, w^+_{d+1}$ is $\geq 2$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:no-consecutive-unit-weights} guarantees at least one).

\textit{Case~1 ($w^+_d \geq 2$).}
The two depth-$(d+1)$ denominators are
$o^-_d \geq o_{2d} + o_{2d+1} = S_d$ and $o^+_d \geq o_{2d} + 2o_{2d+1} > S_d$.
At depth $d+2$, each member's denominator is at least the sum of its two
depth-$(d+1)$ parents' denominators (for any weight $\geq 1$), so both are
$\geq o^-_d + o^+_d \cdot 1 \geq S_d$.  More precisely, one member at $d+2$ has
denominator $\geq o^-_d + w^+_{d+1} o^+_d \geq S_d + S_d = 2S_d$ and the other
$\geq o^-_d \geq S_d$, giving $S_{d+2} \geq 3S_d$.

\textit{Case~2 ($w^+_d = 1$, forcing $w^+_{d+1} \geq 2$).}
At depth $d+1$: $o^-_d = o_{2d}$ (the coarser parent is carried) and $o^+_d = S_d$.
With $w^+_{d+1} \geq 2$, the depth-$(d+2)$ denominators satisfy
$o^-_{d+1} \geq o_{2d} + S_d$ and $o^+_{d+1} \geq o_{2d} + 2S_d$, so
$S_{d+2} \geq (o_{2d} + S_d) + (o_{2d} + 2S_d) = 2o_{2d} + 3S_d > 3S_d$.

In both cases $S_{d+2} \geq 3S_d$, so by induction $S_d \geq 2 \cdot 3^{\lfloor d/2 \rfloor}$.

\emph{Individual bounds.}
For $d \leq 1$: at $d=0$ both $o_0=o_1=1 \geq \frac{2}{3}\cdot 1$; at $d=1$
we showed $o_2 \geq 2$ and $o_3 \geq 3$, both $\geq \frac{2}{3}\cdot 3^0 = \frac{2}{3}$.
For $d \geq 2$, every member at depth $d$ is a child produced at a step
where the weight was $\geq 2$ (by Lemma~\ref{lem:no-consecutive-unit-weights}
applied at depth $d-1$ or $d-2$),
so its denominator is at least the sum $S_{d-2}$ of both depth-$(d-2)$
denominators.  Setting $k = \lfloor d/2 \rfloor \geq 1$:
\[
  \min(o_{2d},\, o_{2d+1}) \;\geq\; S_{d-2} \;\geq\; 2 \cdot 3^{k-1}
  \;=\; \tfrac{2}{3}\cdot 3^k \;=\; \tfrac{2}{3}\cdot 3^{\lfloor d/2\rfloor}.
\]
Since $(3/2)^{k-1} \geq 1$ for $k \geq 1$, we also have
$\tfrac{2}{3}\cdot 3^k = 2^k \cdot (3/2)^{k-1} \geq 2^k = 2^{\lfloor d/2\rfloor}$.
Hence both $o_{2d}, o_{2d+1} \geq \tfrac{2}{3}\cdot 3^{\lfloor d/2\rfloor} \geq 2^{\lfloor d/2\rfloor}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Linear depth bound]
\label{thm:linear-depth}
Let $\theta > 1$ be irrational.  For every depth~$d$ and both members
$e/o \in \{r_{2d}, r_{2d+1}\}$ of the depth-$d$ pair,
\[
  d \;\leq\; \frac{2}{\log_2 3}\,\log_2(e + o) + 2,
  \qquad \frac{2}{\log_2 3} \approx 1.262.
\]
The depth grows at most logarithmically in $e + o$, so $O(\log N)$ iterations
of the mediant-tree algorithm suffice to produce an approximant of size $N = e + o$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
We combine Lemmas~\ref{lem:no-consecutive-unit-weights}
and~\ref{lem:denominator-growth}.
Let $e/o$ be either member of the depth-$d$ pair.
From Lemma~\ref{lem:denominator-growth}, $o \geq \tfrac{2}{3}\cdot 3^{\lfloor d/2\rfloor}$, so
$3^{\lfloor d/2\rfloor} \leq \tfrac{3}{2}\,o \leq \tfrac{3}{2}(e+o)$.
Taking logarithms base~$3$:
\[
  \lfloor d/2\rfloor
  \;\leq\; \log_3\!\bigl(\tfrac{3}{2}(e+o)\bigr)
  \;=\; \frac{\log_2(e+o) + \log_2(3/2)}{\log_2 3}.
\]
Hence $d \leq 2\lfloor d/2\rfloor + 1 \leq \tfrac{2}{\log_2 3}\log_2(e+o) +
\tfrac{2\log_2(3/2)}{\log_2 3} + 1$.
Since $\tfrac{2\log_2(3/2)}{\log_2 3} + 1 < 2$, this gives
$d \leq \tfrac{2}{\log_2 3}\log_2(e+o) + 2$.
\end{proof}

The optimal per-target slope $m^*_\theta$ depends on $\theta$ through the average
size of partial quotients: large partial quotients drive faster denominator
growth and hence a smaller $m^*_\theta$.  For $\theta = \phi$ (all partial
quotients equal to $1$), the asymptotic slope is $1/(2\log_2\phi) \approx 0.72$;
for $\theta = \log_2 3$ the empirical slope is approximately $0.45$.

\begin{remark}
\label{rem:universal-bound}
Defining $m^*_\theta$ as the infimum of slopes $m$ for which there exists $b$
with $d \leq m \log_2(e + o) + b$ for all $d \geq 0$ and both members $e/o$
of the depth-$d$ pair,
Theorem~\ref{thm:linear-depth} gives $d \leq \frac{2}{\log_2 3}\log_2(e+o)+2$
for every irrational $\theta > 1$, and hence $m^* = \sup_\theta m^*_\theta \leq 2/\log_2 3 < 2$.
\end{remark}

\begin{conjecture}[Two-step pair-sum growth]
\label{conj:two-step-growth}
For every irrational $\theta > 1$ and every depth $d \geq 0$,
\[
  S_{d+2} \;\geq\; \tfrac{10}{3}\,S_d,
\]
where $S_d = o_{2d} + o_{2d+1}$ is the pair-sum of denominators at depth $d$.
Consequently, for every depth $d \geq 0$ and both members $e/o \in \{r_{2d}, r_{2d+1}\}$,
\[
  d \;\leq\; \frac{2}{\log_2(10/3)}\,\log_2(e+o) + 3,
  \qquad \frac{2}{\log_2(10/3)} \approx 1.151,
\]
and hence $m^* \leq 2/\log_2(10/3) < 2/\log_2 3$.
\end{conjecture}

\label{rem:two-step-deferred}

\end{remark}

Figure~\ref{fig:oracle-conjecture} shows that slope varies with partial-quotient
size.  Irrationals whose partial quotients are predominantly small (such as $\phi$
and $\sqrt{2}$) tend to produce steeper curves (more depth per bit of $\log_2(e+o)$),

\section{Relationship to Sturmian Words}
\label{sec:sturmian}
%------------------------------------------------------------

\textbf{Standing notation for this section.}
Throughout, $\theta > 1$ is real and
\[
  \alpha = \frac{1}{1+\theta}
\]
is the Sturmian slope; $s$ denotes the Sturmian word with slope $\alpha$.
For any approximant $r = (e, o)$ produced by the mediant tree, set $N = e + o$
(the length of the mediant word $\pi(r)$).
For a non-terminal bounded pair $(r_{2d}, r_{2d+1})$ at depth $d$
(i.e.\ $\Kval \neq 0$, so neither estimate equals $\theta$ exactly),
set $n_i = e_i + o_i$ for $i \in \{2d, 2d+1\}$, and define
\[
  \hat{\alpha}_i \;=\; \frac{1}{1+r_i} \;=\; \frac{o_i}{e_i+o_i} \;=\; \frac{o_i}{n_i}.
\]
Since $x \mapsto 1/(1+x)$ is order-reversing and $r_{2d}$, $r_{2d+1}$ strictly bracket $\theta$
(both conditions hold at non-terminal depths),
the values $\hat{\alpha}_{2d}$ and $\hat{\alpha}_{2d+1}$ strictly bracket $\alpha$:
\[
  \min(\hat{\alpha}_{2d},\, \hat{\alpha}_{2d+1}) \;<\; \alpha \;<\; \max(\hat{\alpha}_{2d},\, \hat{\alpha}_{2d+1}).
\]
Since $r_{2d}$ and $r_{2d+1}$ are Farey-adjacent (Definition~\ref{def:bounded-pair}),
Lemma~\ref{lem:farey-image} gives that $\hat{\alpha}_{2d}$ and $\hat{\alpha}_{2d+1}$
are also Farey adjacent.

\subsection{Structural properties of mediant words}

Before stating the main Sturmian identity we record four structural results
about finite mediant words.

\begin{theorem}[Every mediant word ends in $\OO$]
\label{thm:parity-ends-O}
For every estimate $r$ produced by the mediant tree, the mediant word $\pi(r)$
ends in $\OO$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By structural induction on the mediant tree.

\emph{Base case.} $\pi(e/1) = \EE^e\OO$, which ends in $\OO$.

\emph{Inductive step.} Let $(r_{2d}, r_{2d+1})$ be the parent bounded pair and
assume $\pi(r_{2d})$ and $\pi(r_{2d+1})$ both end in $\OO$.
For each $w^\dagger \in \{\wminus, \wplus\}$, set
$r^\dagger = \mathrm{Mediant}(r_{2d}, r_{2d+1}, w^\dagger)$.
Write $u = \pi(r_{2d})$ and $v = \pi(r_{2d+1})$.
By Definition~\ref{def:parity-sequence}: if $\Dval > 0$ then
$\pi(r^\dagger) = v^{w^\dagger} \cdot u$, which ends in $u$; if $\Dval < 0$ and
$w^\dagger \geq 1$ then $\pi(r^\dagger) = u \cdot v^{w^\dagger}$, which ends in $v$;
and if $\Dval < 0$ and $w^\dagger = 0$ (which occurs when $\wplus = 1$) then
$\pi(r^\dagger) = u$.
In every case $\pi(r^\dagger)$ ends in $u$ or $v$, both of which end in $\OO$
by the inductive hypothesis.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Overestimate criterion]
\label{thm:last-sturmian}
Let $r = (e, o)$ be an approximant produced by the mediant tree.

\textup{(1)} If $e \neq \theta o$ (i.e.\ $r \neq \theta$ exactly), the Sturmian symbol
$s_{N-1}$ satisfies
\[
  s_{N-1} = \begin{cases} \OO & \text{if } e > \theta o \text{ ($r$ an overestimate),} \\
                           \EE & \text{if } e < \theta o \text{ ($r$ an underestimate).} \end{cases}
\]

\textup{(2)} If $e \neq \theta o$, since $\pi(r)[-1] = \OO$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:parity-ends-O}), $\pi(r)[-1] = s_{N-1}$ if and only if $r$ is an
overestimate.

\textup{(3)} If $e = \theta o$ (so $\theta$ is rational and $r = \theta$), then
$s_{N-1} = \OO = \pi(r)[-1]$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\textbf{Parts (1) and (2).}
The Sturmian word satisfies $s_j = \OO$ iff the fractional part
$f_j := \{(j+1)\alpha\}$ lies in $[0, \alpha)$.
So $s_{N-1} = \OO$ iff $\{(e+o)\alpha\} \in [0,\alpha)$.

Set $\Kval = o(e - \theta o)$, so $e = \theta o + \Kval/o$.
Since $\alpha = 1/(1+\theta)$:
\[
  (e+o)\alpha = o + \frac{\Kval\alpha}{o}.
\]
Since $o$ is a positive integer, $\{(e+o)\alpha\} = \{\Kval\alpha/o\}$.
Since $r$ is a good estimate (Lemma~\ref{lem:tree-good-estimate}), $|\Kval|/o < 1$; with $\alpha < 1/2$ we have
$|\Kval\alpha/o| < \alpha < 1/2$, so:
\begin{itemize}
  \item $\Kval > 0$: $\{\Kval\alpha/o\} = \Kval\alpha/o \in (0,\alpha)$, so $s_{N-1} = \OO$.
  \item $\Kval < 0$: $\{\Kval\alpha/o\} = 1 + \Kval\alpha/o \in (1-\alpha,1) \subset (\alpha,1)$,
        so $s_{N-1} = \EE$.
\end{itemize}
For a tree estimate, $\pi(r)[-1] = \OO$ by Theorem~\ref{thm:parity-ends-O}.
Combined with Part~(1), $\pi(r)[-1] = s_{N-1}$ iff both equal $\OO$, i.e.\ iff $r$ is an overestimate.

\textbf{Part (3).}
If $e = \theta o$ then $(e+o)\alpha = (\theta o + o)/(1+\theta) = o \in \mathbb{Z}$,
so $\{(e+o)\alpha\} = 0 \in [0,\alpha)$, giving $s_{N-1} = \OO$.
Since $r = \theta$ is produced as $r^- = \mathrm{Mediant}(r_{2d}, r_{2d+1}, \wminus)$ at
the exact step, it is a mediant tree approximant, so $\pi(r)[-1] = \OO$ by
Theorem~\ref{thm:parity-ends-O}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
When $r = \mathrm{Mediant}(r_{2d}, r_{2d+1}, \wplus)$ is the child at the bracketing weight,
the overestimate condition $e > \theta o$ is equivalent to $\Dval(r_{2d}, r_{2d+1}) > 0$: since
$\wplus$ is the bracketing weight, $\operatorname{sgn}(e - \theta o) = \operatorname{sgn}(f(\wplus)) = \operatorname{sgn}(B) = \operatorname{sgn}(\Dval)$
(where $f(w) = A + wB$ as in Section~\ref{sec:optimal-w}, and $\Dval = \Dval(r_{2d}, r_{2d+1})$
is the determinant of the \emph{parent pair}).
For the companion child $r = \mathrm{Mediant}(r_{2d}, r_{2d+1}, \wminus)$
(when $\wplus = 1$, the companion is $r_{2d}$ itself), the relationship is reversed,
provided $r \neq \theta$ (i.e.\ $-A/B \notin \mathbb{Z}$).
The sign of $\Dval$ of the parent pair is therefore not a uniform criterion for a general good estimate;
the sign of $\Kval = o(e - \theta o)$ is.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[Universal $\EE\EE$ prefix]
\label{prop:EE-prefix}
For every non-leaf estimate $r$ produced by the mediant tree (i.e.\ $|\pi(r)| > \lceil\theta\rceil + 1$),
the mediant word begins $\pi(r)[0{:}2] = \EE\EE$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:branch-standard-pair}, $\pi(r) = \mathrm{Ch}^-(e, o)$.
Every non-leaf estimate $r = e/o$ lies strictly between the two leaf estimates
$\lfloor\theta\rfloor/1$ and $\lceil\theta\rceil/1$, so in particular $e/o > \lfloor\theta\rfloor \geq 1$,
giving $e > o$.
By Definition~\ref{def:christoffel}, $\mathrm{Ch}^-(e,o)[i] = \OO$ iff
$\lfloor(i+1)o/(e+o)\rfloor > \lfloor i\cdot o/(e+o)\rfloor$.
For $i = 0$: $1 \cdot o/(e+o) < 1$ (since $o < e+o$), so
$\lfloor o/(e+o)\rfloor = 0 = \lfloor 0\rfloor$, giving $\pi(r)[0] = \EE$.
For $i = 1$: $e > o$ implies $e+o > 2o$, so $2o/(e+o) < 1$, giving
$\lfloor 2o/(e+o)\rfloor = 0$, and $\pi(r)[1] = \EE$.
Hence $\pi(r)[0{:}2] = \EE\EE$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Palindrome invariant]
\label{prop:palindrome}
For every estimate $r$ produced by the mediant tree with $N \geq 2$,
the interior slice $\pi(r)[1{:}N{-}1]$ is a palindrome.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:branch-standard-pair}, $\pi(r) = \mathrm{Ch}^-(e, o)$.
By Definition~\ref{def:christoffel}, $\mathrm{Ch}^-(e,o) = \EE \cdot U \cdot \OO$ where
$U$ is the palindromic interior (Berstel~\cite{berstel2009}, Chapter~1).
Therefore $\pi(r)[1{:}N{-}1] = U$ is a palindrome.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Sturmian identity]
\label{prop:sturmian}
Assume $\theta$ is irrational.
For every estimate $r$ produced by the mediant tree,
\[
  \pi(r)[i] = s_i \qquad \text{for all } 0 \leq i \leq N-2.
\]
The terminal position $i = N-1$ is excluded: by Theorem~\ref{thm:parity-ends-O}
the mediant always emits $\OO$ there, while by Theorem~\ref{thm:last-sturmian}
the Sturmian emits $\OO$ if $r$ is an overestimate and $\EE$ if $r$ is an underestimate.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:branch-standard-pair}, $\pi(r) = \mathrm{Ch}^-(e, o)$.
By Definition~\ref{def:christoffel}, $\mathrm{Ch}^-(e,o)[i] = \OO$ iff
$\lfloor(i+1)o/N\rfloor > \lfloor i \cdot o/N\rfloor$, and the Sturmian word
satisfies $s_i = \OO$ iff $\lfloor(i+1)\alpha\rfloor > \lfloor i\alpha\rfloor$.
It suffices to show $\lfloor j \cdot o/N \rfloor = \lfloor j\alpha \rfloor$ for
all $1 \leq j \leq N-1$.

The estimate $r = e/o$ belongs to a bounded pair $(r_{2d}, r_{2d+1})$ at some depth $d$,
so $r = r_i$ for some $i \in \{2d, 2d+1\}$, giving $o/N = \hat{\alpha}_i$ and $N = n_i$.

\textbf{Key lemma (Farey gap).}
No fraction $m/j$ with $0 < j < N$ lies strictly between $\alpha$ and $o/N$.
\emph{Proof:} Suppose $m/j$ with $0 < j < N$ lies strictly between $\alpha$ and $o/N$.
Since $o/N = \hat{\alpha}_i$ is one endpoint of the interval
$[\min(\hat{\alpha}_{2d}, \hat{\alpha}_{2d+1}),\, \max(\hat{\alpha}_{2d}, \hat{\alpha}_{2d+1})]$
and $\alpha$ lies strictly in its interior, $m/j$ lies strictly between
$\hat{\alpha}_{2d}$ and $\hat{\alpha}_{2d+1}$.
By the Farey mediant property (Definition~\ref{def:farey-adjacency},
Section~\ref{sec:background-farey}), any fraction strictly between two Farey-adjacent
fractions has denominator at least $n_{2d} + n_{2d+1} > N$, contradicting $j < N$.

\textbf{Agreement.}
Fix $1 \leq j \leq N-1$ and suppose for contradiction that
$\lfloor j\alpha \rfloor \neq \lfloor j \cdot o/N \rfloor$.
Then there exists an integer $m$ with $j\alpha < m \leq j \cdot o/N$ or
$j \cdot o/N < m \leq j\alpha$ (the two cases are symmetric).
In either case $m/j$ has denominator $j < N$.
Note that $m/j = o/N$ is impossible since $\gcd(o, N) = 1$ and $0 < j < N$,
and $m/j = \alpha$ is impossible since $\alpha$ is irrational.
Hence $m/j$ lies \emph{strictly} between $\alpha$ and $o/N$,
contradicting the Key lemma.
Hence $\lfloor j\alpha \rfloor = \lfloor j \cdot o/N \rfloor$ for all $1 \leq j \leq N-1$,
and therefore $\mathrm{Ch}^-(e,o)[i] = s_i$ for $0 \leq i \leq N-2$.

\paragraph{Terminal position.}
The exclusion of $i = N-1$ follows from Theorems~\ref{thm:parity-ends-O}
and~\ref{thm:last-sturmian}: $\pi(r)$ ends in $\OO$, while $s_{N-1} = \OO$ iff
$r$ is an overestimate ($e/o > \theta$).
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Mismatch characterisation]
\label{cor:mismatch}
Assume $\theta$ is irrational.
The mediant word $\pi(r)$ and the Sturmian prefix $s[0{:}N]$ are identical
if and only if $r$ is an overestimate ($e/o > \theta$).
When $r$ is an underestimate ($e/o < \theta$), they agree at every position except
$i = N-1$, where the mediant emits $\OO$ and the Sturmian emits $\EE$.
\end{corollary}

The single terminal discrepancy is an artefact of truncation, not a genuine
disagreement: the mediant sequence closes its last primitive word with $\OO$ at
position $N-1$, whereas the Sturmian word --- which continues indefinitely --- may
still be in the interior of a longer word at that depth.
As the depth increases and $N \to \infty$, both sequences converge to the same
infinite word.

\begin{remark}
The identification of finite approximants of a Sturmian word with lower Christoffel
words is classical (Lothaire~\cite{lothaire2002}, Berstel et al.~\cite{berstel2009}).
The novel content of Theorem~\ref{prop:sturmian} is the proof via the mediant tree,
which gives an explicit construction of these approximants together with a precise
characterisation of the terminal discrepancy (the last-letter criterion,
Theorems~\ref{thm:parity-ends-O} and~\ref{thm:last-sturmian}).
\end{remark}

This has been verified computationally for several irrational values of $\theta$,
including $\sqrt{2}$, $\log_2 3$, $\log_2 5$, $\phi$, and $\pi$.
For $\theta = \log_2 3$ the verification extends to sequences of length up to
$27{,}869{,}189$ symbols: at every depth the two sequences agree everywhere except
possibly the last symbol, where the mediant emits $\OO$ and the Sturmian emits $\EE$.

\begin{remark}[The mediant sequence is the better finite approximation]
At depths where the two sequences differ, the mediant's finite mediant word is a
closer approximation to $\alpha = 1/(\theta+1)$ in terms of running $\OO$-density.
The terminal $\OO$ emitted by the mediant sequence gives a density slightly
\emph{above} $\alpha$, whereas the Sturmian's terminal $\EE$ gives a density
substantially \emph{below} $\alpha$.

This reflects a structural asymmetry in the two methods.
The Sturmian algorithm at position $N-1$ is mid-word in a longer primitive word,
so it emits $\EE$ --- inherently favouring the above-$\theta$ estimate and leaving the
running density below $\alpha$.
The mediant tree, by contrast, explicitly selects at each step the estimate with
smaller $|\varepsilon|$, i.e.\ $r_{2d+1}$, whichever side of $\theta$ it falls on.
The terminal $\OO$ is a consequence of the Christoffel word structure: every lower
Christoffel word (and hence every mediant word; Theorem~\ref{thm:parity-ends-O}) ends
in $\OO$, regardless of which side of $\theta$ the estimate falls on.

For $\theta = \log_2 3$, the improvement at the discrepancy depths is substantial:
at $N = 1719$ the mediant density error is $1.4 \times 10^{-8}$ against the
Sturmian's $5.8 \times 10^{-4}$ --- roughly four orders of magnitude better.
\end{remark}

The $\OO$-density of $\pi(r)$ for an estimate $e/o$ is
\[
  \frac{o}{e+o} = \frac{1}{e/o + 1} \xrightarrow{e/o \to \theta} \frac{1}{\theta+1} = \alpha,
\]
consistent with the Sturmian slope.

\paragraph{Efficiency and lazy generation.}
Although the mediant words are Sturmian, the mediant tree is a far more efficient
way to generate them.
To produce a prefix of $N$ symbols, the naive Sturmian algorithm must evaluate
$s_n = \lfloor(n+1)\alpha\rfloor - \lfloor n\alpha\rfloor$ at each of the $N$
positions, requiring $O(N)$ real-arithmetic operations.

The mediant algorithm instead builds a compressed representation of the same prefix.
Each iteration requires a constant number of real-arithmetic operations to compute
the next bounded pair and weight; after $d$ iterations the tree encodes a Sturmian
prefix of length $N = e_{2d+1} + o_{2d+1}$.
$N$ grows exponentially with $d$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:linear-depth}), so $O(\log N)$ iterations suffice to encode
a prefix of length $N$, each requiring $O(1)$ oracle evaluations.

Once the tree is built, enumerating the $N$ symbols requires \emph{no real
arithmetic at all}: each node specifies an integer weight $w$ and two child words,
and reading off the sequence is a recursive depth-first traversal governed entirely
by integer counts.
No auxiliary memory beyond the call stack is required.

\paragraph{$\OO\OO$-avoidance.}
Since $\theta > 1$ implies $\alpha = 1/(\theta+1) < 1/2$, the Sturmian word
avoids $\OO\OO$: two consecutive $\OO$'s would require
$\lfloor(n+2)\alpha\rfloor - \lfloor n\alpha\rfloor = 2$, but $2\alpha < 1$ makes
this impossible.
The mediant tree gives an independent structural proof: every mediant word ends in
$\OO$ and begins with $\EE$ (since $e \geq 1$; Lemma~\ref{lem:branch-standard-pair} and Definition~\ref{def:christoffel}), so every concatenation
boundary within a branch word $v^w \cdot u$ or $u \cdot v^w$ reads $\cdots\OO\EE\cdots$;
$\OO\OO$ never arises at any boundary, and by induction never arises anywhere.

\begin{remark}
The propagated weights $w^+_d$ are related to the partial quotients of the continued fraction
expansion of $\theta$, though they are not in general identical to them.
A large weight at depth $d$ corresponds to a large partial quotient, producing a long
run of one symbol type in the mediant word --- the same phenomenon that gives rise
to the irregular spacing of $\OO$'s and $\EE$'s in an irrational Sturmian word.
Conversely, small weights correspond to small partial quotients, and the mediant
word advances by only a short block at each such step.
\end{remark}

\subsection*{Connection to continued fractions}
\label{sec:cf-connection}

The connection between the mediant algorithm and continued fractions is a direct
consequence of the Sturmian identity (Theorem~\ref{prop:sturmian}).
In combinatorics on words, the standard sequence for a Sturmian word with slope
$\alpha = 1/(1+\theta)$ is generated by the recurrence $s_n = s_{n-1}^{b_n} \cdot s_{n-2}$,
where the weights $b_n$ are precisely the partial quotients of the continued-fraction
expansion of $\alpha$ (Lothaire~\cite{lothaire2002}, Ch.~2; Widmer~\cite{widmer2010},
Proposition~2.4.6); here $b_1 = a_0$ and $b_n = a_{n-1}$ for $n \geq 2$, with
$\alpha = 1/(1+\theta) = [0;\, a_0+1,\, a_1,\, a_2,\, \ldots]$.
The mediant word sequence agrees with this standard sequence at all interior positions
(Theorem~\ref{prop:sturmian}).
This does \emph{not} imply that the algorithm's weights $\wplus$ equal the partial quotients
$b_k$: the Sturmian identity constrains only interior positions of the words, not which
weights the algorithm selects.
What does follow is that the two children at weights $\wminus$ and $\wplus$ always have
denominators $o_{2d} + \wminus \cdot o_{2d+1}$ and $o_{2d} + \wplus \cdot o_{2d+1}$
(one even-indexed, one odd-indexed depending on which is the better estimate),
mirroring the structure of the classical recurrence $q_{k+1} = b_k q_k + q_{k-1}$.
Exponential denominator growth is established in Section~\ref{sec:convergence} (Theorem~\ref{thm:linear-depth}).

The MT fractions are not in general the same as the CF convergents.
The standard CF recurrence jumps by the full partial quotient $a_k$ at each step,
landing on a convergent $p_k/q_k$.
The MT algorithm picks the optimal bracketing weight $\wplus$, which need not
equal any partial quotient.

\section{Extension to $\theta \leq 1$}
\label{sec:extension}
%------------------------------------------------------------

The algorithm of Sections~\ref{sec:mediant}--\ref{sec:parity} requires $\theta > 1$.
We now show how to handle all other real $\theta$ by algebraic reduction.

If one were content to accept $\OO\OO$ in the mediant word, no special treatment
would be needed: the algorithm and the Sturmian definition extend formally to any
$\theta > 0$, and for $\theta < 1$ the slope $\alpha = 1/(1+\theta) > 1/2$ makes
$\OO\OO$ unavoidable.
The reductions below are motivated by applications (described in~\cite{paper77})
that require $\OO\OO$-free mediant words.

\paragraph{Notation.}
Let $r(\theta)$ denote the rational approximant produced by the algorithm for
$\theta > 1$, i.e.\ the sequence of bounded pairs $(r_{2d}, r_{2d+1})$ and
their limit.
Each subsection below defines $r(\theta)$ for the remaining cases by reducing
to a $\theta' > 1$ and applying a simple transformation.

\subsection{Case $0 < \theta < \tfrac{1}{2}$}

For $\theta \in (0, \tfrac{1}{2})$, define $\theta' = (1-\theta)/\theta > 1$ and set
\[
  r(\theta) = \frac{o}{e + o},
\]
where $e/o = r(\theta')$.
That is, if $r(\theta') = e/o$ then $r(\theta)$ is obtained by swapping the role
of $e$ and $o$: numerator $o$ ($\OO$-count), denominator $e+o$ (total length).

\paragraph{Mediant word.}
The mediant word for $\theta'$ has $\OO$-density
\[
  \frac{1}{1 + \theta'} = \frac{1}{1 + (1-\theta)/\theta} = \theta,
\]
which is less than $\tfrac{1}{2}$ since $\theta < \tfrac{1}{2}$.
Consequently the $\OO\OO$-avoidance result of Section~\ref{sec:sturmian} applies:
the mediant word for $\theta'$ is $\OO\OO$-free, and its $\OO$-density converges
to $\theta$.

\paragraph{Sturmian identity.}
The Sturmian word with slope $\alpha' = \theta$ is the infinite mediant word
produced by the mediant tree for $\theta'$ (Theorem~\ref{prop:sturmian}).
This Sturmian word encodes $\theta$ directly: the $\OO$-density of any long prefix
approximates $\theta$ with the same precision as the underlying mediant approximation
of $\theta'$.

\subsection{Case $\tfrac{1}{2} < \theta < 1$}

For $\theta \in (\tfrac{1}{2}, 1)$, define $\theta' = \theta/(1-\theta) > 1$ and set
\[
  r(\theta) = \frac{e}{e + o},
\]
where $e/o = r(\theta')$.

\paragraph{Mediant word.}
The mediant word for $\theta'$ has $\OO$-density
\[
  \frac{1}{1 + \theta'} = \frac{1}{1 + \theta/(1-\theta)} = 1 - \theta < \tfrac{1}{2},
\]
so it is $\OO\OO$-free and its Sturmian slope is $1 - \theta$.
The sequence encodes $\theta'$; the rational approximant $e/(e+o)$ to $\theta$ is read
off from the $\EE$- and $\OO$-counts of that sequence.

\subsection{Case $\theta \in \mathbb{Z}$}

For integer $\theta = n$, the seed itself is the answer: $r_0 = r_1 = n/1$ and
depth~$0$ is an exact step (as noted in Section~\ref{sec:parity-leaf}).
No algorithm iterations are needed; the mediant word is $\EE^n\OO$.
(The reductions above may produce an integer $\theta'$; this subsection covers
those cases too.)

\subsection{Case $\theta < 0$}

For $\theta < 0$, set
\[
  r(\theta) = -r(-\theta).
\]

\subsection{Degenerate cases $\theta \in \{0,\,\tfrac{1}{2},\,1\}$}

These three values sit at the boundary between regimes and are defined directly:
\[
  r(0) = 0/1, \qquad r(\tfrac{1}{2}) = 1/2, \qquad r(1) = 1/1.
\]
The corresponding mediant words are $\EE^\infty$, $(\EE\OO)^\infty$, and
$(\EE\OO)^\infty$ respectively (at $\theta = 1$ the $\OO$-density is $1/2$,
consistent with the $\theta \to 1^+$ limit of $1/(1+\theta) \to 1/2$).
Note the one-sided limit: as $\theta \to 1^-$ in the $(\tfrac{1}{2}, 1)$ regime,
the Sturmian slope is $1 - \theta \to 0$, so the words tend to $\EE^\infty$,
not $(\EE\OO)^\infty$.

\section{Conclusion and Open Problems}
\label{sec:conclusion}
%------------------------------------------------------------

We have presented a theory of rational approximation for real numbers based on
bounded pairs and the weighted mediant construction.
The core algorithm applies to $\theta > 1$; all other real numbers are handled by
algebraic reduction (Section~\ref{sec:extension}).

\paragraph{What is new.}
The weighted mediant step is the classical Stern--Brocot step.
The first novelty is Theorem~\ref{thm:optimal-w} (Section~\ref{sec:optimal-w}):
the unique optimal weight $\wplus$ is identified via a sign-change bracket on the
linear error function, giving a closed-form formula.
That each iterate (except, for rational $\theta$, the final companion $r^+$ of the
exact step) lies on the Stern--Brocot path of $\theta$ follows from the
weighted-mediant Farey structure (Theorem~\ref{thm:sb-path}).

At the next logical level, the denominator growth lemmas
(Lemma~\ref{lem:denominator-growth} and Lemma~\ref{lem:no-consecutive-unit-weights},
Section~\ref{sec:denominator-growth}) establish that denominators grow geometrically,
bounding the depth by $O(\log N)$.
Lemma~\ref{lem:branch-standard-pair} (every mediant word is a lower Christoffel word,
Section~\ref{sec:parity}) follows from the standard Christoffel concatenation theorem
(Berstel et al.~\cite{berstel2009}); the contribution is the identification that the
mediant algorithm \emph{naturally} produces lower Christoffel words, without any
explicit reference to the Christoffel tree.

Building on these, Lemma~\ref{lem:exact-step} (Section~\ref{sec:optimal-w}) gives
the exact characterisation of the unique rational terminal depth in eight parts;
it is the analytic pivot on which the terminal invariants rest.

The terminal invariants follow: the Sturmian identity
(Theorem~\ref{prop:sturmian}), the palindrome invariant (Theorem~\ref{prop:palindrome}),
the MT-never-skips property (Theorem~\ref{thm:never-skips}), and the
semi-convergent characterisations (Theorems~\ref{thm:semi-r0} and~\ref{thm:semi-r1}).
The Sturmian identity and palindrome are consequences of the Christoffel word theory
(Lothaire~\cite{lothaire2002}, Berstel et al.~\cite{berstel2009}); the contribution
is a direct proof via the mediant construction, together with the last-letter criterion
(Theorems~\ref{thm:parity-ends-O} and~\ref{thm:last-sturmian}) which is new.
The mediant tree taken as a whole is a \emph{compressed transducer}: it encodes the
construction history of a length-$N$ Sturmian prefix in $O(\log N)$ iterations
(Theorem~\ref{thm:linear-depth}), each requiring $O(1)$ oracle evaluations,
after which the symbols are enumerated by a purely integer recursive traversal.

\paragraph{Open problems.}
The following items are genuinely open.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Characterisation of the MT fractions.}
    Every MT fraction is a convergent or semi-convergent of $\theta$ (except, for
    rational $\theta$, the final companion $r^+$ of the exact step, which lies on
    the same side of $\theta$ as $r_{2d+1}$ and is not a convergent or
    semi-convergent); the MT visits
    the subsequence of those whose consecutive mediants straddle $\theta$.
    Characterise this subsequence in terms of the continued-fraction expansion of
    $\theta$, and determine when an MT fraction equals a CF convergent.
  \item \textbf{Cost comparison with CF.}
    The CF algorithm produces convergents in $O(\log N)$ arithmetic steps; the MT does
    likewise but visits different fractions.
    Give a precise comparison of the approximation quality per step between the two
    algorithms.
  \item \textbf{Tighter depth-bound constants.}
    Theorem~\ref{thm:linear-depth} proves $d \le (2/\log_2 3)\log_2(e+o)+2$
    with universal constants.
    The exact value of $m^* = \sup_\theta m^*_\theta \le 2/\log_2 3$
    (Remark~\ref{rem:universal-bound}) is unknown; in particular
    $\phi$ has been shown not to attain the supremum.
\end{enumerate}
\noindent
The following item has a proof that was deferred from this paper.
\begin{enumerate}
  \setcounter{enumi}{3}
  \item \textbf{Two-step growth conjecture.}
    Conjecture~\ref{conj:two-step-growth} proposes the tighter bound
    $d \le (2/\log_2(10/3))\log_2(e+o)+3$ (slope $\approx 1.151$).
    A proof exists but its inclusion would substantially increase the length of
    this paper; it is deferred to a future work.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Worked example: mediant words for \texorpdfstring{$\log_2 3$}{log\_2(3)}}

\begin{example}[Mediant words and trees for $\log_2 3$]
\label{ex:log2-3}

\subsection*{Use of Artificial Intelligence}

Large language models played a substantial role in the development of this paper,
and the author considers it important to describe that role honestly.

\textbf{Review and gap identification.}
Claude (Anthropic) and Gemini (Google) were used as critical reviewers across
multiple structured review rounds.
Each model was presented with the manuscript and asked to identify mathematical
gaps, proof obligations, notational inconsistencies, and expository weaknesses.
The resulting issue lists directly drove successive revisions and are documented
in the paper's revision history.

\textbf{Theorem formulation.}
The boundary between the author identifying a result and an AI model identifying
it is not always sharp.
In several instances the need for a theorem or lemma was recognised during
AI-assisted review rather than by the author independently.
The author regards this as a genuine intellectual contribution by the models
involved, not merely automated checking.

\textbf{Proof development.}
On several occasions, Aristotle (the code name used for the AI reviewer in
this project's review workflow) did not merely flag a conjecture as unproven
but supplied the proof that elevated it to a theorem.
Theorem~\ref{thm:semi-r1} and Theorem~\ref{thm:semi-r0} are examples where
the final rigorous proofs emerged from this collaborative process.
Claude also drafted proofs and theorem statements at various points in the
development; in some cases these drafts were adopted with revision, in others
they were rejected or replaced.

\textbf{Exposition.}
Claude was used to assist with drafting, restructuring, and checking revised
passages of mathematical exposition.
No AI-generated text was incorporated without review and revision by the author.

\textbf{Responsibility.}
The overall research programme, the framing of results, all decisions about
what to accept, defer, or reject, and final responsibility for the correctness
of the paper rest with the author.
The author does not regard AI assistance as diminishing the paper's validity,
but believes readers are entitled to a complete account of how it was produced.
