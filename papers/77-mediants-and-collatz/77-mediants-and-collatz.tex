\title{Mediants and Collatz:\\
  Constructing Generalised Collatz Trajectories\\
  via Rational Approximation}
\author{Jon Seymour}
\date{2026}

\begin{document}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
We describe an application of the mediant tree algorithm of~\cite{paper76} to the
construction of generalised Collatz trajectories.
Given a generalised Collatz system $(g, h)$ with odd step $x \mapsto gx + q$ and
even step $x \mapsto x/h$ (where $h \nmid q$), a parity sequence realising a good rational approximant
to $\theta = \log_h g$ is produced directly by the mediant tree as a mediant word
over $\{\OO, \EE\}$.
We show how to convert such a mediant word into a concrete starting integer $x_0$
via a standard modular-arithmetic pipeline, and explain why the canonical greedy
parity-sequence algorithm produces exactly the Sturmian word for slope
$1/(1+\log_h g)$.
The parity--residue correspondence used in the pipeline is classical (Terras~\cite{terras1976},
Lagarias~\cite{lagarias1985}); what is new is the \emph{generative} role of the mediant
tree as a transducer: it constructs the canonical balanced parity word and the rational
approximant it realises in a single O(log N) integer-arithmetic pass, with no real
arithmetic at runtime.
We compare with Eliahou's continued-fraction bounding method~\cite{eliahou1993}, which
provides lower bounds on Collatz cycle lengths via good approximants of $\log_2 3$ but
gives no means of constructing a parity sequence realising any given approximant.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
%------------------------------------------------------------

A \emph{generalised Collatz sequence} is defined by a pair $(g, h)$ with $g, h \geq 2$
and $\gcd(g, h) = 1$: an element $x$ with $h \nmid x$ (an \emph{odd} element) is mapped
to $gx + q$ and an element with $h \mid x$ (an \emph{even} element) is divided by $h$.
The standard Collatz sequence corresponds to $g = 3$, $h = 2$, $q = 1$.
The parity structure of a trajectory --- the sequence of odd and even steps --- is
governed by $\theta = \log_h g$: a long-descending trajectory must have the ratio
of even steps to odd steps approximate $\theta$ from above.

\paragraph{The question this paper answers.}
A non-trivial cycle in a Collatz system must have its parity sequence balanced so that
the net effect is multiplication by exactly $1$: formally, $g^o = h^e$ for a cycle
with $o$ odd and $e$ even steps.
Since $\log_h g$ is irrational, no exact balance exists, but arbitrarily close rational
approximants $e/o \approx \theta$ produce parity sequences where $g^o/h^e$ is
arbitrarily close to $1$.
The mediant tree provides a canonical \emph{generator} for such sequences:
for any desired approximation quality, it produces an explicit $e/o$, the balanced
parity word of length $e+o$ that realises it, and (via the pipeline of
Section~\ref{sec:pipeline}) a concrete starting integer $x_0$ whose trajectory
follows that word exactly.
This provides a systematic tool for exploring the structure of near-cycle parity
sequences --- the region of parity space where any non-trivial cycle, were it to exist,
would have to live.

The companion paper~\cite{paper76} develops the \emph{mediant tree}.
The present paper describes the Collatz application that originally motivated it.
The main contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item A pipeline (Section~\ref{sec:pipeline}) for converting a mediant word into
    a concrete starting integer $x_0$ whose generalised Collatz trajectory follows
    the prescribed parity pattern for exactly $e + o$ steps.
  \item A proof (Section~\ref{sec:greedy}) that the canonical greedy parity-sequence
    algorithm produces exactly the Sturmian word for slope $1/(1+\log_h g)$ ---
    the same word the mediant tree generates.
  \item A discussion (Section~\ref{sec:eliahou}) situating the work relative to
    Terras~\cite{terras1976}, Lagarias~\cite{lagarias1985}, and Eliahou~\cite{eliahou1993},
    and explaining what the generative transducer adds over prior descriptive approaches.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Organisation.}
Section~\ref{sec:prelim} recalls the key definitions and results from~\cite{paper76}.
Section~\ref{sec:collatz} introduces generalised Collatz sequences and parity
sequences.
Section~\ref{sec:pipeline} presents the step-by-step pipeline from mediant word
to Collatz trajectory.
Section~\ref{sec:greedy} describes the greedy algorithm and its Sturmian
interpretation.
Section~\ref{sec:not-sturmian} discusses why general Collatz trajectories are not
Sturmian.
Section~\ref{sec:eliahou} compares with Eliahou's approach.
Section~\ref{sec:future} describes observations on the carry polynomial's Laurent
symmetry and future work directions: defect perturbation theory and palindromicity
as an obstruction to limit cycles.

\section{Preliminaries}
\label{sec:prelim}
%------------------------------------------------------------

We recall the definitions and results from~\cite{paper76} that are used in this
paper.
All notation is consistent with that reference.

A \emph{ratio} is a pair $(e, o) \in \NN^2$ with $\gcd(e, o) = 1$, written $e/o$.
Given a real $\theta > 1$, a ratio $r = e/o$ is a \emph{good estimate} of $\theta$
if $|e - \theta o| < 1$.
Two good estimates $r_i = e_i/o_i$ and $r_{i+1} = e_{i+1}/o_{i+1}$ form a
\emph{bounded pair} $(r_i, r_{i+1})$ if they bracket $\theta$ and satisfy
$\Delta(r_i, r_{i+1}) := e_{i+1} o_i - e_i o_{i+1} = \pm 1$.

Given a bounded pair $(r_i, r_{i+1})$ and a weight $w \geq 0$, the
\emph{weighted mediant} is
\[
  \mathrm{Mediant}(r_i, r_{i+1}, w) = \frac{e_i + w\,e_{i+1}}{o_i + w\,o_{i+1}}.
\]
The \emph{optimal integer weight} $\wplus$ is the unique integer such that the pair
$\bigl(\mathrm{Mediant}(r_i, r_{i+1}, \wminus),\, \mathrm{Mediant}(r_i, r_{i+1}, \wplus)\bigr)$
brackets $\theta$, where $\wminus = \wplus - 1$; it is given in closed form by
Theorem~8.1 of~\cite{paper76}, which also guarantees $\wplus \geq 1$.

A \emph{leaf} estimate has denominator $o = 1$; its mediant word is
$\EE^e\OO$ (Definition~9.1 of~\cite{paper76}).
The children of a bounded pair at the optimal weight $\wplus$ themselves form a
bounded pair (Corollary~8.2 of~\cite{paper76}).

The \emph{mediant word} $\pi(r)$ of a ratio $r$ produced by the mediant tree is a
string over $\{\OO, \EE\}$ of length $e + o$ encoding the sequence of weighted-mediant
choices made to reach $r$ from the initial bounded pair.
It is a lower Christoffel word (Lemma~9.3 of~\cite{paper76}), ends in $\OO$
(Theorem~12.1 of~\cite{paper76}), is $\OO\OO$-free (§12 of~\cite{paper76}),
begins with $\EE\EE$ for every non-leaf estimate (Theorem~12.4 of~\cite{paper76}),
and is identical to the Sturmian word for slope $1/(1+\theta)$ at all positions
except possibly the last (Theorem~12.6 of~\cite{paper76}).

\section{Generalised Collatz Sequences and Parity Sequences}
\label{sec:collatz}
%------------------------------------------------------------

A \emph{generalised Collatz sequence} uses an odd step $x \mapsto gx + q$ and an
even step $x \mapsto x/h$, where $g, h \in \NN$ with $g, h \geq 2$ and
$\gcd(g, h) = 1$, and $q \in \ZZ$ with $q \neq 0$ and $h \nmid q$.
The parity sequence structure depends only on $g$ and $h$ through
$\theta = \log_h g$; the value of $q$ affects only the concrete starting
residue $x_0$ (Step~4 of the pipeline in Section~\ref{sec:pipeline}).
A \emph{parity sequence} is a string over $\{\OO, \EE\}$ recording the sequence
of odd and even steps along a trajectory; $\OO$ denotes an odd step and $\EE$ an
even step.

For a trajectory to descend on average, the ratio of even steps to odd steps must
exceed $\log_h g$: if a segment of length $e + o$ has $e$ even steps and $o$ odd
steps, the net effect is multiplication by $g^o / h^e$, which is less than $1$ iff
$e/o > \log_h g$.
The boundary case $e = \lceil o\log_h g \rceil$, $o$ fixed, gives a good estimate
$e/o$ of $\log_h g$ (when $\gcd(e,o)=1$), and corresponds to the upper-bound condition
\[
  h^{e-1} < g^o < h^e.
\]
A parity sequence realising a good estimate $e/o$ must therefore have exactly $o$
copies of $\OO$ and $e$ copies of $\EE$.
The mediant word generator produces $\OO\OO$-free words by construction, as does the
Sturmian word for slope $\alpha < 1/2$ (equivalently $\theta > 1$).
For $h = 2$, $\OO\OO$-freeness is also a natural consequence of the deterministic
Collatz map: each odd step produces an even result, forcing at least one $\EE$ before
the next $\OO$.
Because every mediant word is $\OO\OO$-free, it is realisable as a deterministic
$h = 2$ Collatz trajectory~\cite{paper71}.%
\footnote{Non-deterministic $h = 2$ Collatz maps --- where the odd/even instruction
is supplied externally rather than derived from the state --- do permit adjacent
$\OO\OO$ symbols; see~\cite{nondeterministic}.}

\section{From Mediant Word to Collatz Trajectory: the Pipeline}
\label{sec:pipeline}
%------------------------------------------------------------

The mediant tree applies to any real $\theta > 1$, not only to logarithms.
The following pipeline converts a mediant word into a concrete Collatz starting
integer for any $(g, h)$ with $\theta = \log_h g$.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Step 1: Choose a real $\theta$ and an initial bounded pair.}
Select any real $\theta > 1$ and a Farey-adjacent bounded pair $(r_0, r_1)$ with
one estimate on each side of $\theta$, with $r_1$ the better estimate
(i.e.\ $|\varepsilon_{r_1}| < |\varepsilon_{r_0}|$).
For the standard Collatz system take $\theta = \log_2 3$; for the generalised
system $(g, h)$ take $\theta = \log_h g$.
The construction applies equally to any irrational $\theta$ (e.g.\ $\pi$,
$\sqrt{2}$), in which case it produces rational approximants without a Collatz
interpretation.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Step 2: Run the mediant tree.}
Apply the optimal weight formula (Theorem~8.1 of~\cite{paper76}) to produce a
sequence of mediant words $\pi(r_{2n+1})$ of increasing length.
At level $n$, the word $\pi(r_{2n+1})$ has $o_{2n+1}$ odd steps and $e_{2n+1}$
even steps, and $e_{2n+1}/o_{2n+1}$ is a good estimate of $\theta$ satisfying
$h^{e-1} < g^o < h^e$ (upper bound) or $h^e < g^o < h^{e+1}$ (lower bound).%
\footnote{These inequalities follow from the good-estimate condition
$|e - \theta o| < 1$ with $\theta = \log_h g$: an upper estimate has
$e - 1 < \theta o < e$, giving $h^{e-1} < h^{\theta o} = g^o < h^e$;
a lower estimate has $e < \theta o < e + 1$, giving $h^e < g^o < h^{e+1}$.
The general statement was verified by machine-checked proof in Lean~4 by an
AI assistant; see \texttt{bounded\_pair\_power\_bounds} in the companion Lean
development.}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Step 3: Derive the path identity.}
For a chosen mediant word $\sigma = \pi(r_{2n+1})$, the function
\texttt{path\_identity($\sigma$)} constructs the \emph{basic path identity}
\[
  h^e\,x_n = g^o\,x_0 + q\,K(g, h),
\]
where $x_0$ is the starting value, $x_n$ is the value after $n = e + o$ steps
following $\sigma$, and $K(g,h)$ is the \emph{carry polynomial} --- a polynomial
in $g$ and $h$ whose coefficients record the cumulative effect of the additive
terms along the trajectory, with $q$ scaling the carry to the chosen additive
constant.
This identity holds for every $t \in \mathbb{N}_0$: for each non-negative integer
$t$, the pair $(x_0 + h^{e+1} t,\; x_n + g^o h\, t)$ is another valid
(start, end) pair following $\sigma$, as seen from the equivalent
\emph{shifted path identity}
\[
  h^e\bigl(x_n + g^o h\, t\bigr) = g^o\bigl(x_0 + h^{e+1} t\bigr) + q\,K(g, h),
\]
which makes explicit that the family of valid $(x_0, x_n)$ pairs forms an
arithmetic progression with step $(h^{e+1}, g^o h)$.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Step 4: Instantiate to concrete $(g, h)$ and solve for $x_0$.}
The function \texttt{path\_identity\_params($\sigma$, $g$, $h$)} substitutes the
concrete multiplier and base, computes $K$, and solves
\[
  x_0 \;\equiv\; -q\,K \cdot (g^o)^{-1} \pmod{h^{e+1}}
\]
for a starting residue $x_0 \in [0, h^{e+1})$. $x_0$ is an element whose parity sequence follows
$\sigma$ for exactly $o + e$ steps and then continues with an $\EE$ (an \emph{even-continuing}
residue). In total, $h-1$ residues mod $h^{e+1}$ follow $\sigma$; the formula selects the unique
even-continuing one, and the other $h-2$ continue with an $\OO$.%
\footnote{That $h^{e+1} \mid g^o x_0 + qK$ is exactly the condition for $x_0$ to follow $\sigma$
and continue with $\EE$ (when $h \nmid q$) was verified by machine-checked proof in Lean~4 by an
AI assistant; see \texttt{step4\_even\_continuing\_iff} in the companion Lean development.
The counts ($h-1$ total followers, 1 even-continuing, $h-2$ odd-continuing) are proved in
\texttt{step4\_count\_followers}, \texttt{step4\_count\_even\_continuing}, and
\texttt{step4\_count\_odd\_continuing}. For $h = 2$ uniqueness follows since $h-1 = 1$.}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Step 5: Read off the approximation data.}
The output tuple $(o,\, e,\, e/o,\, x_0)$ encodes:
\begin{itemize}
  \item $e/o$ --- a rational approximant to $\theta$, with $e/(o+e)$ and $o/(o+e)$
    the even and odd step densities;
  \item $x_0$ --- the even-continuing starting residue $(\bmod\; h^{e+1})$ whose $gx+q$
    trajectory realises the mediant word $\sigma$ for exactly $o + e$ steps.
\end{itemize}
Setting $t = 0$ gives $x_n = (g^o x_0 + qK) / h^e$, which is also determined by
$x_0$.
As $n \to \infty$ the ratio $e_{2n+1}/o_{2n+1}$ converges to $\theta$, with
convergence rate governed by the continued-fraction expansion of $\theta$.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Remark.}
Evaluating the Collatz iteration from $x_0$ is \emph{not} an efficient way to
generate the parity sequence: it requires arithmetic with integers of size
$O(h^e)$, which grows exponentially in the word length.
The parity sequence itself is produced directly and efficiently by the mediant
tree, with no large integer arithmetic.
The value of $x_0$ lies in establishing that a concrete Collatz trajectory
realising the sequence exists --- not in using it to regenerate the sequence.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Remark (residue constraint).}
To the extent that a $3x+1$ trajectory matches a factor of the Sturmian word for
$\theta = \log_2 3$, every odd element of the trajectory is congruent to $1$ or
$3$ modulo~$8$.
This is a consequence of the Sturmian alphabet being $\{\EE\OO, \EE\EE\OO\}$:
an odd step from a residue $r \equiv 1 \pmod{8}$ produces $v_2(3r+1) = 2$ even
steps (an $\EE\EE\OO$ block), while $r \equiv 3 \pmod{8}$ produces
$v_2(3r+1) = 1$ even step (an $\EE\OO$ block); residue $5 \pmod{8}$ forces the
parity sequence $\OO\EE\EE\EE\cdots$, and residue $7 \pmod{8}$ forces the parity
sequence $\OO\EE\OO\EE\OO$; neither pattern occurs in the Sturmian word for
$\log_2 3$, so neither residue can appear in a trajectory matching it.
A full account is given in~\cite{paper33}.

\section{The Greedy Parity Sequence Algorithm}
\label{sec:greedy}
%------------------------------------------------------------

For any generalised Collatz system $(g, h)$ with $g, h \geq 2$ and $\gcd(g,h)=1$,
there is a simple greedy algorithm that generates the idealised parity sequence
using only integer arithmetic --- no floating-point evaluation of $\log_h g$ is
required.
Let $\mathit{short} = \lfloor\log_h g\rfloor$ and
$\mathit{long} = \mathit{short} + 1$ (the two possible $\EE$-run lengths, since
$\log_h g \in (\mathit{short}, \mathit{long})$).

\begin{verbatim}
def collatz_log_h_of_g(g=3, h=2):
    e=o=0
    short=0
    while h**(short+1) <= g:
        short+=1
    long=short+1
    while True:
        if g**(o+1) < h**(e+short):
            yield from 'E' * short
            yield 'O'
            e+=short
            o+=1
        else:
            yield from 'E' * long
            yield 'O'
            e+=long
            o+=1
\end{verbatim}

The condition $g^{o+1} < h^{e+\mathit{short}}$ is a pure integer comparison
equivalent to $(o+1)\log_h g < e + \mathit{short}$, i.e.\ appending a
\emph{short} block $\EE^{\mathit{short}}\OO$ is sufficient to keep the ratio
$e/o$ above $\log_h g$; otherwise a \emph{long} block $\EE^{\mathit{long}}\OO$
is needed.
This is exactly the Beatty/Sturmian greedy rule: at each $\OO$ step the algorithm
selects the shorter run when the fractional part of $(o+1)\log_h g$ is already
covered by the accumulated $e$, and the longer run otherwise.

The resulting sequence is the Sturmian word for slope
$\alpha = 1/(1 + \log_h g)$, characterised by
$s_n = \lfloor(n+1)\alpha\rfloor - \lfloor n\alpha\rfloor$ for all $n \geq 0$.

\begin{proof}[Proof that the greedy output equals the Sturmian word]
Write $\theta = \log_h g$ (irrational: if $\log_h g = a/b$ then $g^b = h^a$,
impossible for coprime $g, h \geq 2$) and $s = \lfloor\theta\rfloor$.
Let $e_k$ denote the total number of $\EE$-steps output after $k$ complete $\OO$-steps.
We claim that $e_k = \lceil k\theta \rceil$ for all $k \geq 0$.

\emph{Base case.} $e_0 = 0 = \lceil 0 \rceil$. \checkmark

\emph{Inductive step.} Assume $e_k = \lceil k\theta \rceil$.
Since $\lceil k\theta \rceil - 1 < k\theta \leq \lceil k\theta \rceil$ and
$s \leq \theta < s+1$, we have
\[
  \lceil k\theta \rceil + s - 1 < (k+1)\theta < \lceil k\theta \rceil + s + 1.
\]
The greedy appends a \emph{short} block ($s$ copies of $\EE$ then $\OO$) iff
$g^{k+1} < h^{e_k + s}$, equivalently $(k+1)\theta < e_k + s = \lceil k\theta \rceil + s$.
In that case $\lceil(k+1)\theta\rceil = \lceil k\theta \rceil + s$,
so $e_{k+1} = e_k + s = \lceil(k+1)\theta\rceil$.
Otherwise $(k+1)\theta > \lceil k\theta\rceil + s$ (strictly, since $\theta$ is irrational),
so $\lceil(k+1)\theta\rceil = \lceil k\theta \rceil + s + 1$,
and $e_{k+1} = e_k + s + 1 = \lceil(k+1)\theta\rceil$.
Either way $e_{k+1} = \lceil(k+1)\theta\rceil$, completing the induction.

Now the $k$-th $\OO$ (1-indexed) is emitted at position $e_k + k - 1$ (0-indexed) in the output.
With $e_k = \lceil k\theta \rceil$ this is position
$\lceil k\theta \rceil + k - 1 = \lfloor k(1+\theta) \rfloor$.
The Sturmian word has $s_n = \OO$ at position $n$ iff $n = \lfloor k(1+\theta) \rfloor$
for some $k \geq 1$, which matches exactly.
Since a binary word over $\{\OO, \EE\}$ is uniquely determined by the index positions
of its $\OO$'s, the greedy output and the Sturmian word are identical.
\end{proof}

For the standard case $g = 3$, $h = 2$ the first 60 symbols are:
\[
  \mathtt{EEOEEOEOEEOEOEEOEEOEOEEOEOEEOEEOEOEEOEOEEOEOEEOEEOEOEEOEOEEO},
\]
with $\OO$-density converging to $1/(1+\log_2 3) = \log_6 2 \approx 0.387$ ---
the \emph{ideally balanced} parity pattern, descending as quickly as theory
permits.
For $g = 5$, $h = 2$ ($\mathit{short} = 2$, $\mathit{long} = 3$, since
$\log_2 5 \approx 2.322$) the first 60 symbols are:
\[
  \mathtt{EEEOEEOEEOEEEOEEOEEOEEEOEEOEEOEEEOEEOEEOEEEOEEOEEOEEEOEEOEEO},
\]
with $\OO$-density converging to $1/(1+\log_2 5) \approx 0.301$.
The $5x+1$ system has three known cycles; the mediant word describes the
idealised parity pattern of a trajectory that descends at the asymptotically
optimal rate for that system.

\section{General Collatz Sequences are not Sturmian}
\label{sec:not-sturmian}
%------------------------------------------------------------

The Sturmian word for slope $\alpha = 1/(1+\theta)$ consists of exactly two block
types: $\EE^{\lfloor\theta\rfloor}\OO$ and $\EE^{\lceil\theta\rceil}\OO$,
interleaved in the Sturmian pattern.
These are precisely the leaf words of the mediant tree for $\theta$.
A Sturmian word can never contain a block of any other length.

A general Collatz trajectory is far less constrained: an actual orbit can produce
$\EE$-runs of any length, interleaved freely.
The mediant word (equivalently, the Sturmian word) is the \emph{ideally balanced}
special case.

Moreover, even among trajectories that approximate $\log_h g$ closely, the
Sturmian alphabet $\{\EE^{\lfloor\theta\rfloor}\OO,\, \EE^{\lceil\theta\rceil}\OO\}$
is not the only primitive alphabet that can arise.
For example, the three known cycles of the $5x+1$ system have alphabets
$\{\EE\OO, \EE\EE\EE\EE\OO\}$, $\{\EE\OO, \EE\EE\EE\EE\EE\OO\}$, and
$\{\EE\OO, \EE\EE\EE\OO\}$ --- each containing the block $\EE\OO$ of length $2$,
which does not appear in the Sturmian alphabet $\{\EE\EE\OO, \EE\EE\EE\OO\}$ at
all.
The mediant tree generates parity sequences over the Sturmian alphabet only; it
produces the optimal approximation to $\theta$ but does not exhaust all primitive
alphabets whose $e/o$ ratio approximates $\theta$.

%------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Relationship to Eliahou's Work}
\label{sec:eliahou}
%------------------------------------------------------------

\paragraph{Classical context.}
The connection between parity sequences and rational approximation of $\log_h g$ is
classical.
Terras~\cite{terras1976} introduced the parity sequence as a tool for analysing Collatz
orbits and observed that a descending trajectory requires the density of even steps to
exceed $\log_2 3$.
Lagarias~\cite{lagarias1985} gave a systematic account of the parity--residue
correspondence, relating the number of odd and even steps in a segment to the
residue class of the starting integer modulo a power of $h$.
Eliahou~\cite{eliahou1993} sharpened this to explicit lower bounds on nontrivial cycle
lengths, using best rational approximants of $\log_2 3$ obtained from the
continued-fraction expansion.

These works are \emph{descriptive}: they characterise what parity sequences must look
like and what constraints a cycle must satisfy, but they do not provide a means of
constructing a parity sequence achieving a given approximation quality.

\paragraph{The generative transducer.}
The contribution of the present work is a \emph{generative} one.
The mediant tree of~\cite{paper76} is a transducer: given a target $\theta = \log_h g$,
it simultaneously constructs a rational approximant $e/o$ to $\theta$ and the canonical
balanced parity word realising it, in a single pass using only integer arithmetic.
No real arithmetic is required at runtime; the approximation quality is controlled
entirely by the depth to which the tree is expanded.
The mediant word is not just any parity word with the right ratio of $\EE$'s to
$\OO$'s: it is the \emph{canonical} one, identical to the Sturmian word for slope
$1/(1+\theta)$ at all interior positions (Theorem~12.6 of~\cite{paper76}), and
palindromic in its interior (Theorem~12.5 of~\cite{paper76}).
These structural properties make it the unique \emph{balanced} representative of its
approximation class.

\paragraph{Comparison with Eliahou.}
Our algorithm constructs approximants directly from bounded pairs via the optimal weight
formula (Theorem~8.1 of~\cite{paper76}), without continued-fraction computation; the
continued-fraction connection emerges as a consequence of the Sturmian identity
(Section~10 of~\cite{paper76}).
Eliahou's framework gives lower bounds on cycle lengths but provides no construction
of a parity sequence realising any given approximant.

The present work generalises in both directions: the mediant tree applies to any real
$\theta$ (not just $\log_2 3$), and the pipeline of Section~\ref{sec:pipeline} produces
a concrete trajectory for any generalised Collatz system $(g, h, q)$ --- with the system
parameters chosen freely to match the approximation target.

\section{Observations and Future Work}
\label{sec:future}
%------------------------------------------------------------

\subsection{Laurent symmetry of the carry polynomial}
\label{sec:laurent}

The palindrome theorem for mediant words (Theorem~12.5 of~\cite{paper76}) has a direct
algebraic consequence for the carry polynomial $K(g, h)$.

Recall that the path identity for a word $\sigma$ of length $e + o$ is
\[
  h^e\,x_n = g^o\,x_0 + q\,K(g, h),
\]
where $K(g, h) = \sum_{k} c_k\, g^{a_k} h^{b_k}$ records the cumulative additive
contributions.
The exponent pair $(a_k, b_k)$ at position $k$ counts the number of odd steps before
and even steps after position $k$ in $\sigma$, so the full set of exponent pairs is
determined by the combinatorial structure of the word.

When $\sigma$ is a mediant word, its interior is a palindrome (Theorem~12.5 of~\cite{paper76}).
The mirror symmetry of the interior forces the exponent pairs to come in symmetric pairs around
the midpoint: if $(a, b)$ appears in $K$, so does $(o - a,\, e - b)$.
Extracting the central scaling factor $h^{e/2}$ (or $g^{o/2}$ --- these are equivalent
up to the factor $g^o / h^e \approx 1$ at a good estimate) yields
\[
  K(g, h) = h^{e/2} \cdot \tilde K(g/h^{\theta},\, h),
\]
where $\tilde K$ is, up to this rescaling, a symmetric Laurent polynomial: every term
with exponent $+n$ in the central variable is paired with a term with exponent $-n$.

This structural property --- that the carry polynomial of any mediant word is a
symmetric Laurent polynomial after central rescaling --- implies that the cumulative
additive contributions \emph{accumulate from the start to the midpoint} in a pattern
that is the exact mirror image of the \emph{descent from the midpoint to the end}.
The palindrome in the word domain acts as a conservation law in the algebraic domain,
constraining $K(g,h)$ far more tightly than an arbitrary parity word of the same length.

A complete treatment (precise statement, proof, and implications for the structure of
$K$ for different depths of the mediant tree) is left as future work.

\subsection{Defect perturbation theory}
\label{sec:perturbation}

The mediant tree generates the \emph{optimal} parity sequence for a given approximation
quality: a word that is Sturmian, palindromic, and OO-free.
Any actual Collatz trajectory that is not Sturmian can be viewed as a perturbation of
this optimal backbone.

Specifically, a \emph{defect} is any position where the trajectory uses a block of length
$\ell \notin \{\lfloor\theta\rfloor, \lceil\theta\rceil\}$ (i.e.\ a block length not in the
Sturmian alphabet for $\theta$).
Each defect at word position $k$ perturbs specific coefficients of $K(g, h)$: replacing
a short block $\EE^{\lfloor\theta\rfloor}\OO$ with a long block $\EE^{\lceil\theta\rceil}\OO$
shifts the exponent pair at position $k$ by $(0, +1)$, and the corresponding change in $K$
is computable from the surrounding word structure.

Because the starting residue is determined by
$x_0 \equiv -q K(g^o)^{-1} \pmod{h^{e+1}}$, every defect causes a predictable arithmetic
shift in the required $x_0$.
This gives the mediant tree the role of a \emph{reference frame}: the distance of any
Collatz trajectory from the optimal backbone can be measured by the cumulative drift in
$x_0$ induced by its defects.

This perturbation-theory perspective suggests a systematic approach to the structure of
non-Sturmian Collatz trajectories.
In particular, the known non-trivial cycles of the $5x+1$ system (Section~\ref{sec:not-sturmian})
each have a specific defect structure relative to the Sturmian alphabet
$\{\EE\EE\OO, \EE\EE\EE\OO\}$; computing the drift each defect induces, and the corresponding
shift in $x_0$, would quantify how far each cycle sits from the optimal backbone.
A full development of this defect perturbation theory --- including a formula for the
drift as a function of defect type and position, and a classification of which drift
patterns are compatible with the modular closure constraint for a cycle --- is left as
future work.

\subsection{Palindromicity as an obstruction to limit cycles}
\label{sec:palindrome-cycles}

The palindrome invariant (Theorem~12.5 of~\cite{paper76}) characterises the
\emph{family} of optimal descent paths generated by the mediant tree, not arbitrary
Collatz trajectories.
This has a structural implication for limit cycles.

A non-trivial cycle in a Collatz system must have a parity sequence that is a finite
periodic word (a cyclic word or necklace).
For such a word to be palindromic under some cyclic shift, it would need to have
\emph{multiple symmetry axes} (since a cyclic palindrome under any shift is a cyclic
palindrome under many shifts), making it extremely constrained.
In particular, a cyclic word that is palindromic under every shift must be
highly periodic --- essentially a repetition of a very short unit --- and such a
word cannot approximate an irrational slope $\theta = \log_h g$ at any reasonable
precision.

More concretely, the mediant-tree argument for why cycles are structurally
obstructed proceeds in two steps:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Pure Sturmian backbone is aperiodic.}
  The Sturmian word for slope $\alpha = 1/(1+\theta)$ with $\theta$ irrational is
  by definition aperiodic --- it cannot be written as a finite repetition of any
  shorter word.
  The mediant tree generates longer and longer factors of this infinite Sturmian word.
  No finite factor of the Sturmian word can close into a cycle, because the full word
  is not eventually periodic.

\item \textbf{Defects violate modular closure.}
  Any parity sequence that is not Sturmian must contain defects (non-Sturmian blocks).
  The defect perturbation theory of Section~\ref{sec:perturbation} associates a drift
  in $x_0$ to each defect.
  For a trajectory to close into a cycle, the total drift must be exactly zero modulo
  $h^{e+1}$ (the starting residue must equal the ending residue).
  Whether and under what conditions the defect drifts can cancel to produce such closure
  is a precise modular arithmetic question.
\end{enumerate}

If both prongs of this argument can be made rigorous --- showing that the pure Sturmian
backbone cannot close, and that any defect structure violating palindromicity creates
drift incompatible with modular closure --- then the framework provides a structural
impossibility argument for non-trivial cycles.
This is speculative at present; making it rigorous would require a careful analysis of
which defect configurations are consistent with the modular closure constraint.
