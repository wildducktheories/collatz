\title{Mediants and Collatz:\\
  Constructing Generalised Collatz Trajectories\\
  via Rational Approximation}
\author{Jon Seymour}
\date{2026 (v0.15)}

\begin{document}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
We describe an application of the mediant tree algorithm of~\cite{paper76} to the
construction of generalised Collatz trajectories.
Given a generalised Collatz system $(g, h)$ with odd step $x \mapsto gx + q$ and
even step $x \mapsto x/h$ (where $h \nmid q$), a parity sequence realising a good rational approximant
to $\theta = \log_h g$ is produced directly by the mediant tree as a mediant word
over $\{\OO, \EE\}$.
We show how to convert such a mediant word into a concrete starting integer $x_0$
via a standard modular-arithmetic pipeline, and explain why the canonical greedy
parity-sequence algorithm produces exactly the Sturmian word for slope
$1/(1+\log_h g)$.
The parity--residue correspondence used in the pipeline is classical (Terras~\cite{terras1976},
Lagarias~\cite{lagarias1985}); what is new is the \emph{generative} role of the mediant
tree as a transducer: it constructs the canonical balanced parity word and the rational
approximant it realises in $O(\log N)$ iterations, each requiring $O(1)$ oracle
evaluations (exact comparisons of $g^o$ with $h^e$).
We compare with Eliahou's continued-fraction bounding method~\cite{eliahou1993}, which
provides lower bounds on Collatz cycle lengths via good approximants of $\log_2 3$ but
gives no means of constructing a parity sequence realising any given approximant.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
%------------------------------------------------------------

A \emph{generalised Collatz sequence} is defined by a pair $(g, h)$ with $g, h \geq 2$
and $\gcd(g, h) = 1$: an element $x$ with $h \nmid x$ (an \emph{odd} element) is mapped
to $gx + q$ and an element with $h \mid x$ (an \emph{even} element) is divided by $h$.
The standard Collatz sequence corresponds to $g = 3$, $h = 2$, $q = 1$.
The parity structure of a trajectory --- the sequence of odd and even steps --- is
governed by $\theta = \log_h g$: a long-descending trajectory must have the ratio
of even steps to odd steps approximate $\theta$ from above.

\paragraph{The question this paper answers.}
A non-trivial cycle in a Collatz system must have its parity sequence balanced so
that the trajectory closes: following the cycle once gives $h^e x_0 = g^o x_0 + qK$,
i.e.\ $(h^e - g^o)x_0 = qK$.
The product-form identity (see~\cite{eliahou1993,paper21}) gives
$h^e = \prod_{j=0}^{o-1}(g + q/x_j)$, where $x_0, \ldots, x_{o-1}$ are the
odd elements of the cycle.
Taking the geometric mean of both sides, $h^{e/o}$ is the geometric mean of the
numbers $g + q/x_j$.
For $q > 0$ and positive cycle elements, the map $x \mapsto g + q/x$ is
strictly decreasing, so its geometric mean equals $g + q/\hat{x}$ for a unique
\emph{mean estimate}
\[
  \hat{x} \;=\; \frac{q}{h^{e/o} - g},
\]
which satisfies $x_{\min} \leq \hat{x} \leq x_{\max}$ (where $x_{\min}$ and
$x_{\max}$ are the minimum and maximum odd elements) by monotonicity.
In particular, $\hat{x}$ is an upper bound on $x_{\min}$: at least one odd cycle
element must lie at or below the mean estimate.
This quantity is large when $h^{e/o} \approx g$, i.e.\ when $e/o \approx \theta = \log_h g$.
Since $\theta$ is irrational, $h^{e/o} \neq g$ for any rational $e/o$, but
arbitrarily close rational approximants $e/o \approx \theta$ produce parity sequences
where $\hat{x}$ is arbitrarily large.
The mediant tree provides a canonical \emph{generator} for such sequences:
for any desired approximation quality, it produces an explicit $e/o$, the balanced
parity word of length $e+o$ that realises it, and (via the pipeline of
Section~\ref{sec:pipeline}) a concrete starting integer $x_0$ whose trajectory
follows that word exactly.
This provides a systematic tool for exploring the structure of near-cycle parity
sequences --- the region of parity space where any non-trivial cycle, were it to exist,
would have to live.

The companion paper~\cite{paper76} develops the \emph{mediant tree}.
The present paper describes the Collatz application that originally motivated it.
The main contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item A pipeline (Section~\ref{sec:pipeline}) for converting a mediant word into
    a concrete starting integer $x_0$ whose generalised Collatz trajectory follows
    the prescribed parity pattern for exactly $e + o$ steps.
  \item A proof (Section~\ref{sec:greedy}) that the canonical greedy parity-sequence
    algorithm produces exactly the Sturmian word for slope $1/(1+\log_h g)$ ---
    the same word the mediant tree generates.
  \item A discussion (Section~\ref{sec:eliahou}) situating the work relative to
    Terras~\cite{terras1976}, Lagarias~\cite{lagarias1985}, and Eliahou~\cite{eliahou1993},
    and explaining what the generative transducer adds over prior descriptive approaches.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Organisation.}
Section~\ref{sec:prelim} recalls the key definitions and results from~\cite{paper76}.
Section~\ref{sec:collatz} introduces generalised Collatz sequences and parity
sequences.
Section~\ref{sec:pipeline} presents the step-by-step pipeline from mediant word
to Collatz trajectory.
Section~\ref{sec:greedy} describes the greedy algorithm and its Sturmian
interpretation.
Section~\ref{sec:not-sturmian} discusses why general Collatz trajectories are not
Sturmian.
Section~\ref{sec:eliahou} compares with Eliahou's approach.
Section~\ref{sec:future} describes observations on the carry polynomial's Laurent
symmetry and future work directions: mutations of the limiting parity sequence
and limiting parity sequences as a baseline for cycle analysis.

\section{Preliminaries}
\label{sec:prelim}
%------------------------------------------------------------

We recall the definitions and results from~\cite{paper76} that are used in this
paper.
All notation is consistent with that reference.

A \emph{ratio} is a pair $(e, o) \in \NN^2$ with $\gcd(e, o) = 1$, written $e/o$.
Given a real $\theta > 1$, a ratio $r = e/o$ is a \emph{good estimate} of $\theta$
if $|e - \theta o| < 1$.
Two good estimates $r_{2d} = e_{2d}/o_{2d}$ and $r_{2d+1} = e_{2d+1}/o_{2d+1}$ form a
\emph{bounded pair} $(r_{2d}, r_{2d+1})$ if they bracket $\theta$ and satisfy
$\Delta(r_{2d}, r_{2d+1}) := e_{2d+1} o_{2d} - e_{2d} o_{2d+1} = \pm 1$.

Given a bounded pair $(r_{2d}, r_{2d+1})$ and a weight $w \geq 0$, the
\emph{weighted mediant} is
\[
  \mathrm{Mediant}(r_{2d}, r_{2d+1}, w) = \frac{e_{2d} + w\,e_{2d+1}}{o_{2d} + w\,o_{2d+1}}.
\]
The \emph{optimal integer weight} $\wplus$ is the unique integer such that the pair
\[
  \bigl(\mathrm{Mediant}(r_{2d}, r_{2d+1}, \wminus),\;
         \mathrm{Mediant}(r_{2d}, r_{2d+1}, \wplus)\bigr)
\]
brackets $\theta$, where $\wminus = \wplus - 1$; it is given in closed form by
Theorem~8.1 of~\cite{paper76}, which also guarantees $\wplus \geq 1$.

A \emph{leaf} estimate has denominator $o = 1$; its mediant word is
$\EE^e\OO$ (Definition~9.1 of~\cite{paper76}).
The children of a bounded pair at the optimal weight $\wplus$ themselves form a
bounded pair (Corollary~8.2 of~\cite{paper76}).

The \emph{mediant word} $\pi(r)$ of a ratio $r$ produced by the mediant tree is a
string over $\{\OO, \EE\}$ of length $e + o$ encoding the sequence of weighted-mediant
choices made to reach $r$ from the initial bounded pair.
It is a lower Christoffel word (Lemma~9.3 of~\cite{paper76}), ends in $\OO$
(Theorem~12.1 of~\cite{paper76}), is $\OO\OO$-free (§12 of~\cite{paper76}),
begins with $\EE\EE$ for every non-leaf estimate (Theorem~12.4 of~\cite{paper76}),
and is identical to the Sturmian word for slope $1/(1+\theta)$ at all positions
except possibly the last (Theorem~12.6 of~\cite{paper76}).

\subsection{Structural polynomials~\texorpdfstring{\cite{paper71}}{[paper71]}}
\label{sec:structpoly}

We import the following definitions and result from~\cite{paper71}.

Let $w$ be a parity sequence with $o \geq 1$ odd steps and $e$ even steps.
Reading $w$ from left to right, maintain a running count of $\EE$-steps seen so far;
$\kappa_j$ is the value of that count at the moment the $(j+1)$-th $\OO$-step is
encountered (i.e.\ the number of $\EE$'s strictly before it).
Concretely: $\kappa_0$ is the number of leading $\EE$'s before the first $\OO$
(so $\kappa_0 = 0$ iff $w$ begins with $\OO$); each subsequent $\kappa_j$ is
$\kappa_{j-1}$ plus the number of $\EE$'s between the $j$-th and $(j+1)$-th $\OO$.
The sequence $(\kappa_0, \ldots, \kappa_{o-1})$ is the \emph{$\kappa$-tuple} of $w$
(Definition~2 of~\cite{paper71}); it is a non-decreasing sequence of integers
recording how the $\EE$-count accumulates across the $\OO$-steps of $w$.
For an $\OO\OO$-free (deterministic) word, each consecutive pair of $\OO$'s has at
least one $\EE$ between them, so the inequalities are strict:
\[
  0 \;\leq\; \kappa_0 \;<\; \kappa_1 \;<\; \cdots \;<\; \kappa_{o-1} \;<\; e + \kappa_0;
\]
(when $\kappa_0 = 0$, i.e.\ the word begins with $\OO$, this recovers
$\kappa_{o-1} < e$; when $\kappa_0 \geq 1$ the word ends in $\OO$ and
$\kappa_{o-1} = e$, which satisfies $\kappa_{o-1} < e + \kappa_0$ since
$\kappa_0 \geq 1$).
For a non-deterministic word (containing $\OO\OO$) adjacent $\OO$'s contribute
equal consecutive $\kappa$ values, so the strict inequalities relax to $\leq$.

The \emph{structural polynomial} of $w$ (Definition~3 of~\cite{paper71}), written
$K(g, h)$ and also called the \emph{carry polynomial}, is
\[
  K(g, h) \;=\; \sum_{j=0}^{o-1} g^{o-1-j}\, h^{\kappa_j} \;\in\; \ZZ[g,h].
\]
When $o = 0$ we set $K = 0$.

\begin{theorem}[Path identity, Theorem~1 of~\cite{paper71}]
\label{thm:path-identity}
Let $(g, h, q)$ be a generalised Collatz basis and $w$ a parity sequence with
$o$ odd steps and $e$ even steps.
If $x_n = \Phi_w(x_0)$ is the value obtained by applying $w$ to $x_0$, then
\[
  h^e\,x_n \;=\; g^o\,x_0 \;+\; q\,K(g, h).
\]
\end{theorem}

\subsection{Laurent polynomials and palindromic structure}
\label{sec:laurent-prelim}

A \emph{Laurent polynomial} in variables $g, h$ is a finite sum
$\sum_{a,b} c_{ab}\, g^a h^b$ where the exponents $a, b$ range over integers
(possibly negative), with integer coefficients $c_{ab}$.
Laurent polynomials form a ring $\ZZ[g^{\pm 1}, h^{\pm 1}]$ containing the
ordinary polynomial ring $\ZZ[g, h]$ as a subring.

A Laurent polynomial $F \in \ZZ[g^{\pm 1}, h^{\pm 1}]$ is \emph{self-reciprocal
with centre $(p, r)$} if
\[
  F(g, h) \;=\; g^p h^r\, F(g^{-1}, h^{-1}).
\]
Equivalently, after the substitution $g = G \cdot g_0$, $h = H \cdot h_0$ with
$g_0^p h_0^r = 1$, the polynomial is invariant under $(G, H) \mapsto (G^{-1}, H^{-1})$:
it is a Laurent polynomial in $G, H$ symmetric under simultaneous inversion.
(We use $(p, r)$ rather than $(p, q)$ to avoid collision with the Collatz offset $q$.)

\paragraph{Why palindromes produce self-reciprocal polynomials.}
Consider a polynomial $F(g,h) = \sum_{j=0}^{n-1} g^{a_j} h^{b_j}$ whose exponent
pairs $(a_j, b_j)$ are determined by the positions of a fixed letter (say $\OO$)
in a word $w$.
If $w$ is a \emph{palindrome} --- i.e.\ $w$ reads the same forwards and backwards ---
then reflecting $w$ maps the $j$-th $\OO$ from the left to the $j$-th $\OO$ from the
right, giving the pairing $(a_j, b_j) \leftrightarrow (a_{n-1-j}, b_{n-1-j})$.
For the carry polynomial $K$ of a palindromic word specifically, the reflection swaps
\[
  (o-1-j,\; \kappa_j) \;\longleftrightarrow\; (j,\; e-\kappa_j),
\]
so that for each term $g^{o-1-j} h^{\kappa_j}$ in $K$ there is a matching term
$g^j h^{e-\kappa_j}$.
Summing over all pairs gives $K(g,h) = g^{o-1} h^{e}\, K(g^{-1}, h^{-1})$, i.e.\
$K$ is self-reciprocal with centre $(o-1, e)$.

For a mediant word $\sigma = \EE \cdot P \cdot \OO$ with $P$ a palindrome, the
leading $\EE$ shifts every $\kappa_j$ by $1$ and the trailing $\OO$ contributes
the unpaired term $h^e$.  Separating $C = K - h^e$, the palindrome mechanism
applied to the interior $\OO$'s yields
$C(g,h) = g^o h^{e+1} C(g^{-1}, h^{-1})$: centre $(o, e+1)$.

This is the mechanism by which the palindrome theorem for mediant words
(Theorem~12.5 of~\cite{paper76}) implies the Laurent symmetry of the carry
polynomial $C = K - h^e$ observed in Section~\ref{sec:laurent}.

\section{Generalised Collatz Sequences and Parity Sequences}
\label{sec:collatz}
%------------------------------------------------------------

A \emph{generalised Collatz sequence} uses an odd step $x \mapsto gx + q$ and an
even step $x \mapsto x/h$, where $g, h \in \NN$ with $g, h \geq 2$ and
$\gcd(g, h) = 1$, and $q \in \ZZ$ with $q \neq 0$ and $h \nmid q$.
The parity sequence structure depends only on $g$ and $h$ through
$\theta = \log_h g$; the value of $q$ affects only the concrete starting
residue $x_0$ (Step~4 of the pipeline in Section~\ref{sec:pipeline}).
A \emph{parity sequence} is a string over $\{\OO, \EE\}$ recording the sequence
of odd and even steps along a trajectory; $\OO$ denotes an odd step and $\EE$ an
even step.

For a trajectory to descend on average, the ratio of even steps to odd steps must
exceed $\log_h g$: if a segment of length $e + o$ has $e$ even steps and $o$ odd
steps, the net effect is multiplication by $g^o / h^e$, which is less than $1$ iff
$e/o > \log_h g$.
The boundary case $e = \lceil o\log_h g \rceil$, $o$ fixed, gives a good estimate
$e/o$ of $\log_h g$ (when $\gcd(e,o)=1$), and corresponds to the upper-bound condition
\[
  h^{e-1} < g^o < h^e.
\]
A parity sequence realising a good estimate $e/o$ must therefore have exactly $o$
copies of $\OO$ and $e$ copies of $\EE$.
The mediant word generator produces $\OO\OO$-free words by construction, as does the
Sturmian word for slope $\alpha < 1/2$ (equivalently $\theta > 1$).
For $h = 2$, $\OO\OO$-freeness is a necessary condition for realisability as a
deterministic Collatz trajectory: each odd step $x \mapsto 3x+1$ produces an even
result (since $3x+1$ is even when $x$ is odd), forcing at least one $\EE$ before
the next $\OO$; an $\OO\OO$ in the parity sequence would require an odd element
mapping directly to another odd element, which is impossible.
Conversely, for $h = 2$, any $\OO\OO$-free word is realisable.
The parity of $x_i$ depends only on $x_0 \bmod 2^{\kappa_i + 1}$, where $\kappa_i$
is the number of $\EE$-steps before position $i$.
Since the word is $\OO\OO$-free, consecutive $\OO$-steps are separated by at least
one $\EE$-step, so the $\kappa_i$ are strictly increasing at each $\OO$.
Each of the $e+1$ levels $\kappa = 0, \ldots, e$ therefore contributes exactly one
fresh bit, so the set of $x_0$ whose trajectory follows the word is exactly one
residue class modulo $2^{e+1}$.
The modular inversion in Step~4 of the pipeline produces a valid $x_0$ in this
class~\cite{paper71}.
Thus for $h = 2$, a parity word is realisable as a deterministic Collatz trajectory
if and only if it is $\OO\OO$-free.%
\footnote{Non-deterministic $h = 2$ Collatz maps --- where the odd/even instruction
is supplied externally rather than derived from the state --- do permit adjacent
$\OO\OO$ symbols; see~\cite{nondeterministic}.}

\section{From Mediant Word to Collatz Trajectory: the Pipeline}
\label{sec:pipeline}
%------------------------------------------------------------

The mediant tree applies to any real $\theta > 1$, not only to logarithms.
The following pipeline converts a mediant word into a concrete Collatz starting
integer for any $(g, h)$ with $\theta = \log_h g$.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Step 1: Choose a real $\theta$ and an initial bounded pair.}
Select any real $\theta > 1$ and take the initial integer bounded pair
the unique Farey-adjacent pair of integer rationals $\lfloor\theta\rfloor/1$ and
$\lceil\theta\rceil/1$ bracketing $\theta$, ordered as $(r_0, r_1)$ so that $r_1$
is the better estimate (i.e.\ $|\varepsilon_{r_1}| < |\varepsilon_{r_0}|$).
For the standard Collatz system take $\theta = \log_2 3$; for the generalised
system $(g, h)$ take $\theta = \log_h g$.
The construction applies equally to any irrational $\theta$ (e.g.\ $\pi$,
$\sqrt{2}$), in which case it produces rational approximants without a Collatz
interpretation.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Step 2: Run the mediant tree.}
Apply the optimal weight formula (Theorem~8.1 of~\cite{paper76}) to produce a
sequence of mediant words $\pi(r_{2d+1})$ of increasing length.
At depth $d$, the word $\pi(r_{2d+1})$ has $o_{2d+1}$ odd steps and $e_{2d+1}$
even steps, and $e_{2d+1}/o_{2d+1}$ is a good estimate of $\theta$ satisfying
$h^{e-1} < g^o < h^e$ (upper bound) or $h^e < g^o < h^{e+1}$ (lower bound).%
\footnote{These inequalities follow directly from the good-estimate condition
$|e - \theta o| < 1$ with $\theta = \log_h g$: an upper estimate has
$e - 1 < \theta o < e$, giving $h^{e-1} < h^{\theta o} = g^o < h^e$;
a lower estimate has $e < \theta o < e + 1$, giving $h^e < g^o < h^{e+1}$.}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Step 3: Derive the path identity.}
For a chosen mediant word $\sigma = \pi(r_{2d+1})$, form its $\kappa$-tuple and
carry polynomial $K(g,h)$ as defined in Section~\ref{sec:structpoly}.
Since $\sigma$ is $\OO\OO$-free the $\kappa$-tuple is strictly increasing.
Theorem~\ref{thm:path-identity} then gives the \emph{basic path identity}
\[
  h^e\,x_n \;=\; g^o\,x_0 + q\,K(g, h),
\]
where $x_0$ is the starting value and $x_n$ the value after $e+o$ steps following
$\sigma$.

The \emph{shifted path identity} follows immediately by substituting
$x_0 \leftarrow x_0 + h^{e+1}t$ and $x_n \leftarrow x_n + g^o h\,t$
for any $t \in \mathbb{N}_0$:
\[
  h^e\bigl(x_n + g^o h\,t\bigr)
  \;=\; g^o\bigl(x_0 + h^{e+1}t\bigr) + q\,K(g,h),
\]
which holds since the two new $t$-terms cancel ($h^e \cdot g^o h\,t = g^o \cdot h^{e+1} t$).
Hence the set of valid starting values is the arithmetic progression
$x_0 + h^{e+1}\mathbb{Z}$, with corresponding end values $x_n + g^o h\,\mathbb{Z}$
(see also~\S\ref{sec:laurent} for further structure of $K$).

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Step 4: Instantiate to concrete $(g, h)$ and solve for $x_0$.}
The function \texttt{path\_identity\_params($\sigma$, $g$, $h$)} substitutes the
concrete multiplier and base, computes $K$, and solves
\[
  x_0 \;\equiv\; -q\,K \cdot (g^o)^{-1} \pmod{h^{e+1}}
\]
for a starting residue $x_0 \in [0, h^{e+1})$.
The modular inverse $(g^o)^{-1} \pmod{h^{e+1}}$ exists because $\gcd(g^o, h^{e+1}) = 1$,
which follows from the system hypothesis $\gcd(g, h) = 1$: since $g$ and $h$ share
no prime factor, neither do any powers of $g$ and $h$.
$x_0$ is an element whose parity sequence follows $\sigma$ for exactly $o + e$ steps
and then continues with an $\EE$ (an \emph{even-continuing} residue).
In total, $h-1$ residues mod $h^{e+1}$ follow $\sigma$; the formula selects the
unique even-continuing one, and the other $h-2$ continue with an $\OO$.
(For $h = 2$ uniqueness is immediate since $h-1=1$; the general count follows from
the fact that since $g^o$ is invertible mod $h^{e+1}$, exactly \emph{one} residue
mod $h^{e+1}$ satisfies $h^{e+1} \mid g^o x_0 + qK$, and among the $h$ residues
$x_0, x_0 + h^e, \ldots, x_0 + (h-1)h^e$ that agree mod $h^e$, exactly one has
$h \nmid x_n$, because the final $\OO$ gives $x_n \equiv gx_{n-1} + q \pmod{h}$
and $gr + q \equiv 0 \pmod{h}$ has exactly one solution $r$ since $g$ is invertible
and $h \nmid q$.)

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Step 5: Read off the approximation data.}
The output tuple $(o,\, e,\, e/o,\, x_0)$ encodes:
\begin{itemize}
  \item $e/o$ --- a rational approximant to $\theta$, with $e/(o+e)$ and $o/(o+e)$
    the even and odd step densities;
  \item $x_0$ --- the even-continuing starting residue $(\bmod\; h^{e+1})$ whose $gx+q$
    trajectory realises the mediant word $\sigma$ for exactly $o + e$ steps.
\end{itemize}
Setting $t = 0$ gives $x_n = (g^o x_0 + qK) / h^e$, which is also determined by
$x_0$.
As $d \to \infty$ the ratio $e_{2d+1}/o_{2d+1}$ converges to $\theta$, with
convergence rate governed by the continued-fraction expansion of $\theta$.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Remark.}
Evaluating the Collatz iteration from $x_0$ is \emph{not} an efficient way to
generate the parity sequence: it requires arithmetic with integers of size
$O(h^e)$, which grows exponentially in the word length.
The parity sequence itself is produced directly and efficiently by the mediant
tree.  At each depth one comparison of $g^o$ with $h^e$ is required; finding
the optimal weight $w^+$ takes $O(\log w^+)$ comparisons at that depth, but
$\sum_d \log w^+_d = O(\log N)$ over all $d$, so the total real-arithmetic work
to produce a prefix of length $N$ is $O(\log N)$ comparisons of numbers of size
$O(h^e)$.
The value of $x_0$ lies in establishing that a concrete Collatz trajectory
realising the sequence exists --- not in using it to regenerate the sequence.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Remark (residue constraint).}
To the extent that a $3x+1$ trajectory matches a factor of the Sturmian word for
$\theta = \log_2 3$, every odd element of the trajectory is congruent to $1$ or
$3$ modulo~$8$.
This is a consequence of the Sturmian alphabet being $\{\EE\OO, \EE\EE\OO\}$:
an odd step from a residue $r \equiv 1 \pmod{8}$ produces $v_2(3r+1) = 2$ even
steps (an $\EE\EE\OO$ block), while $r \equiv 3 \pmod{8}$ produces
$v_2(3r+1) = 1$ even step (an $\EE\OO$ block); residue $5 \pmod{8}$ forces the
parity sequence $\OO\EE\EE\EE\cdots$, and residue $7 \pmod{8}$ forces the parity
sequence $\OO\EE\OO\EE\OO$; neither pattern occurs in the Sturmian word for
$\log_2 3$, so neither residue can appear in a trajectory matching it.
A full account is given in~\cite{paper33}.

\section{The Greedy Parity Sequence Algorithm}
\label{sec:greedy}
%------------------------------------------------------------

For any generalised Collatz system $(g, h)$ with $g, h \geq 2$ and $\gcd(g,h)=1$,
there is a simple greedy algorithm that generates the idealised parity sequence
using only integer arithmetic --- no floating-point evaluation of $\log_h g$ is
required.
Let $\mathit{short} = \lfloor\log_h g\rfloor$ and
$\mathit{long} = \mathit{short} + 1$ (the two possible $\EE$-run lengths, since
$\log_h g \in (\mathit{short}, \mathit{long})$).

\begin{verbatim}
def collatz_log_h_of_g(g=3, h=2):
    e=o=0
    short=0
    while h**(short+1) <= g:
        short+=1
    long=short+1
    while True:
        if g**(o+1) < h**(e+short):
            yield from 'E' * short
            yield 'O'
            e+=short
            o+=1
        else:
            yield from 'E' * long
            yield 'O'
            e+=long
            o+=1
\end{verbatim}

The condition $g^{o+1} < h^{e+\mathit{short}}$ is a pure integer comparison
equivalent to $(o+1)\log_h g < e + \mathit{short}$, i.e.\ appending a
\emph{short} block $\EE^{\mathit{short}}\OO$ is sufficient to keep the ratio
$e/o$ above $\log_h g$; otherwise a \emph{long} block $\EE^{\mathit{long}}\OO$
is needed.
This is exactly the Beatty/Sturmian greedy rule: at each $\OO$ step the algorithm
selects the shorter run when the fractional part of $(o+1)\log_h g$ is already
covered by the accumulated $e$, and the longer run otherwise.

The resulting sequence is the Sturmian word for slope
$\alpha = 1/(1 + \log_h g)$, characterised by
$s_n = \lfloor(n+1)\alpha\rfloor - \lfloor n\alpha\rfloor$ for all $n \geq 0$.

\begin{proof}[Proof that the greedy output equals the Sturmian word]
Write $\theta = \log_h g$ (irrational: if $\log_h g = a/b$ then $g^b = h^a$,
impossible for coprime $g, h \geq 2$) and $s = \lfloor\theta\rfloor$.
Let $e_k$ denote the total number of $\EE$-steps output after $k$ complete $\OO$-steps.
We claim that $e_k = \lceil k\theta \rceil$ for all $k \geq 0$.

\emph{Base case.} $e_0 = 0 = \lceil 0 \rceil$. \checkmark

\emph{Inductive step.} Assume $e_k = \lceil k\theta \rceil$.
Since $\lceil k\theta \rceil - 1 < k\theta \leq \lceil k\theta \rceil$ and
$s \leq \theta < s+1$, we have
\[
  \lceil k\theta \rceil + s - 1 < (k+1)\theta < \lceil k\theta \rceil + s + 1.
\]
The greedy appends a \emph{short} block ($s$ copies of $\EE$ then $\OO$) iff
$g^{k+1} < h^{e_k + s}$, equivalently $(k+1)\theta < e_k + s = \lceil k\theta \rceil + s$.
In that case $\lceil(k+1)\theta\rceil = \lceil k\theta \rceil + s$,
so $e_{k+1} = e_k + s = \lceil(k+1)\theta\rceil$.
Otherwise $(k+1)\theta > \lceil k\theta\rceil + s$ (strictly, since $\theta$ is irrational),
so $\lceil(k+1)\theta\rceil = \lceil k\theta \rceil + s + 1$,
and $e_{k+1} = e_k + s + 1 = \lceil(k+1)\theta\rceil$.
Either way $e_{k+1} = \lceil(k+1)\theta\rceil$, completing the induction.

Now the $k$-th $\OO$ (1-indexed) is emitted at position $e_k + k - 1$ (0-indexed) in the output.
With $e_k = \lceil k\theta \rceil$ this is position
$\lceil k\theta \rceil + k - 1 = \lfloor k(1+\theta) \rfloor$.
The Sturmian word has $s_n = \OO$ at position $n$ iff $n = \lfloor k(1+\theta) \rfloor$
for some $k \geq 1$, which matches exactly.
Since a binary word over $\{\OO, \EE\}$ is uniquely determined by the index positions
of its $\OO$'s, the greedy output and the Sturmian word are identical.
By Theorem~12.6 of~\cite{paper76}, the mediant word $\pi(r_{2d+1})$ also equals the
Sturmian word at all interior positions.
Hence the greedy output, the Sturmian word, and the mediant word are all the same
word (up to the final letter of the mediant word).
\end{proof}

For the standard case $g = 3$, $h = 2$ the first 60 symbols are:
\[
  \mathtt{EEOEEOEOEEOEOEEOEEOEOEEOEOEEOEEOEOEEOEOEEOEOEEOEEOEOEEOEOEEO},
\]
with $\OO$-density converging to $1/(1+\log_2 3) = \log_6 2 \approx 0.387$ ---
the \emph{ideally balanced} parity pattern, descending as slowly as theory
permits.
For $g = 5$, $h = 2$ ($\mathit{short} = 2$, $\mathit{long} = 3$, since
$\log_2 5 \approx 2.322$) the first 60 symbols are:
\[
  \mathtt{EEEOEEOEEOEEEOEEOEEOEEEOEEOEEOEEEOEEOEEOEEEOEEOEEOEEEOEEOEEO},
\]
with $\OO$-density converging to $1/(1+\log_2 5) \approx 0.301$.
The $5x+1$ system has three known cycles; the mediant word describes the
idealised parity pattern of a trajectory that descends at the asymptotically
optimal rate for that system.

%------------------------------------------------------------
\section{General Collatz Sequences are not Sturmian}
\label{sec:not-sturmian}
%------------------------------------------------------------

The Sturmian word for slope $\alpha = 1/(1+\theta)$ consists of exactly two block
types: $\EE^{\lfloor\theta\rfloor}\OO$ and $\EE^{\lceil\theta\rceil}\OO$,
interleaved in the Sturmian pattern.
These are precisely the leaf words of the mediant tree for $\theta$.
A Sturmian word can never contain a block of any other length.

A general Collatz trajectory is far less constrained: an actual orbit can produce
$\EE$-runs of any length, interleaved freely.
The mediant word (equivalently, the Sturmian word) is the \emph{ideally balanced}
special case.

Moreover, even among trajectories that approximate $\log_h g$ closely, the
Sturmian alphabet $\{\EE^{\lfloor\theta\rfloor}\OO,\, \EE^{\lceil\theta\rceil}\OO\}$
is not the only primitive alphabet that can arise.
For example, the three known cycles of the $5x+1$ system have alphabets
$\{\EE\OO, \EE\EE\EE\EE\OO\}$, $\{\EE\OO, \EE\EE\EE\EE\EE\OO\}$, and
$\{\EE\OO, \EE\EE\EE\OO\}$ --- each containing the block $\EE\OO$ of length $2$,
which does not appear in the Sturmian alphabet $\{\EE\EE\OO, \EE\EE\EE\OO\}$ at
all.
The mediant tree generates parity sequences over the Sturmian alphabet only; it
produces the optimal approximation to $\theta$ but does not exhaust all primitive
alphabets whose $e/o$ ratio approximates $\theta$.

%------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Relationship to Eliahou's Work}
\label{sec:eliahou}
%------------------------------------------------------------

\paragraph{Classical context.}
The connection between parity sequences and rational approximation of $\log_h g$ is
classical.
Terras~\cite{terras1976} introduced the parity sequence as a tool for analysing Collatz
orbits and observed that a descending trajectory requires the ratio of even to odd steps
to exceed $\log_2 3$.
Lagarias~\cite{lagarias1985} gave a systematic account of the parity--residue
correspondence, relating the number of odd and even steps in a segment to the
residue class of the starting integer modulo a power of $h$.
Eliahou~\cite{eliahou1993} sharpened this to explicit lower bounds on nontrivial cycle
lengths, using best rational approximants of $\log_2 3$ obtained from the
continued-fraction expansion.

These works are \emph{descriptive}: they characterise what parity sequences must look
like and what constraints a cycle must satisfy, but they do not provide a means of
constructing a parity sequence achieving a given approximation quality.

\paragraph{The generative transducer.}
The contribution of the present work is a \emph{generative} one.
The mediant tree of~\cite{paper76} is a transducer: given a target $\theta = \log_h g$,
it simultaneously constructs a rational approximant $e/o$ to $\theta$ and the canonical
balanced parity word realising it, in $O(\log N)$ iterations, each requiring $O(1)$
oracle evaluations (exact comparisons of $g^o$ with $h^e$).
The approximation quality is controlled entirely by the depth to which the tree is expanded.
The mediant word is not just any parity word with the right ratio of $\EE$'s to
$\OO$'s: it is the \emph{canonical} one, identical to the Sturmian word for slope
$1/(1+\theta)$ at all interior positions (Theorem~12.6 of~\cite{paper76}), and
palindromic in its interior (Theorem~12.5 of~\cite{paper76}).
It is the lower Christoffel word $\mathrm{Ch}^-(e, o)$ --- the unique
\emph{lower Christoffel} word with $e$ copies of $\EE$ and $o$ copies of $\OO$
(Lemma~9.3 of~\cite{paper76}), equivalently the unique conjugate of the form
$\EE \cdot P \cdot \OO$ with $P$ a palindrome among all circularly balanced words
with these letter counts.

\paragraph{Comparison with Eliahou.}
Our algorithm constructs approximants directly from bounded pairs via the optimal weight
formula (Theorem~8.1 of~\cite{paper76}), without continued-fraction computation; the
continued-fraction connection emerges as a consequence of the Sturmian identity
(Section~10 of~\cite{paper76}).
Eliahou's framework gives lower bounds on cycle lengths but provides no construction
of a parity sequence realising any given approximant.

The present work generalises in both directions: the mediant tree applies to any real
$\theta$ (not just $\log_2 3$), and the pipeline of Section~\ref{sec:pipeline} produces
a concrete trajectory for any generalised Collatz system $(g, h, q)$ --- with the system
parameters chosen freely to match the approximation target.

\subsection{Laurent symmetry of the carry polynomial}
\label{sec:laurent}

The palindrome theorem for mediant words (Theorem~12.5 of~\cite{paper76}) has a direct
algebraic consequence for the carry polynomial $K(g, h)$.

Recall the carry polynomial $K(g,h)$ and path identity from
Section~\ref{sec:structpoly} (Theorem~\ref{thm:path-identity}).

Every mediant word has the form $\sigma = \EE \cdot P \cdot \OO$ where $P$ is a
palindrome (Theorem~12.5 of~\cite{paper76}).
The leading $\EE$ shifts every $\kappa_j$ up by $1$; the trailing $\OO$ (the last
odd step, $j = o-1$) has $\kappa_{o-1} = e$ (all even steps precede it), contributing
the term $g^0 h^e = h^e$.
Separating this unpaired term, write
\[
  K(g, h) = h^e + C(g, h),
\]
where $C = \sum_{j=0}^{o-2} g^{o-1-j} h^{\kappa_j}$ absorbs the contributions from
the interior $\OO$'s.
The palindrome symmetry of $P$ forces $C$ to be self-reciprocal with centre
$(o, e+1)$ in the sense of Section~\ref{sec:laurent-prelim}:
\[
  C(g, h) \;=\; g^{o} h^{e+1}\, C(g^{-1}, h^{-1}),
\]
so centering by $g^{o/2} h^{(e+1)/2}$ yields a Laurent polynomial in
$g^{1/2}, h^{1/2}$ invariant under simultaneous inversion.

By the mechanism of Section~\ref{sec:laurent-prelim}, this means the cumulative
additive contributions \emph{accumulate from the start to the midpoint} in a
pattern that is the exact mirror image of the \emph{descent from the midpoint to
the end}: the $\kappa$-multiset of $C$ is invariant under
$\kappa_j \mapsto e+1-\kappa_j$.
This symmetry holds for every word of the form $\EE \cdot P \cdot \OO$ with $P$
a palindrome, of which mediant words are the canonical example.

The implications of this symmetry for the structure of $K$ at different depths of the
mediant tree --- and for the cycle closure question --- are left as future work.

\subsection{Mutations of the limiting parity sequence}
\label{sec:perturbation}

The mediant tree generates a specific \emph{mediant word} $\pi(r_{2d+1})$ for a given
approximation scale: a finite word that is Sturmian up to its last letter, with
palindromic interior, and OO-free, approximating the infinite limiting Sturmian sequence.
This provides a natural reference object whose properties are well understood.
Any parity word with the same $e/o$ ratio can be viewed as a \emph{mutation} of this
mediant word, and different mutations conserve different properties while altering
others.

Two examples illustrate how mutations can differ in what they conserve:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Block-length mutations.}
  Replacing a Sturmian block $\EE^\ell\OO$ with $\EE^{\ell'}\OO$ where
  $\ell' \notin \{\lfloor\theta\rfloor, \lceil\theta\rceil\}$ introduces a
  block length outside the Sturmian alphabet.
  This changes $e - \theta o$ by the nonzero integer $\ell' - \ell$, shifting
  $e/o$ away from $\theta$ for deep mediant words where $|e - \theta o| < \frac{1}{2}$,
  and alters every term of $K(g,h)$ from the modified block's $\OO$ onward.
  It also changes the residue condition on $x_0$.

\item \textbf{Reordering mutations.}
  Permuting the blocks of a Sturmian word --- for example, rearranging
  $\EE\EE\OO\cdot\EE\EE\OO\cdot\EE\OO\cdot\EE\EE\OO\cdot\EE\OO$ (i.e.\
  $\pi(8/5)$) as $\EE\EE\OO\cdot\EE\EE\OO\cdot\EE\EE\OO\cdot\EE\OO\cdot\EE\OO$
  --- keeps the same letters and hence the same $e/o$ ratio, and preserves
  the multiset of block lengths.
  What changes is the \emph{order} of the blocks, which alters the run structure
  and the carry polynomial $K(g,h)$.
  For $\theta > 1$, every Sturmian block $\EE^\ell\OO$ has $\ell \geq 1$, so
  no reordering can create an $\OO\OO$ adjacency.
  The reordered word above is unbalanced (it contains both $\OO\EE\OO\EE\OO$
  and $\EE\EE\OO\EE\EE$) and is not a factor of any Sturmian word.
\end{enumerate}

These two examples differ in what they conserve.
Block-length mutations change $e - \theta o$ by a nonzero integer, typically
shifting $e/o$ away from $\theta$ for deep mediant words where $|e - \theta o| < \frac{1}{2}$.
Reordering mutations keep $e/o$ fixed but change the internal structure of $K(g,h)$
and generally break the palindrome invariant (though some permutations that maintain
the E$\cdot$P$\cdot$O decomposition may preserve it).
In each case the mediant word serves as a baseline:
starting from the ideal word and applying controlled mutations, one can ask how
properties such as the mean estimate $\hat{x}$, the min/max cycle element bounds,
or the modular closure condition respond to each class of change.
This is left as a direction for future investigation.

\subsection{Limiting parity sequences and cycle constraints}
\label{sec:palindrome-cycles}

At each denominator $o_{2d+1}$ produced by the MT algorithm the mediant tree
identifies the \emph{mediant word} $\pi(r_{2d+1})$, a finite approximant of
the infinite limiting Sturmian sequence over the block alphabet
$\{\EE^{\lfloor\theta\rfloor}\OO,\, \EE^{\lceil\theta\rceil}\OO\}$
whose $e/o$ ratio converges to $\theta = \log_h g$.
This mediant word is the algebraic reference object for the scale in
question.
Its carry polynomial $K(g,h)$ has the interior part $C(g,h) = K(g,h) - h^e$
with a symmetric Laurent structure (Section~\ref{sec:laurent}), a consequence
of the palindromic interior of every mediant word (Theorem~12.5
of~\cite{paper76}).
Whether this structure imposes constraints on cycle existence --- and if so,
what those constraints are --- is not claimed here in either direction.
The purpose of this section is simply to motivate the study of limiting
sequences as the natural algebraic baseline for cycle analysis at any scale.

Two observations connect the limiting sequence to any actual cycle candidate.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Exact closure requires arithmetic balance.}
  A cycle satisfies $x_n = x_0$, which via the path identity
  $h^e x_n = g^o x_0 + q K(g,h)$ yields the exact integer equation
  \[
    (h^e - g^o)\, x_0 = q\, K(g,h).
  \]
  Because $\theta = \log_h g$ is irrational, $h^e \neq g^o$ for any finite
  parity word with $o \geq 1$, so any cycle requires a precise arithmetic balance
  between the multiplicative mismatch $h^e - g^o$ and the carry polynomial $K$.
  The limiting sequence for the relevant scale sets the algebraic context
  for this balance.

\item \textbf{Eliahou's constraints bound the letter-frequency scale.}
  For the standard $3x+1$ system, Eliahou's analysis~\cite{eliahou1993}
  of the product form of the cycle equation
  $h^e = g^o \prod_{j}(1 + q/(g x_j))$ shows that the $e/o$ ratio of any
  non-trivial cycle satisfies
  \[
    \log_2 3 \,<\, \frac{e}{o} \,\leq\, \log_2\!\Bigl(3 + \frac{1}{m}\Bigr),
  \]
  where $m$ is the minimum cycle element.
  This means any cycle word must sit in a narrow interval \emph{just above}
  $\theta = \log_2 3$, and hence its letter frequencies are close to those
  of the limiting Sturmian sequence for the same scale.
  (This specific bound is for the $3x+1$ system; analogous product-formula
  constraints exist for general $(g, h, q)$.)
  Whether ``close in frequency'' forces structural proximity to a mediant
  word --- and whether that proximity is algebraically significant --- are
  open questions that the limiting-sequence framework is designed to address.
\end{enumerate}

The mediant tree thus provides a natural baseline for cycle analysis: for any
parity word with a given $e/o$ ratio, the corresponding limiting sequence
describes what the ``ideal'' word at that scale looks like algebraically.
How a cycle candidate relates to this ideal --- and whether the Laurent
symmetry of the limiting carry polynomial plays any role in ruling cycles in
or out --- is left as a direction for future investigation.
